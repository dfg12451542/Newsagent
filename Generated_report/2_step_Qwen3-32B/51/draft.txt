Wales' Sustainable Farming Scheme has been hailed as 'absolutely the right scheme' by Deputy First Minister Huw Irranca-Davies, who emphasized its role in balancing agricultural viability with environmental goals. Speaking ahead of the policy's official launch, Irranca-Davies acknowledged that while the scheme may not satisfy everyone, it reflects 'more than 12 months of intense work' with farming unions, environmental groups, and industry stakeholders. 'It’ll be a whole farm approach and a whole nation approach that delivers for the people of Wales,' he stated, adding that the scheme aims to support farming that meets 'high animal welfare standards' while addressing ecological challenges.  The revised iteration of the Sustainable Farming Scheme (SFS) follows widespread criticism of its initial design, which prompted protests from farmers who rejected the proposed terms. The new version, set to be announced next week, will detail payment rates for farmers participating in the program. Irranca-Davies declined to confirm the release of an impact assessment, citing ongoing work, but expressed confidence that the scheme will improve both 'viable farm businesses and environmental benefits.' Critics, however, have called for greater transparency and democratic oversight.  Plaid Cymru’s Delyth Jewell MS highlighted concerns that farmers have long felt unheard, stressing the need to 'make sure their lives are made sustainable too.' Conservative Senedd leader Darren Millar echoed calls for the policy to be debated and voted on by politicians to ensure 'democratic legitimacy,' while urging that food security remain central to the plan. With 80% of Wales' landscape managed by farmers, the government has framed the SFS as critical to addressing climate change and ecosystem decline, placing farmers at the heart of environmental stewardship and agricultural resilience.
