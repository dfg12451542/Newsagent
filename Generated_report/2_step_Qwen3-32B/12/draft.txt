Liverpool paid a heartfelt tribute to the late Diogo Jota during their first match since his passing, marking the emotional occasion with solemnity and fan-driven homage. Ahead of the clash against Preston, the club observed a minute’s silence, while the away end was adorned with flags and scarves commemorating the former Porto and Wolves forward. Preston captain Ben Whiteman laid a wreath before the traveling Liverpool supporters, setting the tone for a day steeped in remembrance. During the 20th minute, the crowd erupted into chants of Jota’s name, a gesture echoed by Preston fans who stood in solidarity. The match programme featured a black-and-white image of Jota holding the Premier League trophy, alongside tributes to him and his brother Andre Silva, whose passing in 2022 had already left a profound mark on the club.  Fans expressed their grief and pride in the club’s tribute. Jane, a Liverpool supporter wearing a custom T-shirt dedicated to Jota, said, 'I feel very emotional... I feel very proud of my team and all the tributes they’ve put together.' For Mathew, a parent who shared the news of Jota’s death with his seven-year-old son Rafael, the day was bittersweet. 'It’s very hard for him to understand,' Mathew said, while Rafael added, 'I feel gutted. But Liverpool is a family and we’ve all come together to pay our respects in the right way.'  Head coach Arne Slot addressed the emotional weight of the occasion in his first interview since Jota’s passing, emphasizing authenticity in processing grief. 'If we want to laugh, we laugh; if we want to cry, we’re going to cry,' Slot said. 'We will always carry him with us in our hearts, in our thoughts, wherever we go.' On the pitch, players honored Jota through personal gestures: Darwin Nunez replicated two of his trademark celebrations, while Cody Gakpo scored with a tribute to Jota’s squad number 20. Liverpool have since retired the number across all club platforms, announcing it at 20:20 BST on Friday.  The outpouring of emotion extended beyond the match, with historical tributes highlighted in the *Liverpool Echo* and *Daily Express* back pages. For thousands of fans who traveled to Deepdale, the game symbolized both a farewell and a celebration of Jota’s legacy. As Slot noted, the match underscored the unbreakable bond between the club, its players, and supporters—a testament to the enduring impact of a player 'ador[ed] by followers of his club.'
