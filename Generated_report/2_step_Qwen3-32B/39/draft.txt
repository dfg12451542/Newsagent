Rory McIlroy has expressed that winning The Open 2025 at Royal Portrush would be 'just as emotional' as his Masters victory in 2024, which completed his career Grand Slam. The Northern Irish golfer, a favorite for this week's championship, reflected on the significance of competing on home soil, where he finished second in 2019. 'It would be absolutely incredible. I’m so grateful and appreciative of the support I get from home, and they really make me feel that out there,' McIlroy told BBC Sport NI, emphasizing the unique energy of playing in front of his home crowd.      Historical context highlights Portrush's emotional resonance for Irish golfers. Shane Lowry's 2019 Open victory at the venue sparked raucous celebrations, setting a high bar for this year's event. McIlroy, who struggled with the pressure of his first major on home soil in 2019—posting a 79 on the opening day—has since refined his approach. 'I’m a better player, manage my game better, and even if I have an off day, it’s not going to be a 79 like it was in 2019,' he noted, acknowledging his growth. This time, he aims to embrace the attention rather than isolate himself, stating, 'It’s better for me to accept adulation… and for the fans to see me in a positive light.'      McIlroy's confidence is bolstered by strong recent form, including a solid performance at the Scottish Open. 'I’m excited with where my game’s at,' he said, crediting a two-week break after the Travelers Championship for helping him reset. With the Open returning to Portrush just six years after Lowry's triumph, McIlroy’s pursuit of a second major on Irish soil promises to be a focal point of the tournament, blending personal ambition with the weight of history.
