Residents and councillors in Ilkley have clashed over proposed parking changes, with one Conservative councillor accusing the council of adopting a 'Scrooge-like' approach. The debate unfolded during a Bradford Council meeting, where concerns were raised about the impact of new charges on local businesses, religious communities, and vulnerable groups. Ilkley Conservative councillor Andrew Loy condemned the plan, stating, 'It’s like Ebenezer Scrooge is writing the council’s parking policy,' and highlighted fears that parishioners could face fines for parking outside churches on Christmas Day. The proposal has sparked widespread backlash, with a petition gathering over 1,500 signatures against the changes, as reported by the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS).  Local resident Richard Downing, who presented the petition, argued the changes would 'be immensely damaging' to small businesses, warning that charging for short visits would deter shoppers and reduce footfall. 'Having to pay to visit shops for a few items will make it inconvenient to shop locally – footfall will be down and people will be drawn to retail parks,' he said. Loy echoed these concerns, predicting a 'blow' to restaurants, theaters, and cultural venues like Ilkley Playhouse and King’s Hall. Evening parking charges, he added, could also discourage attendance at local performances.  Councillor Alex Ross Shaw defended the policy, framing it as part of broader budget management efforts. 'We’re looking at trying to find ways of managing the budget. Standardising parking charges is one way of doing this,' he said, acknowledging public frustration but insisting changes were being applied 'right across the district.' Despite this, Loy and others argued Ilkley faces unique challenges, with Loy stressing the unfair impact on the elderly, parents, and churchgoers. The council voted to send the petition to a future executive meeting, leaving the final decision pending. Critics, however, remain unconvinced, with Downing urging the council to 'scrap the proposed changes' to protect the town’s economic and social fabric.
