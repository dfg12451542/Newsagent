Wales secured a hard-fought 22-31 victory over Japan in Kobe on July 13, 2025, with fly-half Dan Edwards lauding the team's resilience amid challenging conditions. The win, described by Edwards as a testament to the squad's character, came against the backdrop of historical struggles for Welsh rugby, where defeating Japan—once a rarity—now brings relief and cautious optimism. 'It shows the character we've got. That was a real statement that when it gets tough, we can pull through,' Edwards said, reflecting on the team's ability to overcome doubters and a sweltering heat that tested both physical and mental endurance.      A pivotal moment arrived in the closing stages when replacement flanker Taine Plumtree executed an audacious reverse pass, setting up Edwards for a crucial late try. Plumtree, who replaced Alex Mann at half-time, credited interim head coach Matt Sherratt's directive to 'be brave and express ourselves' for the daring play. 'I heard Rog out the back so I pulled it back. Thank God it came through,' Plumtree explained, while Sherratt admitted the risky maneuver exceeded his expectations: 'I didn't intend for him to be that brave, but it worked!'      The victory also marked a personal milestone for winger Keelan Giles, who reflected on his journey from bench warmth to match impact. 'It's almost like a full-circle moment, playing against Japan and being on the bench nine years ago against Japan and not getting on,' Giles said, expressing gratitude for the support of his family and the fans. With Wales having endured 18 consecutive losses prior to this win, Giles emphasized the emotional weight of breaking the streak: 'It's almost the monkey off our backs. It's something to build on now.'      Sherratt highlighted the team's strategic boldness as a turning point, praising the bench's energy in the stifling Kobe humidity. 'It would have been easy to go to a safe lineout at the front. We have thrown to the back, run a more risky play,' he said, signaling a shift in the team's approach. As Wales looks ahead to the autumn fixtures, Edwards and his teammates remain focused on momentum: 'Now it's time to kick on and get more wins.' The victory, though hard-earned, has reignited hope for a reinvigorated era in Welsh rugby.
