South African President Cyril Ramaphosa has suspended Police Minister Senzo Mchunu following allegations of ties to organized crime syndicates, announcing the move in a live televised address on Sunday. The suspension comes amid claims that Mchunu interfered in investigations into political killings and corruption within law enforcement, undermining the constitution and threatening national security. Ramaphosa emphasized the urgency of the matter, stating, 'These allegations call for an urgent and comprehensive investigation,' and announced the formation of a judicial commission led by the country’s deputy chief justice to examine the claims. Law professor Firoz Cachalia has been appointed as interim police minister during the probe.  The allegations against Mchunu were first detailed by KwaZulu-Natal provincial police chief Gen Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi, who accused the minister of orchestrating the disbandment of a 2018 task force investigating political murders. Mkhwanazi alleged that Mchunu, with support from controversial businessman Vusimuzi Matlala, ordered the removal of 121 case files from the unit, halting critical investigations. 'These dockets have sat untouched since March, with five cases already ready for arrests,' Mkhwanazi stated, sharing text messages and payment records as evidence. Matlala, previously linked to a lucrative police contract before his May arrest for attempted murder, is alleged to have financially supported Mchunu’s political career.  Mchunu has denied the accusations, calling them 'wild allegations' and vowing to cooperate with the investigation. In a statement, he asserted, 'Honour and integrity are the virtues I personally subscribe to,' while acknowledging Ramaphosa’s decision. The president, under growing public pressure to address corruption, has faced scrutiny over Mchunu’s influential role in the African National Congress (ANC). The judicial commission will probe not only Mchunu but also current and former police officials and national executives, signaling a broad effort to address systemic issues. The case has intensified political tensions, with Ramaphosa’s recent cancellation of a trip to Spain underscoring the gravity of the crisis.
