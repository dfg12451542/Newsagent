A historic 800-year-old leper hospital in Warwick is set for a £2.8m restoration to transform its two 15th-century Grade II* listed buildings into affordable homes. The St Michael’s Place project, led by West Midlands Heritage and Warwick District Council, aims to rescue the derelict Master’s House and St Michael’s Chapel in Saltisford, which have stood abandoned for over five decades. Funding from the National Lottery Heritage Fund will support the preservation of the site, renowned for its medieval heritage, while adapting it for modern use.  Councillor Helen Adkins emphasized the dual goals of preservation and sustainability, stating, 'We will be able to preserve the legacy of the site and rescue these long-neglected historic buildings, offering them a sustainable future.' The project also includes community-focused initiatives, such as a heritage community assistant role, workshops on traditional craft skills, and public events to engage locals with the site’s history. Corinne Brazier, chair of West Midlands Heritage, praised the collaborative effort, crediting 'the work of our colleagues and the support of the local community' for reaching this milestone.  The restoration marks a new chapter for the site, which once served as a leper hospital during the Middle Ages. 'We are now able to ensure that St Michael’s Place will have a very homely future,' Brazier added. The initiative balances historical conservation with social value, converting the buildings into affordable rented homes while safeguarding their architectural significance for future generations.
