A fire that devastated a Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA) club in west Belfast was deliberately started, police confirmed on Sunday. The blaze, which broke out at approximately 18:20 BST on Saturday, caused 'extensive damage' to the facility, according to Insp Dalton of the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI). 'It’s absolutely unacceptable that a community organisation should be targeted in this way,' he added, emphasizing the gravity of the attack on a local institution.  Belfast city councillor Arder Carson condemned the incident as a 'blatant act of vandalism,' highlighting the club’s vital role in supporting youth through summer camps and upcoming championship preparations. 'A club that does so much for the local community... has been subject to such an attack,' Carson said, expressing both despair and anger. He also criticized the recklessness of the act, noting the prolonged dry weather had heightened the risk in the rural, green area.  The attack echoes a 2023 fire at a GAA club in County Antrim, where a scoreboard, shop, and signage were destroyed. Police are now urging witnesses or those with information to come forward. 'Setting fire outdoors at any time is reckless, but this level is just beyond reckless,' Carson remarked, underscoring the broader implications for public safety and community trust.
