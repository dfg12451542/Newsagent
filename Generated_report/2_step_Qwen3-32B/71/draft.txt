The FIFA Club World Cup has faced sharp criticism from global player union Fifpro, with General Secretary Sergio Marchi condemning the tournament’s organization as a 'fiction' prioritizing profit over player welfare. In a strongly worded statement, Marchi accused FIFA President Gianni Infantino of adopting an 'authoritarian' approach, dismissing concerns about athlete health and working conditions. 'What was presented as a global celebration of football was nothing more than a fiction created by FIFA, promoted by its president, without dialogue, sensitivity, and respect for those who sustain the game,' Marchi said, drawing parallels between the tournament’s grandiose staging and the 'bread and circuses' of Nero’s Rome.  The criticism follows revelations that Fifpro was excluded from a key player welfare meeting held by Infantino on the eve of the Club World Cup final in New York. Marchi alleged that the tournament was conducted under 'unacceptable conditions' that endangered players’ physical integrity, exacerbating concerns about an overcrowded international calendar and insufficient rest periods. 'We cannot continue to play with the health of players to fuel a marketing machine,' he warned, emphasizing that FIFA’s unilateral decisions risked normalizing unsustainable practices ahead of the 2026 World Cup.  FIFA has defended its actions, stating that the New York talks 'addressed key issues concerning player health,' including the international match calendar. However, Marchi dismissed these efforts as insufficient, accusing the governing body of prioritizing economic gains over athlete safety. 'There is no spectacle possible if the voices of the protagonists are silenced,' he said, urging FIFA to heed warnings about systemic inequalities and precarious working conditions in football. The dispute underscores growing tensions between player representatives and FIFA as the sport’s schedule becomes increasingly demanding.
