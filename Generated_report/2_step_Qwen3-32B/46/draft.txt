HBO's new television adaptation of J.K. Rowling's *Harry Potter* has officially begun production, with the first image of the young star in costume released Monday. The series, announced earlier this year following the casting of three child actors as Harry, Ron, and Hermione in May, now introduces 11-year-old Dominic McLaughlin as Harry Potter. The reveal marks a major milestone for the long-anticipated project, which aims to reimagine the iconic fantasy saga for a new generation.      Joining McLaughlin in pivotal roles are Rory Wilmot as Neville Longbottom, Amos Kitson as Dudley Dursley, Louise Brealey as Madam Rolanda Hooch, and Anton Lesser as Garrick Ollivander. The cast also welcomes returning film series stars, including John Lithgow as Albus Dumbledore, Nick Frost as Rubeus Hagrid, Janet McTeer as Minerva McGonagall, and Paapa Essiedu as Severus Snape. Producers highlighted the inclusion of acclaimed talent from *The Crown*, with director of photography Adriano Goldman and hair and makeup designer Cate Hall leading key creative roles.      The first season is slated for a 2027 debut, with production underway across multiple locations. Fans of the original book and film series have expressed anticipation for the new take, though some have called for fresh perspectives to distinguish the adaptation from its cinematic predecessor. The series will explore the early years of Harry’s journey at Hogwarts, setting the stage for a multi-season exploration of the Wizarding World.
