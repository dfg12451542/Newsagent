England's hopes of salvaging the Lord's Test against India were reignited on the fourth day as Brydon Carse delivered a masterclass in pressure bowling, dismissing India's Karun Nair and captain Shubman Gill to keep the hosts in the contest. With India 58-4 at the close, 135 adrift of their target of 193, the match teeters on a knife's edge ahead of a decisive final session. 'We would have loved 250 and beyond but we would have taken 190 and our chance to bowl on that pitch,' said England batting coach Marcus Trescothick, acknowledging the challenge of navigating a pitch that has claimed 12 England wickets—a record for a home Test.      The day unfolded in dramatic fashion, with chaos punctuating England's batting collapse. Harry Brook was bowled sweeping Akash Deep for 23, while Joe Root anchored a fragile innings with a gritty 40, facing 96 balls without a boundary. Washington Sundar, India's all-rounder, celebrated his 200th Test appearance by dismissing both Root and Stokes, declaring, 'Every wicket was very, very crucial' in a match that could decide the series. His confidence was echoed by India's fans, who chanted for Jasprit Bumrah as the seamer 'spit' deliveries at Zak Crawley, setting the tone for a searing afternoon.      Historical parallels loom large, with England's 2022 record chase of 378 against India at Lord's casting a shadow over India's tactical calculations. 'It was absolutely gripping—World Cup finals don’t generate that kind of madness,' remarked BBC commentator Jonathan Agnew, capturing the electric atmosphere as tensions flared between teams after a late-day 'handbags' incident. Despite England's batting woes, Carse's intervention and Ben Stokes' final-over dismissal of Akash Deep kept the hosts alive. The stage is set for a climactic finale, with India's decision to declare—and England's determination to avoid a draw—promising further drama at cricket's spiritual home.
