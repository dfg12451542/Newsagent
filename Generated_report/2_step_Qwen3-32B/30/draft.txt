The 2025 Evian Championship concluded in dramatic fashion as South Korea's Grace Kim claimed her first major title in a three-way playoff, edging out close friend Atthaya Thitikul of Thailand and England's Lottie Woad. Kim secured victory with a 12-foot eagle putt on the second replay of the 18th hole, capping a tense showdown after both she and Thitikul finished regulation at 14 under par. Woad, who had set the clubhouse target with a seven-under 64, fell just short despite a stellar performance that saw her earn two points toward LPGA Tour eligibility via the Elite Amateur Pathway.      The playoff hinged on clutch moments throughout the final round. Thitikul missed an eight-foot birdie putt on the 18th that would have secured her a maiden major, while Woad lamented her chances after racing a 10-foot birdie putt past the hole earlier in the day. 'Is there even any point, she's not making six,' Woad remarked to her caddie as Thitikul surged late, a moment underscoring the razor-thin margins in championship golf. Kim, meanwhile, steadied her game with a two-foot putt for birdie on the 18th to join Thitikul in the playoff.      Woad, who triumphed at the Irish Open last week, remains in contention for an LPGA Tour spot, having reached 20 points—the threshold for the Elite Amateur Pathway. 'I'm going to discuss it with my family and coaches,' she said post-tournament, adding that turning professional has 'always been in my dreams.' Her performance, though falling short of victory, reinforced her status as a rising star. Kim's win marks a breakthrough for the 24-year-old, who now joins an elite cohort of major champions. Historical context shows Woad's high finish at Evian could prove pivotal, echoing past instances where strong major performances propelled amateur golfers to professional careers.
