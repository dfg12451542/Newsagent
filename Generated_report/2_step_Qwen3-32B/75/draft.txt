American golfer Chris Gotterup clinched his second PGA Tour title by edging out world number two Rory McIlroy at the 2025 Scottish Open, finishing at 15 under par with a final-round 66 to secure a two-shot victory at the Renaissance Club. The world number 158, who entered the day tied with McIlroy at the top, showcased composure under pressure, including a pivotal birdie on the par-five 16th hole to maintain his lead. Gotterup, in just his second competitive appearance on a links course, earned £1.2 million and a debut at The Open next week at Royal Portrush, where McIlroy will defend his Claret Jug title.      'I just hung in there tight and tried to keep it together,' said Gotterup, who birdied three of his first eight holes to build momentum. The 25-year-old later reflected on the emotional weight of the win: 'It’s amazing. It’s all hitting me. It’s just so cool.' McIlroy, a former Scottish Open champion, saw his challenge wane on the back nine, finishing second alongside England’s Marco Penge. Despite the loss, the Northern Irishman remained positive, acknowledging Gotterup’s 'great round' and noting the 'pretty windy' conditions made precision difficult.      The victory marks a breakthrough for Gotterup, who overcame early nerves with a bogey on the first hole before rallying strongly. Meanwhile, McIlroy’s near-miss adds to his mixed recent history at the event—he had previously won in 2022 but has yet to secure back-to-back titles here. Other notable performers included Matt Fitzpatrick and Nicolai Hojgaard, who finished three shots off the lead, while Xander Schauffele and Scottie Scheffler joined McIlroy at nine under.      With his triumph, Gotterup not only claimed a coveted Open berth but also upstaged one of golf’s global stars, delivering a memorable performance on a course known for its exacting challenges. As the focus shifts to Royal Portrush, both players will aim to build on this momentum ahead of July’s major championship.
