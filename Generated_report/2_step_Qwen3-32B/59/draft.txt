Shubhanshu Shukla, the first Indian astronaut to visit the International Space Station (ISS), is set to return to Earth after a historic mission. The Axiom-4 (Ax-4) crew, including Shukla, undocked from the ISS on Monday and is scheduled to splash down off the coast of California on 15 July at 09:30 GMT (15:00 India time). During a live broadcast, the crew was seen departing the orbiting laboratory, marking the culmination of a mission that extended beyond its initial two-week plan to conduct 60 scientific experiments, seven of which were designed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (Isro).  Speaking from space, Group Captain Shukla reflected on his journey, stating, 'It has been an incredible journey... The journey of our human space mission is very long and difficult. But if we are determined, even the stars are attainable.' He also described India's perspective from orbit as 'ambitious, fearless, confident, and proud,' emphasizing the nation's growing space ambitions. Isro, which invested 5 billion rupees ($59 million) to secure Shukla's seat and training, highlighted that his hands-on experience will directly inform India's future human spaceflight programs.  This mission marks a significant milestone for India, as Shukla is only the second Indian to travel to space, following cosmonaut Rakesh Sharma's 1984 flight aboard a Russian Soyuz. Sharma's journey occurred during the Soviet era, while Shukla's mission underscores India's modern, self-driven advancements in space exploration. Before his launch, Shukla called the past year 'transformative,' expressing that he carried 'the hopes and dreams of a billion hearts.' His return signals not just the end of a personal odyssey, but a new chapter for India's aspirations among the stars.
