The Sudanese Rapid Support Forces (RSF) launched a devastating assault on the besieged city of el-Fasher on Friday, capturing a cattle market, prison, and military base in a seven-hour battle, witnesses reported. The attack, described by Mathilde Vu of the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) as a 'death trap,' has intensified the humanitarian crisis in the city, where weekly shelling and targeted attacks on civilians have become routine. 'What we're hearing is stories of horror and terror,' Vu said, citing weekly attacks on infrastructure and the harrowing exodus of residents fleeing by foot or donkey to escape armed men and sexual violence.  Residents of el-Fasher painted a grim picture of survival amid the chaos. Siddig Omar, a 65-year-old local, recounted how an RSF shell struck a civilian vehicle near his home, killing five people. Others described a city stripped of basic necessities: 'There is no bread, no food, and no work... Even if you have money, there's nothing available,' one resident said. Hospitals lack medicine, and makeshift shelters in the neighboring town of Tawila—home to 379,000 displaced people—are at risk of destruction from heavy rains, compounding a cholera outbreak. 'We are in a very critical situation,' another resident added, noting even staple foods like ombaz (peanut-based paste) are scarce.  Historical context reveals the attack as part of a protracted conflict: the Sudanese army has now launched 220 offensives against the RSF, which has repeatedly targeted el-Fasher from surrounding trenches. Despite the army's Saturday counteroffensive repelling the RSF beyond the city limits, drone shelling continued, and Vu emphasized dwindling aid as a dire consequence. 'People [in el-Fasher] just rely on the solidarity of others,' she said, noting families share meager rations while calling for urgent international assistance. 'We are calling out for help—please, we urgently need assistance,' a resident pleaded, encapsulating the desperation of a city trapped in cycles of violence and neglect.
