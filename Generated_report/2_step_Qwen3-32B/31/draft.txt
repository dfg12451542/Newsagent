Actor Sean Bean has condemned a proposed housing development on green belt land in his hometown of Sheffield, calling it an attack on a working-class community. In a strongly worded objection submitted to the council, Bean, 66, stated the plan would 'practically obliterate the character and nature of our area' and accused local authorities of treating residents as 'second-class citizens who will just have to put up with it.' The development, which targets the S13 area of Handsworth, has sparked fierce opposition from campaigners who argue it disproportionately impacts working-class neighborhoods while wealthier parts of the city remain untouched.      Sadie Charlton, a campaigner with Save S13 Green Belt - The Sapphire McCarthy Campaign, praised Bean's involvement, saying his public support 'boosted our morale' and validated the community's concerns. The campaign, named in honor of late activist Sapphire McCarthy, gained momentum after Charlton, an occupational therapist, reached out to Bean following McCarthy's death. Bean had previously connected with McCarthy, who spearheaded the opposition before her passing. 'He was really interested in keeping in touch about how the campaign is going because obviously he cares about Handsworth,' Charlton noted, highlighting Bean's ongoing engagement with the cause.      The consultation period for the housing plan closed on 11 July, but campaigners remain determined. Charlton emphasized that the community's solidarity has grown since the campaign began, stating, 'This is just the beginning for us.' She also reflected on the political awakening in S13, admitting she 'didn’t even know what a local plan was' before joining the fight. With Bean's high-profile backing and a renewed grassroots effort, opponents of the development are vowing to continue their push to protect the green belt, framing the issue as a battle for social equity and environmental preservation in Sheffield.
