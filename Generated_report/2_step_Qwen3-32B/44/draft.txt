Donegal manager Jim McGuinness has asserted that his side has 'deserved the right' to face Kerry in the 2025 All-Ireland Senior Football Championship final, following a dominant semi-final victory over Meath. The Ulster champions secured their place in the decider with a commanding 3-26 to 0-15 win at Croke Park, with second-half goals from Oisin Gallen, Ciaran Moore, and Conor O'Donnell proving decisive. 'Full credit to the boys, they have done a brilliant job, and we got over the line today,' McGuinness told BBC Sport, reflecting on the team's performance.  McGuinness acknowledged the challenge ahead, with Kerry—a side renowned for their All-Ireland pedigree and Croke Park expertise—poised as formidable opponents. 'We know what we will get with Kerry, they are a team who know their way around Croke Park like the back of their hand,' he noted. The Donegal manager emphasized the significance of the opportunity, stating, 'We’ve deserved the right to play that game and I’m delighted they’ve got that opportunity.' The final is scheduled for 27 July.  This marks a pivotal moment for McGuinness, who returned to manage Donegal in 2024 after previously guiding them to All-Ireland glory in 2012 and 2014. His tenure last year ended in heartbreak, as the team was eliminated in the semi-finals by Galway. 'Please god, now they can take it,' he said, expressing hope that this year’s campaign will culminate in a historic title. With preparations underway, McGuinness urged focus, stating, 'We have to bring the highest level possible to our performance' as Donegal aims to end their 17-year All-Ireland drought.
