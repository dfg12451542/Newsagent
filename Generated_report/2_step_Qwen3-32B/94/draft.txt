South Korean medical students are set to return to classrooms after ending a 17-month boycott, marking a significant development in the country's ongoing healthcare crisis. The Korean Medical Association (KMA) announced that thousands of students will resume classes, though no specific timeline has been provided. The move follows prolonged protests against government policies, including a controversial plan to cap annual medical school enrollments. According to Yonhap News Agency, 8,305 students will face grade retention, requiring them to repeat their academic year due to missed coursework.  Prime Minister Kim Min-Seok hailed the decision as a 'big step forward,' emphasizing the need for collaboration between the government, Congress, and citizens to address systemic issues in healthcare. 'It's time to take a deeper look at the medical field, the Congress, and the government, so that citizens can help solve problems,' he stated. The KMA pledged to 'restore the academic calendar and improve training conditions,' expressing trust in the government and parliament to facilitate reforms.  The boycott, which began in early 2023, coincided with widespread protests by junior doctors over grueling working conditions and staffing shortages. While students prepare to return, resident physicians continue their strike, highlighting unresolved tensions within the healthcare sector. Analysts note that the students' decision may ease immediate pressures on medical education but underscores the broader challenges facing South Korea's healthcare system, including workforce sustainability and policy reforms.
