Cirencester's historic Old Station, designed by Isambard Kingdom Brunel and closed in 1965, is set to be temporarily reopened as part of the Cirencester History Festival from 25 October to 1 November 2025. Festival producer Jess Yarrow has called on residents to share memories, photographs, or memorabilia from the station's 'golden age of rail,' aiming to recreate its atmosphere with prams, benches, and vintage luggage. 'We know that there are lots of people out there who can remember the station in its heyday,' said Ms. Yarrow, noting its role in wartime as a hub for evacuees and soldiers. 'We’d love to hear stories and see anything tucked away that brings its history to life.'  The Grade-II listed building, which many now see as 'a slightly sad, empty-looking structure,' holds hidden treasures, including a boardroom where Brunel sought a meeting space outside London. Architect Jonathan Rixon, who is refurbishing the station for his offices, emphasized its significance: 'It’s important to protect our industrial heritage. I’m passionate about preserving this piece of history.' Visitors will tour the site, experience a virtual reality recreation of the Victorian-era station by Cirencester College students, and engage with an audio project sharing oral histories.  Free events on both festival Saturdays will celebrate the station's legacy, from its bustling 19th-century operations to its poignant wartime role. Ms. Yarrow hopes to reconnect the community with a landmark that, while faded, remains a testament to an era of steam trains and global connections—captured in archival photos like a 1940s image of station workers smiling beside a steam engine.
