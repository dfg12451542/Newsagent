Rory McIlroy closed the 2025 Scottish Open at The Renaissance Club in North Berwick with a strong performance, finishing 13-under par after 19 birdies across the week, though he fell short of claiming the title. The Northern Irishman reflected on the tournament with optimism, emphasizing his satisfaction with his gameplay despite the near miss. 'No frustration; it's been a great week,' McIlroy said, highlighting his consistency over four rounds. 'I'm really happy with where my game is; the way I played over the weekend; the shots that I hit, how I controlled my ball flight. Missing the trophy, that's about it.'      Challenging conditions, including wind and tricky greens, played a role in McIlroy's narrow defeat. 'It was pretty windy and was hard to get the ball super close, but I gave myself plenty of chances coming down the stretch,' he noted. He also acknowledged the difficulty of the putting surfaces, admitting, 'I feel like the greens got a little bumpy. I had some good putts that just didn't go in, whether they were mis-reads or poor speed.' Despite these hurdles, McIlroy viewed the week as a success, stating he achieved his goals ahead of the upcoming Open Championship.      The Scottish Open marked McIlroy's return to competitive form after a season of mixed results. With the Open Championship set to begin at Portrush next week, the four-time major champion expressed eagerness to shift focus. 'I feel like I've gotten out of this week what I wanted,' he said. 'I'm looking forward to getting to Portrush tonight and getting out on to the golf course early tomorrow.' McIlroy's resilience and adaptability under pressure continue to position him as a key contender in the golfing world, as he aims to add another major title to his legacy.
