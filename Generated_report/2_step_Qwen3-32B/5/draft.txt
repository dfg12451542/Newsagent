Trust in policing has seen a significant boost in the Peterborough area following the implementation of the Alliance project, a collaborative initiative targeting organized crime in the Millfield community. According to Peterborough City Council, the project—centered on Lincoln Road—has led to a 700% increase in community intelligence reporting and enhanced public confidence in law enforcement. The council also reported the seizure of 700,000 illegal cigarettes, 20,000 vapes, and £115,000 in cash during the operation, alongside the installation of new drug needle bins to address public health concerns.      The Alliance project, a partnership between the council and Cambridgeshire Police, aimed to disrupt criminal networks and improve safety in public spaces. Insp Sam Tucker of Cambridgeshire Police acknowledged the initiative’s potential, stating, 'We can definitely use some of the concepts in there but to use this initiative in its entirety is unlikely,' highlighting the project’s intensity and focus on long-term sustainability. However, a recent council report revealed challenges in engaging local groups, with external efforts struggling to make inroads in some community sectors.      Historical context underscores the importance of trust-building in policing. Det Supt Jon Capps previously emphasized the significance of community trust, noting, 'Coming forward to the police is a very big step,' and stressing the need for continued efforts to strengthen relationships. While the Alliance project has demonstrated measurable success, authorities have indicated that immediate expansion to other areas of Peterborough is unlikely. Police agencies involved remain committed to securing funding to sustain the initiative, recognizing its role as an 'intense project about longevity and sustainability' in fostering safer communities.
