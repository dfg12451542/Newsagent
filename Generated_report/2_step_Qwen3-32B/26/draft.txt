A historic steam locomotive has begun a new chapter at the North Norfolk Railway in Sheringham, over nine years after its remarkable discovery and restoration. On Saturday, the engine, which had been absent from active service for decades, successfully pulled passenger carriages for the first time since its refurbishment. Alex Alder, a key figure in the locomotive’s rediscovery, called the moment 'absolutely incredible,' noting that it marked a milestone in the engine’s long journey from obscurity to revival. 'After nine years of efforts, for the very first time ever she pulled passenger trains,' Alder said, reflecting on the project’s scale and dedication.  The locomotive’s origins trace back to a serendipitous tip-off that led Alder to a Hertfordshire convent, where the engine had been stored for years. 'Discovering the engine was like hearing of a mythical Egyptian tomb,' Alder recalled, describing the 39-ton machine as 'a miracle' to behold. The restoration, costing £200,000 and undertaken by a team of volunteers, took nearly a decade to complete. The engine’s return to service now includes regular runs between Sheringham and Holt, with Graham Hukins of the North Norfolk Railway calling it 'absolutely wonderful' to witness. 'This weekend is the very first time it has been paired with passenger carriages,' Hukins added, celebrating both the locomotive’s revival and the railway’s 50th anniversary.  Looking ahead, the locomotive will serve a dual role. For six months of the year, it will be used for filming before returning to the North Norfolk Railway. This arrangement ensures the engine’s legacy continues both as a working heritage asset and a cinematic icon, bridging its storied past with new adventures on the tracks and the screen.
