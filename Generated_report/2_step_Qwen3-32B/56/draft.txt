A 55-mile ultramarathon runner was airlifted to safety in the Lake District after collapsing from heat-related illness during the grueling mountain race. The incident occurred in Grisedale Valley at approximately 16:00 BST on Saturday, prompting a coordinated rescue effort involving Patterdale Mountain Rescue Team (PMRT), North West Ambulance Service, and the coastguard. Witnesses reported the runner became pale, vomited, and lost consciousness, necessitating urgent medical intervention.  Rescuers deployed a helicopter to winch the athlete to safety, transporting him to the King George playing field in Patterdale for initial medical assessments. PMRT confirmed the operation, which lasted two and a half hours, required 11 team members and coastguard support. The runner was subsequently evaluated by a team doctor at Patterdale Base before being transferred to a hospital for further checks. Extreme heat conditions in the region have raised concerns about the safety of endurance events, with organizers in Eryri (Snowdonia) recently canceling races ranging from 10K to ultra-marathon distances.  This incident underscores the risks posed by rising temperatures to athletes competing in remote, high-altitude environments. PMRT emphasized the critical role of rapid response systems in such emergencies, as health officials continue to monitor the impact of prolonged heatwaves on public activities across the UK.
