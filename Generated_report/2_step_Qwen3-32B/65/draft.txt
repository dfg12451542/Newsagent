Chelsea secured a 3-0 victory over Paris St-Germain in the 2025 Club World Cup final in New Jersey on Sunday, but the post-match ceremony took an unexpected turn when former U.S. President Donald Trump remained on stage during the trophy lift. Trump, who had been invited to present the trophy, stayed alongside captain Reece James and goalkeeper Robert Sanchez as James hoisted the trophy, prompting confusion among players and officials. 'They told me he was going to present the trophy and then exit the stage,' James remarked, 'but he wanted to stay.' FIFA president Gianni Infantino eventually moved to the rear of the stage, allowing Trump to remain briefly before departing with him.      During the match, Trump watched the game with Infantino and shared remarks with Dazn at halftime, stating he was 'having a really great time.' In a later interview, he praised Infantino for his work in 'soccer'—a term he contrasted with 'football,' saying, 'I guess we call it soccer. I’m not sure that change could be made very easily.' When asked if he could issue an executive order to rename the sport, Trump quipped, 'I think we could do that. I think I could do that.'      The Club World Cup title grants Chelsea the distinction of 'world champions' until the 2029 competition, with the club set to display a commemorative badge on their kits. This victory follows historical moments such as the 2021 Club World Cup win, though this year’s final stood out for its high-profile political presence. Cole Palmer, who described being 'a bit confused' by Trump’s extended stay, highlighted the unusual nature of the ceremony. As the golden era of global club football continues, Chelsea’s triumph adds another chapter to the intersection of sports and celebrity involvement in the sport.
