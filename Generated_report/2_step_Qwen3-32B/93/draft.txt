Tyneside has seen a mix of cultural tributes, infrastructure challenges, and academic recognitions this week. A permanent memorial to the Sycamore Gap tree, illegally cut down two years ago, was unveiled in Northumberland. A section of its iconic trunk is now on display at the Sill National Landscape Discovery Centre, where visitors can touch it—a decision praised by artist Charlie Winney, who remarked, 'I’ve been blown away by how huggable it is.' The tree, a symbol of the region’s natural heritage, had drawn global attention after its vandalism, and the memorial aims to preserve its legacy.  Meanwhile, the Tyne and Wear Metro faced renewed service disruptions as three new trains were taken out of commission due to water leakage faults. The issue mirrors previous air-conditioning problems that plagued the fleet, raising concerns about the reliability of the upgraded system. Transport bosses are working to resolve the technical setbacks, which have sparked frustration among commuters.  In a separate development, Newcastle University announced that historian and broadcaster David Olusoga will receive an honorary degree this summer. The accolade recognizes his contributions to public understanding of history, particularly his work on colonialism and social justice. The week’s events highlight Tyneside’s blend of community resilience, infrastructure hurdles, and cultural achievements.
