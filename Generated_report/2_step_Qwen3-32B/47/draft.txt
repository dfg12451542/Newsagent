The operator of a waste recycling site in South Elmsall, Minore (Mineral Processing Ltd), has appealed against the Environment Agency’s (EA) decision to suspend its environmental permit over health hazard concerns. Carly Chambers, EA area manager for Yorkshire, confirmed the appeal has been lodged, stating the permit will remain in place until the Planning Inspectorate rules on the case. However, the suspension notice itself stays active, prohibiting additional waste from entering the site and mandating the removal of waste violating permit conditions. Non-compliance, Chambers warned, would constitute an offence. She emphasized the EA’s awareness of the site’s community impact, noting, 'our increased regulatory response continues' to address concerns.  The EA’s action follows historical patterns of intervention at waste sites, such as the suspension of permits in Basildon for similar health and environmental risks. In South Elmsall, the agency alleges the facility poses a public health threat, though Minore disputes this. A company spokesperson asserted operations are currently compliant, stating the site is 'excavating and processing materials' in line with the suspension notice. They highlighted regular water sampling showing no adverse effects on Frickley Beck and expressed concern over job losses, noting employees have worked there for over 12 years.  The dispute underscores tensions between regulatory oversight and business continuity. While the EA prioritizes public safety, Minore contends its practices meet legal standards. The outcome of the appeal will determine whether the permit is reinstated or revoked, with the site’s future and community relations hanging in the balance.
