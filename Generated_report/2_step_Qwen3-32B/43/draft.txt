Australia's tour of the West Indies took a dramatic turn on day one of the day/night Test as the visitors collapsed to 225 all out, trailing the home side by 209 runs. The tourists, who had appeared in control at 157-3, lost their final seven wickets for just 68 runs, with Steve Smith (48) and Cameron Green (46) unable to steady the innings after their dismissals around the dinner break. Green later described the challenging conditions, stating, 'Every pitch has been tough. The more balls you spend in the middle you hope it gets better but it just hasn't been the case,' while acknowledging the need for adaptation against West Indies' bowlers.      West Indies' bowlers exploited the tricky conditions, with Shamar Joseph leading the charge with figures of 4-33. Jayden Seales and Justin Greaves each claimed three wickets, dismantling Australia's middle order. The home side ended the day at 16-1, with Mitchell Starc marking his 100th Test appearance by dismissing opener Kevlon Anderson for three. Starc, only the second Australian pace bowler after Glenn McGrath to reach this milestone, capitalized on Anderson's inside edge to leave West Indies in a strong position.      This match echoes the low-scoring patterns seen in the first Test in Barbados, where Australia secured a 159-run victory despite neither side surpassing 200 in the opening three innings. With both teams struggling against the conditions, the contest appears poised for an early conclusion, raising questions about the pitch's consistency and the batsmen's ability to adapt. As Green noted, 'It's a bit of a grind first time with these balls, but a lot of learnings. Time in the middle is key,' highlighting the challenges facing the touring side in this pivotal Test series.
