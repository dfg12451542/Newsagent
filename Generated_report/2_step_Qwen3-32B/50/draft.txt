A climber sustained a head injury after falling approximately 3 meters at Hoffman Quarry in Buxton, Derbyshire, on Friday, prompting a mountain rescue operation. The incident occurred at around 11:22 BST, when the Buxton Mountain Rescue Team was called to the site at Harper Hill. A spokesperson confirmed the climber briefly lost consciousness following the fall, though further details about his condition remain unspecified.      Emergency services coordinated an air ambulance to extract the injured man from the quarry. He was airlifted to a location where paramedics awaited transfer to a hospital for further treatment. The operation highlighted the challenges of rescuing individuals from the quarry’s rugged terrain, a site known for both recreational climbing and past safety concerns.      An image captured by authorities shows an air ambulance hovering above the quarry against a clear blue sky, underscoring the remote nature of the rescue. While no historical data on prior incidents at the site was available, the event has reignited discussions about safety measures for climbers in the area. Local authorities have not yet released additional information about the climber’s identity or the circumstances leading to the fall.
