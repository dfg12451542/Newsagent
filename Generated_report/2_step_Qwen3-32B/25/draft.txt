The Royal Observatory Greenwich has unveiled the shortlist for its 2025 ZWO Astronomy Photographer of the Year competition, showcasing breathtaking images that highlight the cosmos' beauty and the dedication of astrophotographers worldwide. Sam Wen, founder and CEO of ZWO, emphasized the competition's mission, stating, 'we believe that astrophotography is not only a way to record the cosmos, but also a way to inspire curiosity, foster education and build communities that transcend borders.' He added that the initiative aims to 'bring the experience of connecting with the Universe to more people.'      This year's shortlist features a diverse array of celestial phenomena. Among them is a striking image of Comet C/2023 A3 captured in Hawaii on October 12, 2024, and a vivid aurora over Norway's Senja. Another standout entry, shaped like a pomegranate, was photographed by Deqian Li after six days of camping in China's Hongyuan County. Technical marvels also abound, such as an image composed of 300 individual shots and a dramatic volcanic eruption documented in November 2024.      The winners of the competition's nine categories, along with two special prizes and the overall winner, will be announced on September 11, 2025. A public exhibition of the shortlisted works will run from September 12 at the National Maritime Museum. Historically, similar competitions—such as the inaugural International Aerial Photographer of the Year event, which welcomed global submissions from both professionals and amateurs—have highlighted the growing interest in capturing Earth and space from unique perspectives. The most recent such event occurred in 2022, underscoring the enduring appeal of astronomical and aerial photography in bridging science and art.
