As Live Aid celebrates its 40th anniversary, the legacy of Maurice Jones, a humble West Midlands rock promoter whose pivotal role in the iconic 1985 concert has remained largely unrecognized, is being revisited through the memories of his daughter, Nicola. Jones, who was born in Wednesbury and lived in Walsall, was approached for his industry expertise to help organize the event, overseeing critical aspects such as venue logistics, backstage catering, and artist bookings. His name even appeared prominently at the top of the event’s ticket, though his contributions were overshadowed by the global stars who performed. 'He was very modest. His take on it was that he was there to do a job, he wasn’t there to be in the limelight,' Nicola recalled, emphasizing her father’s unassuming nature.      Nicola, who attended the Wembley concert with her father at age 15, described a once-in-a-lifetime experience. Sitting in the royal box—just rows from David Bowie, Princess Diana, and Prince Charles—she witnessed Freddie Mercury’s legendary Queen performance and mingled backstage with icons like Paul McCartney and Elton John. 'Watching the reaction of the audience was really spectacular,' she said, reflecting on the electric atmosphere that defined Live Aid. Though she admitted her knowledge of Queen was limited at the time, the concert cemented their status as a 'huge band' in her mind.      Despite his integral role, Maurice Jones, who passed away in 2009, never sought public acclaim. 'I think, because of my dad’s nature, he didn’t want to promote himself even though he was very good at promoting bands and acts,' Nicola noted. Historical records highlight his name on the event’s ticket, yet his behind-the-scenes efforts rarely made headlines. Now, as the world reflects on Live Aid’s enduring impact, Nicola hopes her father’s contributions will finally be acknowledged. 'It will be nice if he is remembered in a small way,' she said, expressing pride in his quiet dedication to one of music history’s most transformative events.
