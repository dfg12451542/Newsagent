A memorial bench in Whitby dedicated to Sophie Lancaster, a 20-year-old victim of a 2007 hate crime, has been temporarily relocated to make way for a new giant sightseeing wheel. The Sophie Lancaster Foundation, which maintains the bench on West Cliff, expressed shock at the move, stating they were not consulted. 'For Sylvia, Sophie’s mother, the bench was a tangible connection to Sophie,' a foundation spokesperson said, adding that the decision has caused 'pain' for visitors who use the memorial to reflect on her legacy.  The bench, installed in honor of Sophie—a member of the Goth community murdered by two teenagers in Bacup, Lancashire—has become a focal point for the alternative community since its creation. North Yorkshire Council defended the temporary relocation, calling the wheel a 'respectful, exciting addition' to boost tourism, with plans to restore the bench to its original location on 4 August. Councilor Mark Crane highlighted the wheel’s potential to attract visitors, noting positive feedback from local businesses and residents.  However, Whitby Mayor Sandra Turner condemned the move as a 'serious oversight,' citing a strained relationship between local authorities. The council has since apologized and pledged improved engagement. The foundation, while disappointed, announced plans to rededicate the bench during the Tomorrow’s Ghosts Festival on 1 November. 'Whitby is such a special place for us,' a spokesperson said, praising the outpouring of support from the community. The incident underscores tensions between urban development and preserving cultural memory, as authorities balance growth with honoring historical tragedies.
