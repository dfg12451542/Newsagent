The Wurzels were forced to cut short their performance at the Party at the Palace stage in Wells, Somerset, on Saturday as lead vocalist Pete Budd, 84, and accordionist Tommy Banner fell ill due to heat and sun exposure. Temperatures soared to 31°C during the gig, with Banner describing the stage conditions as having 'no air moving' and exposing the band to 'direct, unobstructed rays of a very strong sun.' The incident has raised concerns amid a broader heatwave gripping the UK.      Banner, 78, recounted the alarming moment Budd collapsed: 'I was about to announce the last song and Pete wasn’t besides me where he normally is. He was in a bad way.' Both musicians were immediately assisted by medical staff and taken offstage, where a nurse evaluated them for heat exhaustion. Banner added he too experienced dizziness and required post-gig care, including exercises to stabilize his condition and advice not to drive.      The event highlights the growing risks posed by extreme heat, particularly for older adults. A recent Imperial College London report found 263 excess deaths linked to heatwaves between June 23 and July 3, underscoring the public health crisis. For The Wurzels, whose members have performed since the 1970s, the incident serves as a stark reminder of the vulnerabilities even seasoned musicians face in escalating climate conditions. Band manager Chris Cook confirmed both Budd and Banner have since recovered but emphasized the need for heightened safety measures at future outdoor events.
