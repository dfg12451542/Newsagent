Thames Water has imposed a hosepipe ban affecting 1.1 million people in Gloucestershire, Oxfordshire, Berkshire, and Wiltshire, citing severe water shortages driven by prolonged dry weather and surging demand. The restriction, set to take effect on 22 July, prohibits non-essential uses like watering gardens or washing cars, with fines of up to £1,000 for violations. The company said the measure is necessary to preserve supplies in areas with postcodes starting with OX, GL, SN, RG4, RG8, and RG9, though businesses reliant on hosepipes—such as car washes—will be exempt.  Nevil Muncaster, Thames Water’s strategic water resources director, emphasized the urgency, stating, 'We have to take action now,' as he highlighted the 'challenging spring and summer' marked by record customer demand and minimal rainfall. He urged all residents to conserve water, suggesting shorter showers and turning off taps during tooth-brushing. However, GMB union representatives criticized the company, with Gary Carter condemning the ban as 'disgraceful' amid rising bills and noting that 570 million litres of water are wasted daily due to leaks.  Thames Water defended its efforts, claiming leakage across its network is at a 'lowest ever level' thanks to technologies like acoustic loggers and smart meters. A spokesperson stated engineers are fixing 650 leaks weekly, but critics argue crumbling infrastructure and insufficient investment have left the company 'with their hands tied.' This ban follows months of warnings from Thames Water about potential restrictions unless rainfall improved—a scenario that has yet to materialize, underscoring the region’s escalating water crisis.
