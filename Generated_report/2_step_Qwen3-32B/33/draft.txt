A Guernsey man in his late 70s was rescued from drowning at Port Grat beach after collapsing in the sea during a swim, thanks to the swift actions of his wife and a fellow swimmer. The incident unfolded on Friday afternoon as Jacky and Chris Meinke, 67 and 77 respectively, waded into the water at the popular bathing spot. According to Jacky Meinke, she heard a splash and initially assumed her husband was behind her. 'When I looked round, his head was face down in the water. I started screaming his name and grabbed his head out of the water—he was completely white,' she recounted. Using her first-aid training, she walloped his back, prompting him to splutter and regain consciousness before they, alongside swimmer Rosie Mclauchlan, hauled him back to shore.  Chris Meinke, who spent the night in hospital, described the experience as 'very scary,' stating, 'I was effectively dead. The hospital thinks I had vertigo—I just didn’t know which way was up.' Medical scans are underway to determine the cause of his sudden collapse. Mclauchlan, who witnessed the ordeal, noted the alarming speed at which the situation escalated: 'He was literally drowning. I was amazed how quickly someone can get into difficulty.' Paramedics and bystanders, including those who retrieved a defibrillator and provided a warm blanket, assisted on the beach before the couple put Meinke in the recovery position.  The incident highlights ongoing water safety efforts in Guernsey, where Alan Jenner, an outdoor sports development officer, emphasizes the importance of educating residents—especially children—about the risks of the island's coastal environment. 'It’s a vital part of learning,' Jenner said, underscoring the role of preparedness in preventing tragedies. The Meinke family expressed profound gratitude for the community’s response, with Jacky Meinke stating, 'I’m so grateful to them all. It was very scary, but I’m just relieved he’s OK.'
