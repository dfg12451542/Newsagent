A Gaza father, Mahmoud Abdul Rahman Ahmed, expressed profound anguish after Israeli airstrikes killed his 12-year-old son, Abdullah, while the boy was queuing for water in the Nuseirat refugee camp on Sunday. 'He was searching for a sip of water,' Mahmoud told the BBC, describing how Abdullah and other displaced civilians gathered with empty jerrycans at a distribution point in the besieged enclave. The strike, which killed 10 people—including six children—and injured 16, occurred as families desperate for basic resources lined up amid ongoing conflict. Graphic footage verified by the BBC showed the aftermath: jerrycans scattered, bodies motionless, and survivors scrambling through rubble as screams echoed.  Emergency services reported that the Israeli military claimed the attack targeted a Palestinian Islamic Jihad militant, citing a 'technical error' that caused munitions to stray. The military expressed regret for civilian casualties but reiterated its actions were 'under review.' Mahmoud condemned the strike as a deliberate message to deny Gaza's population even 'the drinking water they crave,' emphasizing that civilians—particularly children—have borne the brunt of the conflict. 'Their dreams will never be realized,' he said, lamenting the loss of Abdullah and others like him.  UN officials echoed concerns over the humanitarian crisis. Sam Rose of UNRWA described the water queues as a daily struggle, with families receiving meager rations for 'washing, cooking, and drinking.' Catherine Russell of UNICEF called the incident 'horrific,' urging Israel to comply with international humanitarian law. However, Israel’s UN representative, Danny Danon, shifted blame to Hamas, accusing the group of using children as 'human shields' while criticizing the UN for inaction.  This tragedy follows a similar incident last week in Deir al-Balah, where 10 children and three women died waiting for aid. Rose noted that 'a classroom full of children' die daily in Gaza, underscoring the war's relentless toll. For Mahmoud, the loss is personal and universal: 'We are civilians... this war needs to stop.' As grief and anger mount, calls for accountability and an end to the violence grow louder, even as the cycle of conflict and suffering continues.
