Great Britain's Oliver Rowland has claimed his first Formula E championship title after a strategic fourth-place finish in the Berlin ePrix, Germany. The Nissan driver secured the title with two races remaining in the season, becoming the 10th different champion in the series' 11-season history. Rowland reflected on the pressure ahead of the final rounds, stating, 'Honestly, I was just thinking before the race not to have too much damage going into [the final round in] London.' His cautious approach proved pivotal as title rival Pascal Wehrlein, starting on pole for Porsche, crashed to 16th place during the event.      Rowland enters the concluding double-header in London (26-27 July) with a 59-point lead (184 to Wehrlein's 125), ensuring his dominance despite not winning a race this season. 'I can't believe it—I have no words. It's incredible. I was trying to play it safe, but everybody was so aggressive, so at some point I was all in,' Rowland said, balancing prudence with late-race daring. The British driver's triumph marks a significant shift in Formula E's competitive landscape, as manufacturers like Nissan continue to challenge established powerhouses.      Sunday's Berlin race saw Jaguar's Nick Cassidy take victory, with Jake Dennis (Andretti) and Jean-Eric Vergne (DS Penske) rounding out the podium. Rowland, who finished fourth, emphasized the value of clean racing: 'Thankfully I stayed out of trouble.' As Formula E evolves toward its 2026 regulations, Rowland's championship adds to the series' legacy of innovation and unpredictability, even as figures like Red Bull's Christian Horner exit the scene, reflecting on an 'incredible era of motorsport.'
