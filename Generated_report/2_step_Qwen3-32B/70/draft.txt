Oxfordshire is grappling with a rising crisis as one in 38 children are now classified as 'severely' absent from school, with one parent describing her child’s struggle to attend classes as 'harrowing.' Lauren, a parent who spoke under a pseudonym, shared how her child’s anxiety around school escalated to the point where they once told her, 'I don’t want to live any more.' The child, who spends only part of each school day on campus, often waits with Lauren at school for up to two hours, she said. 'You can tell they can’t cope with being there,' Lauren added, reflecting the anguish of watching a child 'not have those opportunities to fit into a normal education system.'  Head teacher Heather Richards acknowledged the challenges, calling chronic absenteeism a 'vicious circle' but highlighted efforts to address it. Schools have introduced targeted sessions to improve attendance, which Richards said have fostered 'real improvement' and stronger peer connections. Lauren’s child, who participates in an online version of these sessions, 'literally leap[s] to go to attend,' she noted, with the child often leaving the sessions 'buoyant and happy' even during difficult weeks.  The Department for Education emphasized the urgency of tackling absenteeism, stating that poor attendance 'could not be clearer' in its negative impact on life outcomes. The government has pledged a 'national effort' to boost attendance through measures like mental health support, family investment, and Ofsted scrutiny. Yet for families like Lauren’s, the emotional toll remains profound. 'Am I imagining these things? Am I a bad parent?' she questioned, capturing the isolation faced by parents navigating a system they feel is failing their children.
