A former student's historic Wimbledon triumph has brought pride to a Wythall school, with physical education teacher Joe Maidment celebrating Lloyd Glasspool's groundbreaking doubles victory. Glasspool, 31, from Redditch, joined partner Julian Cash to become the first all-British duo to win the men’s doubles title at the All England Club in 89 years, marking a milestone in British tennis history. 'To think a student from Woodrush High School in Wythall can win at Wimbledon – it’s just amazing,' Maidment said, reflecting on Glasspool’s journey from school courts to Centre Court.  The victory, achieved on July 13, 2025, caps a remarkable season for Glasspool, who has now secured Wimbledon, Queens, and Eastbourne titles—a feat Maidment hailed as 'the triple crown.' The PE teacher, who coached Glasspool during his school years, shared a lighthearted memory of their past encounters: 'I can now say I’ve played tennis against a Wimbledon champion. Unfortunately, I did lose 6-0, 6-0, and he was only in year eight at the time.' Glasspool himself acknowledged the formative role his teacher played, joking, 'I’ve come a long way since when we played in year seven or eight and I gave you that little thrashing.'  Maidment emphasized the inspirational impact of Glasspool’s success, urging West Midlands residents to 'follow their dreams.' The teacher, who attended the tournament to watch Glasspool’s second-round match, described his performance as 'inspirational.' Post-victory, Glasspool sent a video message to Woodrush High School for its annual sports awards and personally thanked Maidment after the final. 'It gave me a nice little confidence boost to go and do great things in the sport,' Glasspool said of his early experiences. Maidment added, 'I am so, so pleased for him and everyone involved,' underscoring the community’s pride in the champion’s achievements.
