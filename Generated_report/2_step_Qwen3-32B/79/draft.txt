A Salisbury woman celebrated her 100th birthday by completing a 200-mile walking challenge, raising over £11,000 for Salisbury Trust for the Homeless (STFH). Phyll Babb, who finished her final mile around Salisbury Cathedral on Sunday, described the journey as 'delightful' and said, 'I’ve had a ball actually.' Babb, who initially aimed for 100 miles but doubled her target after realizing she was ahead of schedule, credited her lifelong habit of walking, stating, 'I was brought up to walk, so as children we walked everywhere, and I still like walking.'  Family and friends hailed her achievement as inspirational. 'To reach the age of 100 and to do it so fit, looking so well, being so articulate, that’s the really amazing thing she’s done,' said David Evans, a family friend. Ruth Simmons, her great niece, added, 'She is inspirational... she’s been inspirational my whole life.' A local accountant volunteered to track Babb’s progress via Strava, verifying each 20-mile segment in a logbook, which she called 'a delightful way to stay motivated.'  Babb’s fundraising efforts will support her family’s recent home purchase, with proceeds directed toward furnishing the new house, as noted by her nephew, Gordon Pardy. 'To do this in her 100th year was so impressive,' he remarked. The centenarian, who previously celebrated the Wiltshire and Swindon Community Foundation’s 50th anniversary, concluded her challenge with gratitude, thanking supporters for their 'extreme generosity.' Her blend of physical endurance, community spirit, and humor has cemented her as a local icon, proving age is no barrier to ambition.
