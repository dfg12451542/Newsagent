Dave Coldwell, renowned boxing trainer and former mentor to Manny Pacquiao, has revealed he will not watch the Filipino legend’s highly anticipated comeback fight this Saturday. In a candid statement, Coldwell criticized the decision as a 'shortcut' that prioritizes nostalgia over merit, arguing it risks undermining the journey of up-and-coming fighters. 'It feels like a shortcut. A nod to nostalgia rather than merit, and that's unfair to fighters who are working their way up properly,' he said, expressing concern over the precedent set by Pacquiao’s immediate return to high-stakes competition without gradual preparation.      Coldwell, however, emphasized his deep respect for Pacquiao’s legacy, acknowledging the eight-division world champion as 'a global icon, a true legend' whose prime achievements remain unparalleled. Despite this admiration, he questioned the decision to bypass traditional comeback protocols. 'That's the part that really shocked me when I heard of Pacquiao's return,' Coldwell added. 'Usually, a fighter coming back from retirement eases into the sport with tune-up fights. Not Pacquiao. He's skipping all that and going right to the front of the queue.'      The trainer’s remarks follow a public exchange with WBC President Mauricio Sulaiman, who reportedly took issue with Coldwell’s skepticism. Coldwell defended his stance, stating that granting Pacquiao immediate access to top-tier competition 'sets a strange precedent' and overlooks the 'grinding' efforts of fighters who climb the ranks through consistent, challenging bouts. As Pacquiao steps back into the ring, Coldwell’s critique sparks broader debate about equity and tradition in boxing’s evolving landscape.
