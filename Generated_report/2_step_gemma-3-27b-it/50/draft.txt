A climber suffered a head injury after a fall at Hoffman Quarry in Buxton, Derbyshire, on Friday morning. The Buxton Mountain Rescue Team responded to a call at approximately 11:22 BST, arriving at Harper Hill to find a man had fallen while climbing at the popular site. Emergency services swiftly began coordinating a rescue operation.nnThe climber reportedly fell around 3 meters (9.8ft), resulting in a head injury and a brief loss of consciousness, according to a spokesperson. 'The climber fell about 3m (9.8ft) and briefly lost consciousness due to a head injury,' the spokesperson stated. The nature of the injury and the climber’s current condition remain undisclosed.nnDue to the quarry's location and the need for immediate medical attention, the climber was airlifted to a designated landing site. An ambulance was waiting at the site to transport him to a nearby hospital for further treatment. An image taken from the scene shows an air ambulance positioned above the quarry against a clear blue sky.nnHoffman Quarry, a former limestone quarry, has become increasingly popular with climbers in recent years, however, it is also known for its challenging terrain. While the quarry is managed to ensure safe access, incidents do occur, highlighting the inherent risks associated with climbing activities. Authorities remind climbers to prioritize safety measures and be aware of potential hazards when scaling the quarry’s face.
