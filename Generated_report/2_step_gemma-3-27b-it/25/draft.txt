The Royal Observatory Greenwich has unveiled the shortlist for the 2025 ZWO Astronomy Photographer of the Year competition, showcasing breathtaking images of celestial phenomena. From vibrant auroras to distant comets, the collection highlights the beauty and wonder of the cosmos, attracting both amateur and professional astrophotographers from around the globe. The competition, now in its [year - historical data missing, but can imply longstanding tradition], continues to grow in popularity, celebrating the art and science of capturing the night sky.nnFounded on the belief that astrophotography can inspire and educate, ZWO, the competition’s sponsor, plays a vital role in enabling access to this captivating pursuit. “We believe that astrophotography is not only a way to record the cosmos, but also a way to inspire curiosity, foster education and build communities that transcend borders,” stated Sam Wen, founder and CEO of ZWO. This sentiment underscores the competition's broader mission of democratizing access to astronomical observation.nnThe shortlisted images feature remarkable displays like a November 2024 solar eruption lasting roughly an hour, and a unique “pomegranate-shaped” nebula captured after six days of camping in Hongyuan County, China. One striking image portrays Comet C/2023 A3 as it passed over Pu'u O Kaimukī Park in Hawaii on October 12, 2024 - a much anticipated event for astronomy enthusiasts.  Many of the submissions showcase incredible patience and skill, utilizing techniques such as combining hundreds of individual shots to create a single, stunning image.nnPhotographers also demonstrate creative use of timing and environment – as exemplified by an image utilizing 'blue hour' lighting to enhance foreground details in a breathtaking mountain landscape.  Another image captured above the Dolomite mountains in Italy beautifully displays the sunset’s golden light across the peaks.  The winners across nine categories, plus special prizes and the overall champion, will be announced on September 11, 2025, with a public exhibition slated to open at the National Maritime Museum on September 12, offering a closer look at these celestial masterpieces. “Everyone deserves a chance to connect with the Universe — and through our support, we hope to bring that experience to more people,” added Wen.
