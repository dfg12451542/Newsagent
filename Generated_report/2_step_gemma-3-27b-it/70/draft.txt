OXFORDSHIRE - A mother in Oxfordshire has shared the ‘harrowing’ experience of her child’s struggle to attend school, revealing the depths of anxiety that can prevent children from accessing education. The case highlights a growing trend of severe school absenteeism across the UK, with recent data showing one in 38 children in Oxfordshire missing more than half of their school days.nnThe Department for Education acknowledges a ‘broken system’ inherited from previous administrations, emphasizing a need for a ‘national effort’ to improve attendance. A spokesperson stated, “The case for tackling the epidemic of school absence could not be clearer: improved grades, higher wages, better life chances.” They further outlined initiatives like attendance mentors, free breakfast clubs, and increased mental health support as part of a plan to address the issue.nnKnown only as Lauren, the mother described how her child’s anxiety became so acute that they expressed suicidal ideation. “Lauren, not her real name, said her child’s anxiety around school became so severe they once told her they 'didn’t want to live any more',” she recounted. The child found it exceptionally difficult to participate in normal school life, often spending only half an hour a day within the school building.nnLauren described the emotional toll this took on her as a parent, questioning her own abilities and desperately seeking answers. “Am I imagining these things? Am I a bad parent? You question all those things when, deep down, you know your child and you know they’re unhappy,” she said. The frustration of not seeing her child access a ‘normal education system’ was deeply distressing. However, an online support program appears to be offering a lifeline, with Lauren stating that her child “literally leap[s]” to attend sessions, emerging “buoyant and happy” even during difficult weeks.nnHead teacher Heather Richards recognizes the potential for school avoidance to become a “vicious circle,” but also notes improvements in attendance attributed to the support sessions. “They have a stronger connection with some of the children in their year group,” she said. “They also build their confidence.” While similar support systems have met with mixed responses elsewhere – one Essex school reversed its policy after facing parental backlash – Lauren’s experience suggests that targeted interventions can provide crucial support for children struggling with school anxiety.
