The operator of a waste recycling site in South Elmsall, stripped of its licence due to health hazard concerns, has launched an appeal against the decision. Minore, also known as Mineral Processing Ltd, is contesting the Environment Agency's (EA) move, sparking continued anxiety within the local community. The facility, which has operated since the 1970s, initially collecting household waste from East Lancashire, has seen significant expansion, now bordering residential areas, and recently faced scrutiny over its environmental impact.nnA planning inspector’s report released last month identified the site as a potential threat to adjacent watercourses, particularly Frickley Beck. Recent investigations by organizations like the Business and Human Rights Resource Centre have also highlighted broader concerns regarding the environmental and human rights implications of mineral extraction operations. The EA took action following these reports, issuing a notice to suspend operations and requiring the removal of waste breaching permit conditions.nnCarly Chambers, area manager for the EA in Yorkshire, confirmed the appeal. “The environmental permit will now stay in place until the Planning Inspectorate has announced the outcome of this appeal,” she stated. However, she emphasized that “the suspension notice, however, remains in place during this time [This will] prevent more waste being brought onto the site and requires the removal of waste that is in breach of the permit conditions.”  Failure to comply with the suspension notice would constitute an offence, Chambers added.nnDespite the ongoing concerns, Minore maintains its compliance with regulations. A company spokesperson asserted that the site is currently engaged in excavating and processing materials in accordance with the EA's notices. Moreover, they claim regular water sampling has shown no adverse effects on Frickley Beck. However, the company also expressed fears for the livelihood of its employees.  “Our employees have been working at the site for over 12 years and now fear for their jobs due to this escalation,” the spokesperson said, adding that the EA “understood the impact this site is having on the community and our increased regulatory response continues”.
