Warwick, UK – More than £2.8 million has been secured to breathe new life into a historically significant, yet long-neglected, site in Warwick. The funding will transform two 15th-century buildings – the Master’s House and St Michael’s Chapel in Saltisford – into much-needed affordable homes. The ambitious project, known as St Michael’s Place, is being hailed as a success story for heritage preservation and community development.nnThe St Michaels’ Place project received the award from the National Lottery Heritage Fund and aims to “rescue and reuse” these important structures. The buildings have stood derelict for over 50 years, slowly deteriorating despite their historical importance.  The site originally served as a leper hospital, a common feature in medieval England, providing isolation and care for individuals afflicted with the disease.  While details on the hospital's specific operations are scarce, the buildings themselves represent a tangible link to that period and the societal challenges of the time.nnCouncillor Helen Adkins expressed her delight at the funding award, stating, “We will be able to preserve the legacy of the site and rescue these long-neglected historic buildings, offering them a sustainable future.” This sentiment underscores the council’s commitment to both protecting Warwick’s heritage and addressing the need for affordable housing within the district. The project is a collaborative effort between West Midlands Heritage and Warwick District Council.nnThe restoration and conversion into affordable rental homes is intended to be a sensitive process, respecting the historical character of the buildings while providing comfortable and modern living spaces.  Corinne Brazier, chair of West Midlands Heritage, acknowledged the collective effort, saying, “We are now able to ensure that St Michael’s Place will have a very homely future.”  Brazier also credited her colleagues and the local community for reaching this “important delivery stage”.nnBeyond providing housing, the project will also generate community benefits. A new heritage community assistant role will be created, and opportunities to learn about heritage craft skills will be offered. A series of local events and activities are also planned, ensuring that the history of St Michael’s Place is shared with future generations and that the site becomes a valuable asset to the wider community.
