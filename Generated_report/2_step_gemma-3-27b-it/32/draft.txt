Thames Water has announced a hosepipe ban impacting 1.1 million customers across Gloucestershire, Oxfordshire, Berkshire, and Wiltshire, set to take effect on July 22nd. The measure, triggered by prolonged dry weather and heightened demand, prohibits the use of hosepipes for non-essential activities like watering gardens and washing cars, with potential fines of up to £1,000 for non-compliance.  The affected areas correspond to postcodes beginning with OX, GL, SN, RG4, RG8 and RG9. nnNevil Muncaster, Strategic Water Resources Director at Thames Water, emphasized the severity of the situation, stating, “We have to take action now,” and adding that current conditions are unlikely to improve soon.  He urged customers, even those outside the restricted zone, to conserve water through simple measures like turning off taps while brushing teeth and reducing shower times.  This comes amidst concerns about dwindling water supplies, driven by “big spikes in customer demand during hot dry days and very little rainfall.”nnThe ban has sparked criticism, particularly surrounding the company’s performance and investment record. Gary Carter, representing the GMB union, highlighted the alarming level of water wastage, stating, “That’s 570 million litres wasted every single day - the worst in the country.” He further condemned the ban as “disgraceful” given recent bill increases.  Union representatives argue that the situation is a result of “crumbling infrastructure and non-existent investment,” leaving workers unable to address the root causes of leakage.nnWhile Thames Water insists leakage is at its “lowest ever level,” with engineers currently fixing 650 leaks per week, concerns linger about long-term sustainability. The company points to technological advancements, like acoustic loggers and smart meters, as aiding in rapid leak detection and repair. The current crisis however, follows a period of intense scrutiny for Thames Water. The company was recently fined a record £122.7 million by Ofwat for repeated sewage spills and prioritizing shareholder payouts over infrastructure improvements, raising questions about its commitment to responsible water management.
