El-Fasher, Sudan is teetering on the brink of collapse as the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) launched a fierce assault on the city Friday, capturing a cattle market, prison, and military base before being pushed back by the army Saturday. Witnesses describe a seven-hour battle marked by escalating violence and a desperate humanitarian crisis. The RSF broadcast videos showcasing their control over the now-empty livestock market, which has remained closed for months, and briefly held Shalla prison and the headquarters of the military’s Central Reserve Forces.nnDespite the army’s success in repelling the RSF beyond city limits, the situation remains dire. Mathilde Vu, of the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC), labeled el-Fasher a “death trap”, painting a grim picture of ongoing suffering.  “What we’re hearing is stories of horror and terror and weekly shelling, attacks on civilian infrastructure,” Vu stated.  Residents report continued RSF shelling even after the army’s counteroffensive, with Siddig Omar, a 65-year-old resident, detailing how a shell struck a civilian vehicle, killing five people.nnThe escalating conflict is exacerbating a pre-existing humanitarian catastrophe.  Nearly 379,000 people have fled el-Fasher, primarily to Tawila, where they face outbreaks of cholera and the imminent threat of flooding destroying their makeshift shelters.  “People fleeing at night by foot, on donkeys - trying to escape armed men targeting them, maybe raping them,” Vu revealed, highlighting the dangers faced by those attempting to escape the violence.  nnWithin el-Fasher, residents face starvation and a complete lack of essential resources. “Right now, we are suffering deeply, and everyone around us is facing the same hardship,” one resident shared with the BBC Arabic’s emergency radio program.  Reports indicate a severe shortage of food, medicine, and even basic necessities. “There is no bread, no food, and no work to be found. Even if you have money, there's nothing available in the markets to buy,” another resident lamented. The struggling local volunteer efforts are barely enough to offer assistance, as Ms Vu stated, “There are local volunteers - they are really struggling, risking their lives every day to try and provide a little bit of food for people who are mostly starving.”nnThe situation is compounded by dwindling international aid, with Vu noting, “The funding is completely decreasing and the consequence is that you can see it on the ground.” Residents are relying on the solidarity of their neighbors for survival, sharing dwindling resources.  “If they have a little bit of food, they will be sharing it among themselves,” says Vu, underscoring the profound desperation and resilience of the people of el-Fasher as they call for urgent international assistance.
