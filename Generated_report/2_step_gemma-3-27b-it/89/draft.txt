South Africa’s President Cyril Ramaphosa has suspended Police Minister Senzo Mchunu, placing him on immediate leave of absence amid serious allegations of involvement with organized crime groups. The move comes after KwaZulu-Natal provincial police boss Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi publicly accused Mchunu of interference in investigations related to political killings and corruption within law enforcement agencies, prompting growing public pressure on Ramaphosa to act swiftly. The allegations center around claims that Mchunu obstructed investigations into politically motivated killings, particularly in KwaZulu-Natal province, and accepted financial support from individuals allegedly involved in criminal activities.nnIn a televised address to the nation on Sunday, President Ramaphosa announced the suspension and explained that the accusations “undermine the constitution and threaten national security.” He stated, “In Sunday’s live televised speech to the nation, Ramaphosa also announced a judicial commission would investigate the claims, which he said undermined the constitution and threatened national security.”  To ensure an impartial investigation, Ramaphosa has established a judicial commission, led by the country’s deputy chief justice, to thoroughly examine all claims against Mchunu.nnLaw professor Firoz Cachalia has been appointed as interim police minister while the investigation unfolds, the President confirmed. “He added that law professor Firoz Cachalia had been appointed as interim police minister.” While Mchunu denies any wrongdoing, stating he “stood ready to respond to the accusations,” the allegations leveled against him are substantial.  nnGeneral Mkhwanazi alleges that Mchunu received financial backing from businessman Vusimuzi Matlala, who had a lucrative contract with the police department before his arrest for attempted murder in May. Further, Mkhwanazi claims Mchunu ordered the disbandment of a task force investigating political killings in 2018 and the removal of 121 case files from the unit, halting potential arrests. “According to Gen Mkhwanazi, a total of 121 case files were allegedly removed from the unit on the minister's instruction and without the authorisation of his boss, the national police commissioner Gen Fannie Masemola.”nnRamaphosa emphasized the gravity of the situation, noting the commission will investigate not only Mchunu but also current and former police officials, as well as members of the national executive.  Mchunu, in a statement, has pledged his full cooperation with the investigation, stating, “I welcome and respect the president’s decision and pledge my commitment to the process.” He added that “Honour and integrity are the virtues I personally subscribe to and which we all need to make efforts to uphold.” The outcome of the investigation could have significant implications for South Africa’s ongoing fight against crime and corruption.
