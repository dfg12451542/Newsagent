Swansea made history on Sunday, July 13th, 2025, as it hosted the UK’s first ever edition of the IRONMAN Pro Series. Despite soaring temperatures – exceeding 28°C in the air and 24°C in the water, prompting a non-wetsuit swim for professional athletes – the event drew thousands of competitors and enthusiastic supporters to the Welsh coast. The challenging conditions didn’t deter Harry Palmer, who secured his second consecutive victory in Swansea, finishing with a time of 3:51:18 and claiming the overall Pro Series win, narrowly beating out training partner Leon Chevalier by just 49 seconds.nnPalmer acknowledged the heat as a significant factor throughout the race. “It was hot, and I do feel for all the age-groupers who are out there today, because they’ve got much harder conditions than we did, so fair play to everyone racing,” he told BBC Sport Wales. He also described a challenging swim, stating, “I didn’t have the best swim, I’ll be honest. I was in the second or third pack back, so I had some work to do on the bike.”  The athlete found himself amongst strong competitors early on. “I came out [of the swim] with the likes of Will Crudgington…Kristian Høgenhaug, who was one of the guys I was keeping an eye on. He’s an epic rider.”nnOn the women’s side, Kat Matthews, the 2024 Ironman Pro Series champion, triumphed with a time of 4:20:37, marking a significant moment after a three-year hiatus since her last victory in Swansea. “Three years feels like a lifetime. A lot has happened since then,” Matthews reflected. “I feel like a completely different person, and it felt like a different event this time around.” Matthews strategically attacked on the bike, quickly taking the lead despite the arduous conditions. “I was happy to see them quite quickly and pull them back, but then suddenly I was on my own at the front. It was quite nice to feel in control until the run, when I felt a bit hot.”nnThe event was praised by both athletes and organizers for its enthusiastic atmosphere and supportive community. “My favourite part is the support,” Palmer emphasized, adding, “The course is amazing, especially with weather like this, and the Gower is incredible… But it’s the people—it’s the Welsh. They just know how to put on an event and how to support.” Race Director Rebecca Sutherland echoed this sentiment, stating, “It’s always great fun when we go racing in Swansea, and this year was no different. It was another incredible race with thousands of athletes crossing the famous red carpet finish line.”nnWith over half of the age-group participants completing their first IRONMAN 70.3, the Swansea event proved to be a memorable experience for all involved. Organizers have already confirmed the race’s return to the city next summer for its fourth edition, solidifying Swansea’s place as a key location on the IRONMAN Pro Series calendar.
