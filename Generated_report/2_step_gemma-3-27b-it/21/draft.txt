Businesses in the Channel Islands are calling for increased collaboration between Jersey and Guernsey to address shared economic challenges, according to a new survey released by the Institute of Directors (IoD) Jersey. The findings, gathered from 70 members, reveal a significant disparity between optimism regarding individual businesses and the overall economic outlook for Jersey.nnThe IoD Jersey’s second economic confidence survey shows that while 56% of respondents expressed optimism about their own organizations, only 17% felt positive about Jersey’s economy as a whole. Key priorities highlighted for fostering economic growth included substantial investment in infrastructure, proactively attracting new industries to the island, and tackling the ongoing issue of housing affordability. These concerns echo similar findings from a Guernsey IOD survey published in late June, demonstrating a shared struggle across both islands.nnThis call for unified action comes as both Jersey and Guernsey navigate persistent economic headwinds. The disparity in confidence levels signals a need for strategic intervention from political leaders, according to the report.  Leaders are being urged to prioritise joint strategies for dealing with similar issues faced by both islands. nnAlex Ruddy, IoD Jersey Chair, emphasized the clear mandate for action stemming from the survey results. “While our members are confident in their own organisations, they are calling for bold, strategic investment and policy clarity to restore wider economic confidence,” Ruddy stated.  He further stressed that “Collaboration across the Channel Islands, support for innovation, and a focus on infrastructure and housing are essential to Jersey’s future prosperity.”nnRichard Hemans, IoD Guernsey's lead on economics, mirrored this sentiment, stating, “There is clear recognition from the business community in both islands that – given the same challenges apply to both islands and our economic, geographical, demographic, constitutional and fiscal profile is so similar – the best way to confront our mutual challenges is to work together more closely.”  Jersey respondents specifically identified a desire to collaborate with Guernsey on fiscal deficit strategies, including reducing public spending and stimulating economic growth. The survey also pointed to potential avenues for supporting entrepreneurship and innovation through tax incentives, attracting youth talent, and fostering skills development, while noting AI adoption remains in its early phases, with most companies providing training or developing implementation strategies.
