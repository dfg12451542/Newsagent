A quick-thinking Guernsey woman saved her husband’s life after he collapsed in the sea during a swim at Port Grat on Friday afternoon. Jacky Meinke, 67, spotted her husband, Chris, 77, floating face down in the water and immediately sprang into action, pulling him to safety with the assistance of fellow swimmer Rosie Mclauchlan. The incident highlights the potential dangers of open water swimming, even for experienced swimmers.nnMrs. Meinke initially believed her husband was swimming behind her. “I heard a splash and carried on swimming, thinking he was behind me,” she recounted. However, when she turned around, she was met with a terrifying sight. “When I looked round his head was face down in the water,” she said, describing the moment she realized something was wrong.  She quickly grabbed his head and began efforts to revive him, reporting, “I started screaming his name and grabbed his head out of the water, he was completely white.”nnWorking swiftly, Mrs. Meinke managed to get Mr. Meinke to cough up water. “I put his head on my shoulder, walloped his back and he spluttered and came to,” she explained.  Ms. Mclauchlan witnessed the harrowing scene, stating, “He was literally drowning.”  She added, “I was amazed how quickly someone can get into difficulty.” Both women then successfully brought Mr. Meinke back to the beach.nnMr. Meinke was taken to the hospital and spent the night under observation. While the precise cause of his collapse remains unknown, doctors suspect vertigo. “I was effectively dead,” he stated, acknowledging the severity of the situation. “The hospital thinks I had vertigo, I just didn't know which way was up. It wasn't a stroke, I just collapsed. It's never happened before.” Mr. Meinke is now awaiting scan results to determine the underlying cause. nnBoth Mr. and Mrs. Meinke expressed immense gratitude to Ms. Mclauchlan and the passersby who assisted at the scene, including those who retrieved a defibrillator and provided a blanket. “I am so grateful to them all,” said Mrs. Meinke. “It was very scary and I’m not a panicky person, but I’m just relieved he’s OK.” The incident serves as a stark reminder of the importance of water safety and the life-saving potential of quick thinking and bystander intervention.
