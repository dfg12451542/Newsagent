ArtHouse Jersey has announced the latest recipients of its development grants, providing crucial financial support to a diverse group of local artists. The charity’s initiative, offering quarterly funding, aims to bolster the island’s creative landscape by assisting both emerging and established artists in realizing their projects and building sustainable careers within the arts.nnAmong those awarded grants is actor and writer Jack Evans-Rentsch, who is developing a new comedy centered around the unique political dynamics of remote island life. Evans-Rentsch shared plans to film the short, produced by Tiny Speck Productions, on location in Jersey, utilizing a local cast and crew. This commitment to local talent echoes ArtHouse Jersey’s broader mission of fostering a thriving artistic community.nnTiny Speck Productions’ project is just one of several benefiting from the latest round of funding. FlashMob band member Henry Matson announced the band had received support for their second EP, slated for recording later this year. He emphasized the invaluable role ArtHouse Jersey plays, stating that the organization provides opportunities grassroots artists would otherwise lack. nnThe grants aren't limited to film and music; they are also enabling artists to expand their skillset. Hanni El Dardiry Osman plans to use the funding for vital chainsaw skills training, a necessary step in his craft of creating guitars from timber. This demonstrates the breadth of ArtHouse Jersey’s support, extending beyond project funding to encompass professional development.nnFilmmaker Sean Frost is also set to benefit significantly, receiving a grant to produce his immersive cinematic short film, “Memoriam.” As Mr Matson said: “The support that ArtHouse Jersey provides to local artists is invaluable and ensures that grassroots artists are provided with opportunities they would not otherwise have.” Mr Frost explained that the grant will cover a “majority of the budget” for the 8-10 minute film, scheduled to be shot in Jersey this autumn.
