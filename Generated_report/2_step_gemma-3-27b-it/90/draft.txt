A fire at a Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA) club in west Belfast has been deemed a deliberate act of vandalism, according to the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI). The incident, which caused extensive damage to club facilities, occurred on Saturday evening around 18:20 BST. Authorities are currently appealing for information from the public to aid their investigation.nnInvestigators quickly determined the blaze was not accidental. The destruction has prompted widespread condemnation from local representatives and community members.  “The fire is believed to have been started at around 18:20 BST on Saturday,” stated PSNI Insp Dalton. The club, a vital part of the local community, was preparing for its summer programs when the attack occurred, adding to the dismay.nnAccording to Insp Dalton, the fire caused “extensive damage.”  Belfast city councillor Arder Carson described the incident as a “blatant act of vandalism,” expressing the deep frustration felt by many.  “It definitely fills you with despair and some anger that a club that does so much for the local community in terms of working diligently to provide facilities and services that give young people hope and opportunity has been subject to such an attack,” Carson said.nnThe timing of the attack is particularly devastating, as the club was “in the heart” of preparing for its children’s summer camps and the start of a new championship season.  The reckless nature of setting a fire during a period of dry weather was also highlighted by Carson. “Setting fire outdoors at any time is reckless but this level is just beyond reckless,” she stated, adding, “On the period of dry weather we have had, to set fires in an area like that which is rural and totally green is just beyond reckless.”nn“It’s absolutely unacceptable that a community organisation should be targeted in this way,” Insp Dalton added. Police are urging anyone with information regarding the incident to come forward and assist with the ongoing investigation.
