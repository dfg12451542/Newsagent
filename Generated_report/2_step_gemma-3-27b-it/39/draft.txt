Royal Portrush, Northern Ireland – Rory McIlroy has stated that securing a victory at The Open Championship 2025, held at Royal Portrush, would resonate with him as deeply as his momentous Masters win earlier this year. The Northern Irish golfer anticipates an emotionally charged experience should he triumph on his home soil. nnMcIlroy, 36, is returning to the course where he captivated the nation in 2019, a tournament where he ultimately fell short of contention. Acknowledging that experience, he feels he has matured as a player since then. “I’m so happy it’s back so soon because I want to have another crack at it,” McIlroy told BBC Sport, expressing his eagerness to redeem himself. He believes his improved game management and resilience will prevent a repeat of his opening-round 79 from six years ago.nn“It would be amazing,” McIlroy shared with BBC Sport NI, outlining the significance of a potential Portrush win. He further stated, “I think it would be just as emotional, if not more emotional than Augusta – and everyone saw the mess I was after that,” referring to the overwhelming emotion he displayed upon finally completing a career Grand Slam at the Masters. nnThis year’s Open holds particular weight for McIlroy, who admits the tournament was even more prominently marked on his calendar than the Masters. He feels a deeper connection to the event, fueled by the unwavering support of his homeland. “It would be absolutely incredible. I’m so grateful and appreciative of the support I get from home and they really make me feel that out there,” he said, emphasizing his desire to embrace the atmosphere and deliver a memorable performance for the fans. nnMcIlroy intends to fully engage with the enthusiastic crowds, a shift in approach from his previous attempt at Portrush. “I think I probably tried to isolate, and I think it’s better for everyone if I embrace it,” he explained, recognizing the positive impact of connecting with the fans. The 2014 Open Champion feels encouraged by his recent form, particularly his performances in Scotland, and is prepared to contend for the Claret Jug.
