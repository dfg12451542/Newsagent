Great Britain’s Oliver Rowland has been crowned the Formula E champion following a tense race in Berlin, Germany, securing the title with two races remaining in the season. The Nissan driver finished fourth in Sunday’s race at the Tempelhof Airport, enough to mathematically clinch the championship and become the 10th different champion in the electric series’ 11-year history.nnRowland’s victory comes as Porsche’s Pascal Wehrlein, his closest competitor, endured a difficult race, dropping from pole position to 16th place. This result significantly widened the gap in the championship standings, with Rowland amassing 184 points to Wehrlein’s 125. The championship battle had been closely contested throughout the season, but Rowland's consistent performance proved decisive. nn“Honestly, I was just thinking before the race not to have too much damage going into [the final round in] London,” Rowland stated, reflecting on his strategy heading into the German race. While aiming for a cautious approach, the race proved anything but, as Rowland navigated a fiercely competitive field.nnDespite his initial intention to play it safe, Rowland found himself battling intensely with rivals. “I can’t believe it, I have no words. It’s incredible. I was trying to play it safe but everybody was so aggressive, so at some point I was all in,” he explained, detailing the challenging dynamics of the race. Ultimately, his ability to avoid collisions and maintain a solid position proved crucial to securing the title. nnRowland’s victory was further highlighted by his relief at navigating a chaotic race. “Thankfully I stayed out of trouble,” he added, emphasizing the unpredictable nature of Formula E racing. Elsewhere on the podium, Jaguar’s Nick Cassidy claimed first place, followed by Jake Dennis of Andretti and Jean-Eric Vergne of DS Penske, but the day unequivocally belonged to Oliver Rowland and Great Britain.
