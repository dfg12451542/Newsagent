Liverpool played their first match since the tragic death of forward Diogo Jota, a pre-season friendly against Preston North End, on Saturday, July 13th. The match, ultimately won 3-1 by Liverpool, was less a sporting contest and more a poignant tribute to the 30-year-old Portuguese international and his brother, Andre Silva, who also recently passed away. The atmosphere at Deepdale was charged with emotion as thousands of Liverpool fans made the journey, not solely to witness the team’s first run-out of the new campaign, but to collectively mourn and honor Jota's memory.nnA visibly emotional scene unfolded before kickoff as Preston captain Ben Whiteman laid a wreath in front of the travelling Liverpool supporters. The away end was a sea of flags and scarves dedicated to Jota, and fans passionately sang his song as the players walked onto the pitch following a flawlessly observed minute's silence. Throughout the match, spontaneous renditions of Jota’s song echoed around the stadium, with even Preston supporters joining in, applauding and clapping in unison, particularly during the 20th minute – a clear nod to Jota’s iconic shirt number.nnLiverpool manager Arne Slot, in his first interview since the devastating news, emphasized the importance of allowing players to grieve authentically. “If we want to laugh, we laugh; if we want to cry, we’re going to cry,” Slot stated. He added, “If they want to train they can train, if they don’t want to train they can not train. But be yourself, don’t think you have to be different than your emotions tell you.” This sentiment resonated deeply with fans, many of whom expressed their own grief and pride in the team's response.nnThe players themselves found ways to honor Jota on the pitch. Conor Bradley, after scoring the opening goal, looked towards the heavens. Darwin Nunez celebrated his goal by replicating two of Jota’s signature moves, and Cody Gakpo followed suit, holding up two fingers to represent Jota’s squad number.  “We will always carry him with us in our hearts, in our thoughts, wherever we go,” Slot affirmed.  Fans like Jane, wearing a custom T-shirt in Jota's memory, expressed feeling both “very emotional” and “very proud” of the tributes.nnLiverpool have officially retired Jota’s number 20 shirt across all areas of the club, a lasting testament to his impact. While the scoreline was secondary, the match served as a powerful demonstration of the unity and love within the Liverpool family, a community profoundly affected by the loss of a beloved player.  As Mathew, a supporter whose seven-year-old son was deeply upset by the news, poignantly stated, “It’s very hard for him to understand,” highlighting the far-reaching impact of Jota's passing.
