Rory McIlroy expressed contentment despite finishing just short of victory at the Scottish Open, signaling he’s well-prepared as he heads to Royal Portrush for this week’s major championship. McIlroy concluded the tournament at 13-under par, fueled by an impressive 19 birdies over four rounds, but ultimately fell short of claiming the title. While a win was the goal, the Northern Irishman remained remarkably upbeat about his performance and readiness for the challenges ahead.nnMcIlroy emphasized a positive outlook, stating, “No frustration; it’s been a great week.” He focused on the strengths displayed during the tournament, particularly his weekend performance and ability to control his ball flight.  The player acknowledged the narrow margin of defeat but chose to highlight the quality of his game, saying, “I’m really happy with where my game is; the way I played over the weekend; the shots that I hit, how I controlled my ball flight. Missing the trophy, that’s about it.”nnThe final round presented considerable challenges due to windy conditions, making precise approach shots difficult.  “It was pretty windy and was hard to get the ball super close, but I gave myself plenty of chances coming down the stretch,” McIlroy noted.  He also pointed to issues with the putting surfaces as a factor, acknowledging some misreads and difficulties with speed on the greens.  “I feel like the greens got a little bumpy. I had some good putts that just didn’t go in, whether they were mis-reads or poor speed.”nnDespite these minor setbacks, McIlroy appears confident heading into the major at Royal Portrush. He expressed satisfaction with the overall benefits of the Scottish Open, stating, “But I feel like I’ve gotten out of this week what I wanted.” He plans to quickly transition his focus to preparation for the tournament, adding, “I’m looking forward to getting to Portrush tonight and getting out on to the golf course early tomorrow.”nnSpectators heading to Royal Portrush will benefit from a re-admission policy, allowing them to leave the grounds, explore the nearby town centre, and return for continued enjoyment of the championship.
