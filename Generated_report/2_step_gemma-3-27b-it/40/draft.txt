Veteran boxing trainer Dave Coldwell has voiced his disapproval of Manny Pacquiao’s scheduled comeback fight this Saturday, stating he “won’t be watching.” Coldwell, known for his work with multiple championship contenders, believes Pacquiao’s direct return to a high-profile bout circumvents the standard protocols for fighters returning from retirement and diminishes the efforts of those diligently climbing the ranks. “It feels like a shortcut,” Coldwell explained, “A nod to nostalgia rather than merit, and that's unfair to fighters who are working their way up properly.”nnColdwell was quick to acknowledge Pacquiao’s legendary status within the sport. “I realise that might sound harsh given he is one of the sport's all-time greats, an eight-division world champion. I have immense respect for what he achieved in his prime. He’s a global icon, a true legend,” he acknowledged. However, his concerns remain focused on the precedent the fight sets within the boxing world.nnBoxing tradition typically sees returning fighters ease back into competition with a series of tune-up bouts, allowing them to assess their skills and conditioning after a period of inactivity. Pacquiao, however, is bypassing this process entirely, jumping straight into a significant fight. As Coldwell noted, this is a considerable departure from the norm and amplifies the issue of veteran boxers re-entering the sport without the typical build-up.nn“That’s the part that really shocked me when I heard of Pacquiao’s return,” Coldwell added. “Usually, a fighter coming back from retirement eases into the sport with tune-up fights. Not Pacquiao. He’s skipping all that and going right to the front of the queue.” This decision, Coldwell fears, undermines the dedication and hard work of boxers who are steadily building their careers through tough fights and consistent progression.nnColdwell also revealed a disagreement with WBC President Mauricio Sulaiman regarding his concerns. While Sulaiman reportedly expressed disagreement with Coldwell’s skepticism, the trainer remains steadfast in his belief that Pacquiao’s immediate return “sets a strange precedent and undermines the fighters who have been grinding their way up the ranks, paying their dues with tough fights.” The situation raises broader questions about the management of older boxers returning to the ring, a concern highlighted by recent industry analysis.
