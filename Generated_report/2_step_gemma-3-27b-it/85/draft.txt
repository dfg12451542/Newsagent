Nuseirat, Gaza – A father in Gaza is expressing outrage and grief after an Israeli strike killed his son, Abdullah, and nine others while they were waiting in line for water in the Nuseirat refugee camp on Sunday. Mahmoud Abdul Rahman Ahmed recounted how Abdullah was simply “searching for a sip” when he headed with his family’s jerrycans to a distribution point, a common and desperate necessity for residents enduring the ongoing conflict. Graphic footage verified by the BBC depicted the chaotic aftermath of the strike, showing debris, injured civilians, and motionless bodies scattered amongst dozens of yellow jerrycans.nnAccording to Ahmed, the area was filled with displaced people and those exhausted by the war and years of siege. “The children, Abdullah among them, stood in a queue with empty stomachs, empty jerrycans, and thirsty lips,” he stated. Moments after the group gathered, the area was struck, an event Ahmed believes was a deliberate message from Israel. “Minutes after the children and thirsty people of the camp gathered, the warplanes bombed those children and the water distribution point, without prior notice,” he said, adding that the strike aimed to deny even basic needs like drinking water to the population.nnThe Al-Awda hospital in Nuseirat reported that ten people, including six children – Abdullah, Badr al-Din Qaraman, Siraj Khaled Ibrahim, Ibrahim Ashraf Abu Urayban, Karam Ashraf al-Ghussein and Lana Ashraf al-Ghussein – were killed, with another sixteen injured. The Israeli military acknowledged the strike, claiming it targeted a Palestinian Islamic Jihad “terrorist” but attributing the civilian deaths to a “technical error” with the munition, which fell “dozens of meters from the target.” They expressed regret for any harm to uninvolved civilians and stated the incident is under review.nnThe tragedy echoes a similar incident just last week in the nearby town of Deir al-Balah, where ten children and three women were killed while waiting for nutritional supplements. Sam Rose, the acting Gaza director for UNRWA, highlighted the daily struggle for essential resources, stating, “You see children queuing up, by the side of the road, with yellow jerrycans every single morning…waiting for the daily water truck.” He lamented the loss, adding, “Every death is a tragedy. This one is particularly emblematic…But it’s one of many,” noting that, on average, a classroom full of children are killed in Gaza each day since the onset of the war. Catherine Russell, the executive director of UNICEF, called both incidents “horrific” and urged Israel to review its rules of engagement to ensure compliance with international humanitarian law.
