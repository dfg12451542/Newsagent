Cirencester History Festival is seeking recollections of its former railway station, set to be temporarily reopened during the festival this October. The event, running from October 25th to November 1st, will feature talks and activities on local and global history, culminating in a celebration of the station’s “golden age of rail.” The Grade-II listed Old Station, designed by Isambard Kingdom Brunel, has been closed since 1965, but will offer public tours during the festival’s two Saturdays. nnFestival producer Jess Yarrow hopes the event will unearth forgotten memories of the station. “We know that there are lots of people out there who can remember the station in its heyday,” she stated, appealing for stories, photographs and memorabilia. Yarrow added that she hopes people will share recollections of using the station for commutes, or even experiences like witnessing its role in welcoming evacuees and soldiers during World War Two. Currently, many perceive the Old Station as “a slightly sad, empty-looking building,” and the festival aims to breathe life into its past.nnBeyond personal recollections, the festival will showcase the station’s historical significance. Architect Jonathan Rixon, currently refurbishing the station for use as his offices, will lead the tours, highlighting hidden treasures like the boardroom where Brunel himself once sought a meeting space outside of London. Rixon emphasized the importance of preserving the town’s industrial heritage, stating, “It’s important to protect our industrial heritage. I’m passionate about preserving this piece of history and sharing it with the town.”nnThe Old Station’s history extends beyond its days as a bustling railway hub. After its closure in 1965, the building served as a hospital and, during World War II, a prisoner of war camp housing German and Italian soldiers. The festival will incorporate an audio listening project showcasing stories about the station, and Cirencester College students have collaborated with Rixon to create a virtual reality experience, transporting visitors back to the station during the Victorian era. nnYarrow is specifically requesting items that can help recreate the station's atmosphere. “We’d love to see photographs, we’d love to have any memorabilia that people have got tucked away because we’ll be dressing the station as if it were a station with prams and benches and cases, so anything like that people have, we’d love to know about it.” Anyone with relevant memories or artifacts is encouraged to come forward and contribute to this celebration of Cirencester’s railway past.
