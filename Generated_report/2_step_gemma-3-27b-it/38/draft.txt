A memorial bench dedicated to Sophie Lancaster, a 20-year-old woman tragically murdered in 2007 due to her affiliation with the Goth subculture, has been temporarily removed from its location on Whitby’s West Cliff to make way for a large Ferris wheel. The move, intended to boost tourism, has sparked outrage from the Sophie Lancaster Foundation and local residents who feel the decision was made without proper consultation or respect for the memorial’s significance.nnThe Sophie Lancaster Foundation expressed their shock at the relocation. A spokesperson for the charity stated they were “shocked” to find the bench had been moved and that the organisation had not been consulted. For Sylvia Lancaster, Sophie’s mother, the bench represents a vital, tangible connection to her daughter. “For Sylvia, Sophie’s mother, the bench was a tangible connection to Sophie,” a charity spokesperson explained, adding “It was a shock to learn that the bench has been removed to locate a giant sightseeing wheel. Unfortunately, we weren’t informed or consulted about this decision.”nnNorth Yorkshire Council defended the move, claiming the big wheel was “intended as a respectful, exciting addition to the town” and that the memorial would be restored to its original location by August 4th. Councillor Mark Crane further emphasized that the wheel was part of a wider tourism strategy designed to showcase Whitby’s attractions, including its famous abbey. He also noted initial positive feedback from local businesses, anticipating increased foot traffic.nnHowever, the lack of communication has drawn criticism from local officials. Whitby mayor, Councillor Sandra Turner, described the situation as “a serious oversight” and acknowledged a “serious hiccup” in the relationship between the local authorities and the council. Despite assurances from the council that such an incident will not occur again, the decision has highlighted the importance of community engagement in local development projects. The Sophie Lancaster Foundation is planning a rededication ceremony at Tomorrow’s Ghosts Festival on November 1st, expressing their joy at the bench's eventual return, and praising the outpouring of support from the alternative community.
