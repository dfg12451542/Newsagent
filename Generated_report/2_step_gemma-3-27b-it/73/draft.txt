Silverstone witnessed a unique spectacle during last year’s British Grand Prix – a fully functional pit stop performed by the cast of the upcoming F1 movie. Actress Callie Cooke, one of the film’s stars, revealed the extraordinary experience, describing it as “mad” that they were permitted to participate in a live race event.  The film, directed by Joseph Kosinski and starring Brad Pitt, aims for authenticity in its portrayal of the high-octane world of Formula 1, and Cooke’s experience exemplifies that commitment.nnCooke immersed herself in the reality of a Formula 1 garage to prepare for her role.  “I spent the day there and I was changing the tyres and I was changing the oil, changing the wing of the car, and just seeing if I could exist in the world of a garage,” Cooke explained.  Growing up in Northampton, near Silverstone, provided a foundational understanding of the motorsport world, as many of her acquaintances worked with cars.  “I think it did kind of help growing up in Northampton and not far from Silverstone because I do know a lot of people that work with cars, and I just got stuck in that garage.”  nnThe culmination of this training led to the astounding opportunity to participate in a genuine pit stop, positioned between the Mercedes and Ferrari teams.  “We did a real pit stop in between Mercedes and Ferrari, and we did it as quick as the other teams,” Cooke stated. She emphasized the special connection she feels to the Silverstone location, noting, “Because of the Northamptonshire-Silverstone connection, it feels so special and so real, it’s just been the coolest thing.”  Brad Pitt, also enjoyed the Northamptonshire and Silverstone environment, and Cooke recalled, “Pitt loved working in Northamptonshire and Silverstone. It was our home for at least a year. We were pretty much living there.”nnCooke also reflected on the demands of portraying a woman in a traditionally male-dominated industry. “I knew that if I was going to play a woman in this male-dominated industry, I was just going to have to hold my own and get stuck in, and I think maybe that’s how I got the job.” The experience proved to be incredibly rewarding, described by Cooke as “one of the best experiences of my life.” She was the only actor in the pit crew with the rest of the team comprised of mechanics, engineers or stunt performers.nnWorking alongside actual F1 teams and at real Grand Prix locations created a “once-in-a-lifetime experience” for the cast and crew, resulting in a level of realism unseen in previous motorsport films.  Cooke expressed her disbelief that they were “let us get away with it, to be honest,” highlighting the unprecedented access granted and the extraordinary immersion in the world of Formula 1.
