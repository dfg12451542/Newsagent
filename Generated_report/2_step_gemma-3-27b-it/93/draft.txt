This week in Tyne and Wear, a permanent memorial to the felled Sycamore Gap tree was unveiled alongside ongoing disruptions to the new Tyne and Wear Metro service. The region also celebrated the upcoming honorary degree to be awarded to historian David Olusoga by Newcastle University. These stories highlight the blend of remembrance, practical challenges, and academic recognition shaping the week on Tyneside.nnThe Sycamore Gap tree, tragically felled in an act of vandalism two years ago, is now represented by a section of its trunk permanently displayed at the Sill National Landscape Discovery Centre in Northumberland. Visitors will be able to physically connect with a piece of the iconic landmark. Artist Charlie Winney, attending the unveiling, described the memorial as being “blown away by how huggable it is,” emphasizing the emotional impact of preserving a tangible link to the beloved tree.nnHowever, transport challenges continue for Metro commuters. Three of the newly introduced Tyne and Wear Metro trains were temporarily removed from service due to ongoing issues with air-conditioning leaks, with water pouring into the carriages. This represents a recurring problem, forcing operators to troubleshoot and correct the fault, causing inconvenience to passengers.nnBeyond local landmarks and transport, Newcastle University will bestow an Honorary Doctor of Civil Law upon David Olusoga, a social historian with roots in Gateshead. The recognition celebrates Olusoga’s significant contributions to public understanding of British history and his dedication to uncovering previously untold narratives. This summer ceremony will honour his work and its impact on the region and beyond.
