A meticulously restored steam locomotive, affectionately known as “Newstead,” began a new chapter in its history this weekend, entering service with the North Norfolk Railway in Sheringham. The 1929 engine officially re-entered service on Saturday, marking the culmination of a nine-year refurbishment project and drawing enthusiastic crowds to witness the historic moment. The locomotive will now pull passenger carriages between Sheringham and Holt, adding a touch of vintage charm to the popular railway line.nnThe story of Newstead is remarkable, steeped in unexpected twists. Originally discovered abandoned at a Carmelite convent in Hertfordshire in 2016, the locomotive's presence there was something of a mystery. Alex Alder, intimately involved in the engine’s restoration, described the initial discovery as akin to “hearing of a mythical Egyptian tomb.” He recounted, “I fully never expected to be driven down a Hertfordshire country lane, following a wild tip off about a lost engine.” The unexpected location—and subsequent conversations, including “taking tea and biscuits with [a] mother superior…and hearing of ‘Malcolm's engine’” —highlight the unusual journey the locomotive took before its eventual rescue.nnAfter its discovery, Newstead underwent an extensive restoration, costing approximately £200,000 and requiring the dedicated work of a team of volunteers over nine years. “After nine years of efforts, for the very first time ever she pulled passenger trains. It was absolutely incredible,” said Alex Alder, reflecting on the successful debut run. The project represents a significant investment of time, resources, and passion to preserve a piece of railway heritage.nnThe locomotive’s arrival has been met with widespread excitement within the North Norfolk Railway community. Graham Hukins expressed his delight, stating it was “absolutely wonderful” to see the engine running in Sheringham. He further noted, “This weekend is the very first time it has been paired with passenger carriages, and it is an absolute delight to have it join our working fleet,” adding that being part of the engine’s restoration and witnessing its return to working order was especially significant as the railway celebrated 50 years of operation. Newstead promises to be a treasured addition to the railway’s collection, offering a unique experience for passengers and a testament to the dedication of those who brought it back to life.
