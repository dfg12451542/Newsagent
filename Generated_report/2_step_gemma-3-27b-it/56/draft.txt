An ultramarathon runner was airlifted to safety after collapsing from heat exhaustion during a grueling 55-mile mountain race in the Lake District on Saturday. The incident underscores the dangers faced by endurance athletes during the current heatwave sweeping across parts of the UK. Rescuers responded to a call in Grisedale Valley around 16:00 BST after the runner displayed symptoms including paleness, vomiting, and loss of consciousness.nnPatterdale Mountain Rescue Team (PMRT) were swiftly mobilized, coordinating with the North West Ambulance Service and the coastguard. A coastguard helicopter was dispatched to winch the runner to safety, transporting him to the King George playing field in Patterdale.  PMRT reported the runner was then assessed by a team doctor and subsequently transferred to a hospital for further medical evaluation.nnThe rescue operation, lasting two and a half hours, involved 11 dedicated PMRT members alongside the coastguard crew. This incident is part of a wider pattern of event cancellations due to the extreme heat; several running events, including ultramarathons and 10K races in nearby Snowdonia (Eryri), have been called off this weekend as a safety precaution.nnRecent days have been officially classified as a heatwave, raising concerns for participants in strenuous outdoor activities. While the runner is now receiving hospital care, this event serves as a stark reminder of the risks associated with prolonged physical exertion in high temperatures, echoing concerns highlighted following the tragic death of a runner during a parkrun event this summer.
