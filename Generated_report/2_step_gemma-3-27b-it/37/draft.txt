Wearside residents have been grappling with legal battles and seeking justice this week, as stories of hardship and artistic integrity came to light. A local sculptor and a victim of the Post Office Horizon IT scandal are both navigating challenging circumstances, highlighting ongoing issues within the legal and justice systems.nnRay Lonsdale, a sculptor from South Hetton, recently raised thousands of pounds in preparation for a legal challenge against New Zealand sculptor James Wright, who he alleges stole his work. Lonsdale is fighting to protect his livelihood, asserting that his artwork was independently researched and there had been “no copyright infringement,” according to Mr. Wright. This dispute underscores the difficulties artists face in protecting their intellectual property and the financial strain of pursuing legal action.nnMeanwhile, Betty Brown shared her harrowing experience connected to the Post Office Horizon IT scandal, coinciding with the publication of the first official inquiry report. Ms. Brown stated that the scandal “destroyed” her health, echoing the findings of the inquiry which described the impact on wrongly accused individuals as “disastrous.” The inquiry report details the devastating consequences faced by subpostmasters and subpostmistresses wrongly prosecuted based on faulty accounting data. nnThese cases unfold against the backdrop of a wider national reckoning with the Post Office Horizon scandal, which saw hundreds of post office operators falsely accused of theft, fraud and false accounting. The scandal has led to overturned convictions and growing calls for accountability within the Post Office and Fujitsu, the company responsible for the faulty Horizon system. The inquiry’s initial report reinforces the widespread injustice experienced by victims like Ms. Brown.nnElsewhere in Wearside news, Durham University hosted its graduation ceremony at Durham Cathedral, with actor Mark Gatiss addressing the graduating students while adorned in ceremonial robes.
