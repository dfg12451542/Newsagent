A petition to restore the 128-year-old Victoria Marine Lake in St Helier, Jersey, has surpassed 1,000 signatures, signaling growing public concern over the future of the historic sea pool. The surge in support indicates a strong desire to preserve a unique element of the island’s coastline and has prompted hopes for a formal response from the Jersey government. nnThe level of interest in the petition demonstrates substantial public engagement with the issue. Campaigners believe this milestone will compel authorities to address the deteriorating condition of the pool and consider viable restoration options. A dedicated group, the newly-formed Save Our Pool Working Group, is leading the charge, advocating for urgent action to prevent further decline. nnPetition organiser Nina Zaech emphasized the cultural and economic significance of the Victoria Marine Lake, stating that it is a “vital piece of Jersey’s heritage” and can contribute to health, leisure, and tourism. This sentiment highlights the pool’s importance beyond its recreational value, positioning it as a key component of the island’s identity.  nnNotably, Victoria Marine Lake predates the more well-known and listed Havre des Pas pool by eight years, yet it currently lacks protected status, leaving it vulnerable. A 2024 inspection confirmed the need for “comprehensive work” to bring the pool back to its former glory. nnHowever, the path to restoration isn't straightforward. Last month, Infrastructure Minister Andy Jehan expressed doubts about the economic feasibility of repairs, citing planned sea defence upgrades along the waterfront. He suggested that repairs would be “uneconomical” in light of these upcoming projects, creating a considerable hurdle for those seeking to save the historic pool.
