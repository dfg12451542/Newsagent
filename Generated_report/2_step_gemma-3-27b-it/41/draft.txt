Ilkley residents and local businesses are voicing strong opposition to proposed parking changes, with some critics labeling the plans as akin to those of “Ebenezer Scrooge.” Bradford Council is considering standardizing parking charges across the district, a move that would introduce fees in Ilkley where some areas currently offer free parking. A petition garnering over 1,500 signatures was presented to the council highlighting concerns about the detrimental impact on the town's vitality.nnLocal resident Richard Downing, who presented the petition, warned the changes would be “immensely damaging” to Ilkley’s businesses. “It helps people shop locally…Having to pay to visit shops for a few items will make it inconvenient to shop locally – footfall will be down and people will be drawn to retail parks,” he argued. He urged the council to scrap the proposed changes, emphasizing the potential decline in visits to small businesses and evening venues like theaters.nnIlkley Conservative councillor Andrew Loy echoed these concerns, stating the charges would disproportionately affect vulnerable groups. “The charges will unfairly impact certain groups of people…the elderly, less mobile, parents dropping off children at school and parishioners of churches,” he explained, going on to question the fairness of potentially receiving a parking fine while attending church on Christmas Day. Loy bluntly stated the council’s policy felt like it was “written by Ebenezer Scrooge.”nnWhile councillors acknowledged the widespread opposition, they defended the move as a necessary step towards budgetary management. Councillor Alex Ross Shaw stated, “We’re looking at trying to find ways of managing the budget. Standardising parking charges is one way of doing this.” Shaw also pushed back against claims that Ilkley was being unfairly targeted, asserting changes were being implemented “in similar ways right across the district.”nnThe petition has been forwarded to the council's executive for a final decision. The proposals come as Bradford also considers increasing parking charges in the city centre, including evening fees. The debate underscores a broader tension between the need for local government funding and the desire to support thriving local economies.
