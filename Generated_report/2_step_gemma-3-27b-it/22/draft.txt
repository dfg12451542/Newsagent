Wythall, England – A local PE teacher is celebrating the stunning Wimbledon victory of former student Lloyd Glasspool, who, alongside partner Julian Cash, secured the men’s doubles title on Saturday. The win marks a historic moment as the first all-British pair to triumph at the All England Club in 89 years. “To think a student from Woodrush High School in Wythall can win at Wimbledon – it’s just amazing,” exclaimed Joe Maidment, Glasspool’s former PE teacher.nnMr. Maidment was present at Wimbledon to witness Glasspool’s impressive performance during the second round and described the entire tournament run as “inspirational”. He shared that Glasspool, 31, of Redditch, had previously sent a video message to Woodrush High School ahead of their annual sports awards, demonstrating his continued connection to his roots. The duo’s road to victory captivated the nation, culminating in a final that cemented their place in British tennis history.nnBeyond the national pride, Maidment hopes Glasspool’s success will inspire young athletes in the West Midlands. “It should be a message to everyone in the West Midlands to follow their dreams and see what they can achieve,” he stated. He fondly recalled playing tennis against Glasspool himself during the student’s time at Woodrush, playfully admitting, “I can now say I’ve played tennis against a Wimbledon champion. Unfortunately, I did lose 6-0, 6-0, and he was only in year eight at the time!”nnGlasspool, a member of the Warwickshire Lawn Tennis Association, clearly remembers those early matches, joking with his former teacher about “that little thrashing” he delivered. He credits the early competition and support with boosting his confidence, stating, “It gave me a nice little confidence boost to go and do great things in the sport.”  Mr. Maidment expressed immense pride, adding, “I am so, so pleased for him and everyone involved,” and described the Wimbledon title as “the icing on the cake” following Glasspool’s previous wins at Queens and Eastbourne – securing what he called “the triple crown”. Glasspool also extended his gratitude, expressing his “appreciation for the support from you and the whole of the Woodrush School.”
