Orkney, Scotland – The 20th Island Games officially commenced today with a vibrant opening ceremony attended by Princess Anne, providing a memorable experience for athletes and spectators alike. Channel Island competitors described their encounters with the Princess Royal as “amazing,” highlighting her genuine interest in the sporting event and the athletes’ journeys. Alderney runner Benjamin Mutua and his coach, Julie Longden, were among those who had the opportunity to speak with Princess Anne, discussing their travels and participation in the Games. nnMs. Longden expressed her delight, stating, “I have met royalty before but because of her love of sport it was so good to talk to her.” The atmosphere was one of palpable excitement, with attendees praising the organization and spirit of the Games. Television personality Lorraine Kelly, visibly moved by the event, shared that she felt “very emotional” due to her affection for the Orkney Islands and the impressive execution of the opening ceremony. nnThe Island Games, first held in 1985, serve as a platform for athletes from small island nations and territories to compete in a variety of sports. This year’s event is particularly significant as it marks the Games’ 20th anniversary and is being hosted in Orkney, a remote archipelago off the northern coast of Scotland. The event fosters camaraderie and provides valuable competitive experience for athletes who may not have the same opportunities as those from larger nations. nnSpectators traveled from across the Channel Islands to support their teams, with Lucy and Nick Le Boutillier making the journey from Jersey to cheer on their daughter, Tilly, a gymnast competing in the Games. They described a lengthy wait to gain entry to the venue, emphasizing the high demand to witness the opening ceremony. Local resident Doreen Manson, who performed with her exercise group at the ceremony, added, “It’s just something that we’ve never seen before… it’s just great, I think, really exciting.”nnThe 20th Island Games are set to continue throughout the week, offering a celebration of sport, culture, and island identity. The presence of Princess Anne, coupled with the enthusiastic participation of athletes and spectators, has set a positive tone for a competition anticipated to be a resounding success.
