Australia were skittled for 225 all out on the first day of their day/night Test against the West Indies in Adelaide, after a dramatic collapse saw them lose 7 wickets for just 68 runs. Having started strongly at 157-3, the tourists fought back admirably, spearheaded by Shamar Joseph who claimed 4-33, alongside three wickets apiece for Jayden Seales and Justin Greaves. The West Indies responded with 16-1 at the close of play, trailing by 209 runs, with Mitchell Starc marking his 100th Test appearance by dismissing Kevlon Anderson for three.nnMitchell Starc’s milestone was a rare highlight for Australia on a day dominated by West Indies bowling. Despite the early promise, the Australian batting lineup failed to capitalise on a solid platform. Steve Smith’s 48 and Cameron Green’s 46 were the highest scores of the innings, but both fell at crucial moments, triggering the batting collapse. The dinner break appeared to interrupt Australia’s momentum, with wickets tumbling at regular intervals thereafter.nnThe crucial dismissals of Smith and Green proved to be the turning point, allowing the West Indies to seize control. Joseph, in particular, proved a constant menace with his pace and accuracy. Seales and Greaves provided excellent support, exploiting a pitch that offered assistance to the seamers.  The relentless bowling attack applied sustained pressure, ultimately dismantling the Australian batting order.nn“Every pitch has been tough,” conceded Australian all-rounder Cameron Green. “The more balls you spend in the middle you hope it gets better but it just hasn't been the case.” Green also highlighted the challenge of adjusting to the conditions and bowlers, stating, “It’s a bit of a grind first time with these balls, but a lot of learnings. Time in the middle is key - just trying to get used to their bowlers, their conditions.”nnWest Indies faced nine overs under lights at the end of the day, with Starc immediately making his mark on his historic appearance. He secured the breakthrough by bowling Anderson and demonstrated the potential challenges Australia’s bowlers may face. The hosts will look to bowl out West Indies cheaply to gain a significant advantage in this vital Test match.
