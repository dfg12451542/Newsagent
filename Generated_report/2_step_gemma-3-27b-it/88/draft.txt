As Live Aid marks its 40th anniversary, the spotlight is turning to the unsung heroes behind the globally televised concert that raised funds for Ethiopian famine relief. While the legendary performances of artists like Queen and David Bowie are etched in public memory, the vital contributions of individuals working behind the scenes are often overlooked. Among these is Maurice Jones, a rock promoter from the West Midlands whose name appeared prominently on Live Aid tickets, yet remained largely unacknowledged for decades.nnJones, born in Wednesbury and later residing in Walsall, was approached for his expertise due to his extensive connections within the music industry. His daughter, Nicola, reveals that his involvement spanned numerous critical aspects of the event, from venue organization and backstage catering to the complex task of booking the performing bands. “He was very modest,” Nicola shared with BBC WM, “His take on it was that he was there to do a job, he wasn’t there to be in the limelight. He did play an important part.”nnNicola, who attended the Wembley concert with her 15-year-old self, recalls a day filled with star sightings, from Paul McCartney and Elton John backstage to witnessing Freddie Mercury’s iconic performance ignite the crowd. She even found herself seated in the royal box, a row behind David Bowie and two rows behind Princess Diana and Prince Charles.  “Watching the reaction of the audience was really spectacular,” she said, captivated by the atmosphere and the sheer scale of the event.  She admitted she hadn’t fully appreciated Queen's magnitude until that day, realizing their immense popularity while observing the crowd’s reaction.nnDespite his significant role, Jones remained characteristically humble, according to his daughter. “I think, because of my dad’s nature, he didn’t want to promote himself even though he was very good at promoting bands and acts,” Nicola explained. Now, as the 40th anniversary arrives, she hopes her father’s contribution will finally receive the recognition it deserves. “It will be nice if he is remembered in a small way,” she added, expressing her pride in his work and her desire for him to be remembered as an integral part of the historic concert.
