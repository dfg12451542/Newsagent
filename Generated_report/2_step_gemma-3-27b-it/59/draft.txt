Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla, the first Indian astronaut to journey to the International Space Station (ISS), is returning to Earth after a historic mission. The Axiom-4 (Ax-4) spacecraft undocked from the ISS on Monday, carrying Shukla and three other crew members, with a scheduled splashdown off the coast of California in under 24 hours. Indian Science Minister Jitendra Singh anticipates the landing to occur around 15:00 India time (09:30 GMT) on July 15th.nnShukla’s participation in the Ax-4 mission, a collaboration between NASA, ISRO, ESA, and SpaceX facilitated by Axiom Space, represents a monumental leap for India’s space program. ISRO invested 5 billion rupees ($59 million) to secure Shukla’s seat and training, viewing the experience as invaluable preparation for India’s own future human spaceflight endeavors. During their extended stay – longer than the initially planned two weeks – the Ax-4 crew completed 60 scientific experiments, including seven specifically designed by ISRO.nnSpeaking before his departure, Shukla emphasized the broader significance of the mission. “It has been an incredible journey. Even though now it is coming to an end, for you and me there is a long way to go. The journey of our human space mission is very long and difficult. But if we are determined, even the stars are attainable,” Shukla stated. His words reflect the ambitious spirit of a nation investing heavily in its space capabilities.nnFrom orbit, Shukla offered a compelling vision of contemporary India. “Even today we want to know how it looks from space. I'll tell you. From space, today's India looks ambitious. It looks fearless. It looks confident. It looks proud. And so, I can once again say that today's India still looks better than the rest of the world,” he remarked.  His return marks not just the completion of a personal journey, but a significant milestone in India’s growing presence in the global space arena, inspiring a generation to reach for the stars.
