West Belfast: Fire at GAA Club Started Deliberately, Police SaynnIn a shocking incident that has alarmed the local community, a fire that broke out at a GAA club in West Belfast this past Saturday evening was confirmed to have been started deliberately, according to the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI). The blaze, which began at approximately 18:20 BST, resulted in extensive damage to the club's facilities, as stated by PSNI Inspector Dalton. 'It's absolutely unacceptable that a community organisation should be targeted in this way,' Dalton remarked, emphasizing the senseless nature of the act.nnThe incident has drawn sharp criticism and concern from local authorities and community leaders. Belfast city councillor Arder Carson condemned the fire as a 'blatant act of vandalism.' Carson expressed profound disappointment, noting that the club, which plays a crucial role in providing services and opportunities for young people, had been preparing for children's summer camps and the commencement of a new championship season. 'It definitely fills you with despair and some anger that a club that does so much for the local community has been subject to such an attack,' he lamented.nnInsp Dalton and Carson both highlighted the recklessness of setting such a fire, particularly given the tinder-dry conditions and the rural nature of the area. 'On the period of dry weather we have had, to set fires in an area like that which is rural and totally green is just beyond reckless,' Carson stated. Meanwhile, police urge anyone with information on the incident to come forward as they continue to investigate the circumstances surrounding the fire. The community's resilience and determination to rebuild, however, remain unfazed, as they rally together in response to this senseless act.
