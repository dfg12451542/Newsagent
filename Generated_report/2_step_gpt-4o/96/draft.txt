Great Britain's Oliver Rowland clinched his first Formula E title with a strategic fourth-place finish in Berlin, Germany. The Nissan driver emerges as the 10th different champion in the sport's 11-season history, solidifying his position in Formula E with two races still to run, in London later this month.nnReflecting on his performance, Rowland said, 'Honestly, I was just thinking before the race not to have too much damage going into the final round in London.' He also expressed disbelief at his achievement, stating, 'I can't believe it, I have no words. It's incredible. I was trying to play it safe but everybody was so aggressive, so at some point I was all in. Thankfully, I stayed out of trouble.'nnDespite a valiant start at the historic Tempelhof airport circuit, Porsche's Pascal Wehrlein, Rowland's closest contender, slumped to 16th after initially leading on pole. Jaguar's Nick Cassidy claimed victory in Sunday's race, with Jake Dennis of Andretti and DS Penske's Jean-Eric Vergne completing the podium.nnRowland's triumph is underscored by a season of stellar performances, including wins in Mexico, Jeddah, Monaco, and Tokyo, building a formidable lead of 184 points against Wehrlein’s 125 as the London finale approaches. This season has not only etched Rowland's name in racing history but also showcased the fiercely competitive nature of Formula E racing.
