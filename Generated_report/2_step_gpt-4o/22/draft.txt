Wythall PE teacher Joe Maidment is beaming with pride as his former student, Lloyd Glasspool, clinched the men's doubles title at Wimbledon on Saturday, alongside his partner Julian Cash. This victory marks a historic moment as the duo became the first all-British pair to secure the title in 89 years at the prestigious All England Club. 'To think a student from Woodrush High School in Wythall can win at Wimbledon - it's just amazing,' Maidment exclaimed.nnMaidment, who journeyed to Wimbledon to witness Glasspool's impressive play during the tournament, described his former pupil's performance as 'inspirational.' Glasspool, hailing from Redditch, sent a heartfelt message to his alma mater's annual sports awards, recalling his formative years at Woodrush High School. Reflecting on his journey, Glasspool quipped, 'I've come a long way since when we played in year seven or eight and I gave you that little thrashing.'nnThe teacher further emphasized the profound impact of Glasspool's achievement on the local community, noting, 'It should be a message to everyone in the West Midlands to follow their dreams and see what they can achieve.' Since beginning his professional career, Glasspool, now a member of the Warwickshire Lawn Tennis Association, has grasped what Maidment dubbed the 'triple crown,' having won Wimbledon, Queens, and Eastbourne titles.nnGlasspool acknowledged his mentor's influence, stating, 'I appreciate the support from you and the whole of Woodrush School.' His recent triumph at Wimbledon serves as both an inspiration and testament to the potential budding within British tennis, echoing the storied history of the sport within the United Kingdom. With a personal note to Maidment following the tournament, Glasspool's victory is the 'icing on the cake' for a career inspired by early encouragement and unwavering support.
