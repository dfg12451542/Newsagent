England vs India: Brydon Carse keeps England alive on fourth day at Lord'snnIn a thrilling turn of events at Lord's, Brydon Carse's performance with the ball has kept England in the hunt on the fourth day of the test match against India. Bowling from the Pavilion End, Carse was able to dismiss key Indian batsmen Karun Nair and captain Shubman Gill, setting up a potential grandstand finish for the finale. The day ended with India at 58-4, still requiring 135 runs to reach their target of 193 after Ben Stokes dismissed nightwatchman Akash Deep with the final delivery of the day.nnThe match saw an escalation in tension, particularly after the third day's drama, but it truly exploded into life on this fourth day. In a fierce opening spell, England faced exceptional bowling from India's Jasprit Bumrah and Washington Sundar, showcasing their bowling prowess under the baking sun. Marcus Trescothick, England's batting coach, admitted the team had hoped to score above 250 but was satisfied with the chance to bowl on a challenging pitch. Trescothick expressed, 'We would have loved 250 and beyond but we would have taken 190 and our chance to bowl on that pitch and it'll come down to who holds the pressure best.'nnEngland's innings earlier in the day unraveled quickly, with notable dismissals including Harry Brook and former captain Joe Root. Sundar's bowling was instrumental in this, claiming the scalps of Root and Stokes in quick succession, contributing to a record 12 bowled dismissals for the home side in a single Test innings in England. Reflecting on the momentum shifts, India all-rounder Washington Sundar remarked confidently, 'We'd have loved to have about nine wickets [in hand]. It is what it is and we'll definitely win the game.'nnAs the match stands, both teams will head into the final day with an opportunity to emerge victorious, much like when England famously chased down 378 against India at the same venue in 2022. With historical significance in the backdrop and the current series finely poised, the cricketing world watches closely to see if this England side can emulate or even surpass the record-breaking feats of teams from the past. BBC's chief cricket commentator Jonathan Agnew summed up the day's events eloquently: 'We're all a bit hoarse after that. It was absolutely gripping. Even World Cup finals don't quite generate that kind of madness.' The fifth and final day promises a thrilling chapter in this storied cricket rivalry.
