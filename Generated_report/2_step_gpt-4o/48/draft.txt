In a devastating turn of events, the Sudanese city of el-Fasher has become the stage for a fierce battle as the paramilitary group Rapid Support Forces (RSF) stormed the city on Friday. The RSF managed to capture key locations, including a cattle market, a prison, and a military base, causing renewed fear and chaos among the beleaguered population. This incursion is the latest in a series of violent clashes following the descent into civil war in April 2023, fueled by a bitter power struggle between the Sudanese army and the RSF.nnAccording to eyewitness accounts, the RSF's assault on el-Fasher lasted for seven hours, leaving ordinary citizens caught in the crossfire. Mathilde Vu from the Norwegian Refugee Council described the dire conditions, saying, 'What we're hearing is stories of horror and terror and weekly shelling, attacks on civilian infrastructure.' Despite the army's efforts to repel the RSF on Saturday, with claims of inflicting 'heavy losses,' shelling from drones continued, exacerbating the already dire humanitarian situation.nnAs conflict rages on, the city's residents are facing a humanitarian crisis. Local volunteers, risking their lives, are struggling to provide food for the starving populace, with residents of el-Fasher reporting a severe lack of basic necessities - no bread, no work, and a collapse of healthcare services. The deteriorating conditions have forced nearly 379,000 people to flee to Tawila, where they confront outbreaks of cholera and the impending destruction of makeshift shelters by anticipated heavy rains.nnWith the city's infrastructure in ruins, the local community is left to rely on each other's solidarity. Elderly resident Siddig Omar recounted the terrifying reality, stating, 'One of the shells hit a civilian vehicle near my house resulting in the death of five civilians.' As the humanitarian situation worsens and as the conflict continues to fuel one of the worst crises in recent memory, calls for urgent international assistance grow louder, with Mathilde Vu underscoring the need for aid, 'If they have a little bit of food, they will be sharing it among themselves.'nnThe ongoing conflict, which the UN has described as having 'devastating civilian casualties,' continues to trap many Sudanese in a cycle of violence and deprivation. As the RSF and the army prepare for further confrontations, the urgent need for both humanitarian aid and diplomatic intervention becomes ever more critical, as el-Fasher's residents endure what some describe as a city turned 'death trap.'
