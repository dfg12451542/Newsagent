In a decisive 3-26 to 0-15 victory against Meath, Donegal, led by coach Jim McGuinness, secured their place in the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship final against the formidable Kerry side. McGuinness, speaking to BBC Sport, emphasized that Donegal has 'deserved the right' to compete for the championship after their strenuous journey through the season. 'We know what we will get with Kerry,' McGuinness said, acknowledging the challenge posed by their well-seasoned opponents who 'know their way around Croke Park like the back of their hand.'nnDonegal's recent triumph was propelled by remarkable performances from Oisin Gallen, Ciaran Moore, and Conor O'Donnell, with each contributing crucial second-half goals. This victory comes after a challenging previous season, where McGuinness’ hopes were dashed against Galway in the semi-finals. This year, however, things have taken a brighter turn. McGuinness expressed his belief in his team's readiness for the upcoming challenge, stating, 'They know how to win an All-Ireland so it’s a massive, massive challenge but we’ve deserved the right to play that game and I’m delighted they’ve got that opportunity.'nnThe victory not only marks Donegal's resurgence but also sets the stage for an intense final match against Kerry on 27 July. Kerry, the title holders of 2022, claimed their spot in the final by outplaying Tyrone with apparent ease, much like Donegal’s handling of Meath. McGuinness has rallied his team and supporters alike to focus on the task at hand while maintaining high spirits. He remarked on the significance of the occasion, highlighting the balance between savoring the moment and staying focused: 'We have to keep on the job now, prepare as best we can and stay focused on the things that are important.' As Donegal prepares for their showpiece encounter with Kerry, the anticipation builds, promising an electrifying contest on the Irish football stage.
