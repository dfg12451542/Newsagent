In Peterborough, the community's trust in the local police has experienced a notable upswing, credited to the success of the Alliance project, as reported by the Peterborough City Council. The initiative, focused around Lincoln Road in the Millfield area, has increased community confidence in policing and led to a remarkable 700% rise in community intelligence reporting, according to the council.nnThe Alliance project has been a pivotal component of the city’s approach to combatting organized crime. Launched in 2023 under the Home Office’s Clear, Hold and Build strategy, the project has made significant inroads, including the seizure of 700,000 illicit cigarettes, 20,000 vapes, and £115,000 in cash from the area. Trust in the police has also been bolstered by the installation of new drug needle bins, a further step in ensuring community safety.nnDespite these successes, the project has faced challenges, such as difficulties connecting with local groups, as noted in a report to the scrutiny committee. Nonetheless, the agencies involved remain committed to pursuing funding to sustain the initiative, emphasizing its importance for longevity and sustainability. Inspector Sam Tucker of Cambridgeshire Police acknowledged the project's influence yet cited its comprehensive nature as a reason why it might not be applied uniformly across the city.nnHistorically, the Alliance project is part of broader charitable collaborations aimed at crime prevention in the city, including partnerships with Nottinghamshire Police. These efforts have been ongoing for over a decade, involving mentoring for young men and meal provisions for residents, reflecting a long-standing commitment to community support and crime reduction in Peterborough.
