Shubhanshu Shukla: First Indian Astronaut to Go to ISS Returns EarthwardnnAstronaut Shubhanshu Shukla is making his way back to Earth after making history as the first Indian to visit the International Space Station (ISS). A live broadcast on Monday showed the Axiom-4 (Ax-4) mission's undocking, with the four-member crew expected to splash down in the ocean off the coast of California within the next 24 hours, according to Axiom Space. 'It has been an incredible journey,' Shukla stated. 'Even though now it is coming to an end, for you and me there is a long way to go. The journey of our human space mission is very long and difficult. But if we are determined, even the stars are attainable.'nnThe Ax-4 mission reached the ISS on June 26, led by former NASA veteran Peggy Whitson. Alongside Shukla were Polish astronaut Slawosz Uznanski-Wisniewski and Hungarian Tibor Kapu. Indian Science Minister Jitendra Singh confirmed that the splashdown is scheduled for July 15 at approximately 15:00 India time (09:30 GMT). The mission, initially set for two weeks, extended beyond its timeline to allow the crew to perform 60 scientific experiments, including seven projects developed by the Indian Space Research Organization (ISRO).nnThis historic mission comes 41 years after cosmonaut Rakesh Sharma's flight on a Russian Soyuz mission, making Shukla only the second Indian to travel to space. Hailing from Lucknow, born on October 10, 1985, Shukla began his career as a fighter pilot in the Indian Air Force in 2006. ISRO paid a significant sum of 5 billion rupees ($59 million) to secure Shukla's seat on Ax-4 and his training. The mission's success is expected to provide substantial experience for India's future human spaceflight endeavors. 'Even today we want to know how it looks from space. I'll tell you. From space, today's India looks ambitious. It looks fearless. It looks confident. It looks proud,' Shukla noted, reflecting the sentiments of a nation proud of its expanding space aspirations. These significant steps mark a new era for India's participation in international space exploration.
