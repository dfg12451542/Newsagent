South Africa's President Cyril Ramaphosa has suspended Police Minister Senzo Mchunu amid allegations implicating him in connections with organized crime syndicates. This decisive action follows last Sunday's revelations by Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi, the police chief of the KwaZulu-Natal province, who accused Mchunu of meddling in investigations related to political killings and corruption within law enforcement agencies.nnIn a nationally televised address, President Ramaphosa disclosed the establishment of a judicial commission led by the country's deputy chief justice to investigate the charges against Mchunu. Ramaphosa emphasized that the allegations undermined the constitution and posed a threat to national security, highlighting the necessity of an urgent and thorough inquiry. 'These claims call for an urgent and comprehensive investigation,' stated Ramaphosa, appointing law professor Firoz Cachalia as the interim police minister.nnSenzo Mchunu, protesting his innocence, asserted his readiness to address the accusations. 'I welcome and respect the president's decision and pledge my commitment to the process,' Mchunu stated, maintaining that integrity and honor are virtues he upholds. Meanwhile, Gen. Mkhwanazi expounded on his claims, alleging that Mchunu had disbanded a task force initially established in 2018 to probe political killings, purportedly to halt revelations leading to prominent figures linked to a drug cartel.  Mkhwanazi further accused Mchunu of liaising with a controversial businessman, Vusimuzi Matlala, who allegedly financially backed Mchunu's political aspirations.nnThe allegations have sent ripples through South Africa's political landscape, compelling Ramaphosa to cancel an official visit to Spain to focus on this evolving scandal. Public pressure is mounting for swift action, with many South Africans anxiously awaiting the commission's findings, as concerns about corruption within the nation's police force continue to grow.
