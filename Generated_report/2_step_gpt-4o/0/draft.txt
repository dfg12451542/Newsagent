Memories of Cirencester's Old Station are being sought for an upcoming history festival, as the town temporarily reopens its historic railway site. Running from October 25 to November 1, the Cirencester History Festival promises a range of talks and activities celebrating both local and global history. The heart of this year's event will be the town's Grade-II listed Old Station, famously designed by renowned engineer Isambard Kingdom Brunel and closed since 1965.nnFestival producer Jess Yarrow is inviting the public to contribute personal stories, photographs, and any memorabilia they might possess that reflect the station's vibrant past. 'We know that there are lots of people out there who can remember the station in its heyday,' Ms. Yarrow remarked, expressing enthusiasm for community engagement. 'We'd love to hear stories, see photographs, and have any memorabilia. We'll be dressing the station as if it were a station with prams and benches and cases, so anything like that, we'd love to know about it.'nnThe festival aims to transform the Old Station, often perceived as a 'slightly sad, empty-looking building,' into a bustling historical exhibit. During World War Two, the station played a critical role in the town, welcoming evacuees and soldiers, and it is this storied past that Ms. Yarrow hopes will resonate with visitors.nnArchitect Jonathan Rixon, responsible for refurbishing the station into his office, will lead the tours of the building. Rixon is keen on preserving this piece of industrial heritage. 'The building holds many hidden treasures, including a boardroom where Brunel sought a meeting space outside London,' Rixon noted. A passionate advocate for local history, he emphasized the importance of sharing this legacy with Cirencester's residents. The festival will also include an audio project of shared stories and a virtual reality experience developed with Cirencester College students, transporting attendees to the station's Victorian era heyday.
