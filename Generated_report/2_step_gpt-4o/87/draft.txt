Artists Fear AI Using Their Talent for Free, Raising Concerns Over Copyright and CompensationnnIn a rapidly evolving digital age, artists in Herefordshire, including Lily Dooner, are voicing their fears about the rising influence of artificial intelligence (AI) on their livelihoods. These artists are anxious about AI technology potentially replicating and using their unique voices and talents without adequate compensation. 'What really scares me is the idea that one day I might be watching a film, and hear a voice created out of snippets of mine', the 28-year-old Leominster resident expressed. Her concern echoes a growing sentiment among creators who worry that AI could undermine their efforts by making it easier to mass-produce art similar to theirs, often without their consent or financial benefit.nnThe debate surrounding AI and intellectual property is not new. Historical concerns have been voiced by figures like Ed Newton Rex, who highlighted that AI companies often utilize artists' work to build their models, subsequently flooding the market with imitations. This practice results in diminished financial returns for genuine human creators. Proponents of AI, however, like Tim Evans, artistic director and CEO of Powerhouse, argue that when used correctly, AI can be a 'great democratizing tool for creativity'. Yet, he acknowledges the need for a fair compensation model, stating, 'As yet the compensation question is being left unanswered.'nnWhile some artists view AI as a potential complement to their creative processes, others remain apprehensive. Visual artist Rebecca Farkas confessed her concerns to BBC Politics Midlands: 'AI could look at my animations and then it could copy it in exactly my style... you could put a prompt in to say 'make an animation like Rebecca Farkas'.' With the current legislative framework placing the responsibility on creators to opt out of AI data scraping, many artists, like Peter Kyle, are advocating for comprehensive measures to ensure their rights are maintained in the digital era. Kyle emphasizes, 'I will give them the rights that they have in the analogue age… fit for the digital age.'nnAs AI technology continues to mature, the art world stands at a crossroads. The issue underscores a critical need for dialogues between technology developers, artists, and policymakers to balance innovation with respect for artistic integrity and intellectual property rights.
