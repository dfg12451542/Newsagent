Barrios vs Pacquiao: Trainer Dave Coldwell on why he won't watch legend's comeback fightnnAs boxing icon Manny Pacquiao gears up for his much-anticipated comeback bout against WBC welterweight champion Mario Barrios, not everyone is onboard with the spectacle. Esteemed British trainer Dave Coldwell has voiced his dissent, stating plainly, 'I won't be watching Manny Pacquiao's comeback fight on Saturday.' Coldwell elaborates that the fight seems more like 'a nod to nostalgia rather than merit,' which he believes undermines the efforts of current fighters striving through traditional pathways to success.nnPacquiao, who is revered as one of the sport's all-time greats with an unprecedented record of being an eight-division world champion, announced his return to the ring earlier this year. At 46, his decision to challenge Barrios directly, bypassing any tune-up fights typically expected of returning fighters, has raised eyebrows. Coldwell commented on this unusual approach, expressing his surprise and disappointment that Pacquiao might be taking 'a shortcut.'nnHistorically, the boxing landscape is replete with legends making comeback attempts, but not all have met with the success they once had. Pacquiao's quick jump to a title challenge echoes such previous endeavors, which, as Coldwell fears, sets a 'strange precedent.' Citing a discussion with WBC president Mauricio Sulaiman, Coldwell underscores the potential risks and the message it sends to upcoming fighters. The trainer's concerns revolve not just around Pacquiao's legacy but also around what implications his comeback might have for the integrity and hierarchy of competitive boxing.nnMario Barrios, Pacquiao's opponent, is no stranger to high stakes, having been a two-time WBC super-middleweight world champion. His involvement sets the stage for Pacquiao's bout planned for 19 July, less than a week away. With criticism from boxing circles pouring in and support spanning global fans keen on witnessing another chapter of Pacquiao's storied career, the fight promises to be a focal point of debate and anticipation, reflective of a sport where history and present-day dynamics often collide.
