Swansea made history on Sunday by hosting the UK's first-ever edition of the IRONMAN Pro Series. Despite facing challenging conditions with air temperatures soaring above 28°C and water temperatures surpassing 24°C, athletes exhibited remarkable performances. The professional swim was ruled non-wetsuit, marking a first for elite athletes competing under such conditions in the UK.nnDefending champion Harry Palmer claimed back-to-back victories, completing the race in 3:51:18, just 49 seconds ahead of France's Leon Chevalier, with Kieran Lindars rounding out the podium in third. 'The course is amazing, especially with weather like this, and the Gower is incredible,' Palmer told the BBC, acknowledging the challenging conditions and praising the support from the Welsh fans.nnKat Matthews, the overall IRONMAN Pro Series 2024 winner, continued to solidify her dominance with a victory in Swansea, clocking in at 4:20:37. Following her were Britain's Rebecca Anderbury and Germany's Danila Kleiser, who finished second and third, respectively. Matthews remarked, 'Three years feels like a lifetime. A lot has happened since then,' reflecting on her last win in Swansea in 2022.nnThe successful event saw thousands of supporters and volunteers lining the 70-mile course, offering encouragement and welfare support throughout the day. Race Director Rebecca Sutherland noted the event's success, stating, 'It was another incredible race with thousands of athletes crossing the famous red carpet finish line to rapturous spectator support.' With over half of the age-group field tackling their first IRONMAN 70.3, it was a memorable experience for competitors and families alike. The race is set to return to Swansea next summer for the fourth edition of the event.
