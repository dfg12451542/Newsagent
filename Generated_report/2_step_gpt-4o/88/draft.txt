Live Aid at 40: 'My Wednesbury father was an unsung hero of the concert'nnAs Live Aid marks its 40th anniversary, the role of a modest, yet pivotal figure in its history is being brought to light. Maurice Jones, a rock promoter from Wednesbury, is recalled by his daughter Nicola as a significant, though often overlooked, contributor to the iconic 1985 event. Known for his background in the music industry, Jones was tasked with crucial elements of the Live Aid concert organization, including the arrangement of the venue, backstage catering, and band bookings. His daughter reminisces, 'He was very modest. His take on it was that he was there to do a job, he wasn't there to be in the limelight. He did play an important part.'nnDespite not seeking the spotlight for himself, Maurice Jones's efforts were recognized in the music community, with his name featured prominently at the top of the event's ticket. Nicola Jones, who accompanied her father to the Wembley concert at the age of 15, cherishes her memories of being amidst legendary figures such as Paul McCartney, Elton John, and David Bowie. She vividly recalls the awe-inspiring performance by Freddie Mercury and Queen, noting, 'Watching the reaction of the audience was really spectacular.' Nicola shared her recollections with pride, stating how the experience opened her eyes to the grandeur of bands like Queen.nnWhile Maurice Jones passed away in 2009, his legacy endures through the fond memories his family holds of his noteworthy contributions to Live Aid. 'It will be nice if he is remembered in a small way,' Nicola expressed, highlighting her desire for her father's quiet yet essential role to be acknowledged. As the world celebrates the monumental impact of Live Aid, the spotlight briefly shifts to the unsung heroes like Jones who made the concert a reality, honoring his legacy on this 40th anniversary.
