A petition to restore the 128-year-old Victoria Marine Lake in St Helier has garnered significant local interest, recently reaching 1,000 signatures. This surge in community support signifies a collective effort to preserve a structure considered by many as a cornerstone of Jersey's coastal heritage. nnPetition organiser Nina Zaech stressed the pool's importance, calling it a 'vital piece of Jersey's heritage' that could enhance health, leisure, and tourism initiatives. This sentiment is echoed by campaigners, including the newly-formed Save Our Pool Working Group, who are advocating for the site's urgent restoration. The mass public interest has triggered an official response from Jersey's government, indicating the potential for further developments.nnThe need for conservation was underscored by a 2024 inspection, which revealed that the pool required 'comprehensive work.' Despite this, Infrastructure Minister Andy Jehan stated last month that repairs would be 'uneconomical' due to planned sea defence upgrades. The pool, which boasts a distinctive horseshoe shape and is surrounded by the scenic backdrop of St Helier, continues to inspire passion among both locals and visitors who frequent the historic site.nnThe Victoria Marine Lake has stood the test of time as a landmark feature in St Helier, offering a glimpse into the past as well as providing a unique recreational space. Historical images and accounts portray a lively venue that has been a cherished part of the coastal landscape. As the debate over its future unfolds, advocates for its preservation remain hopeful that their efforts will lead to a viable solution that honors the pool's legacy while accommodating modern coastal needs.
