An ultramarathon runner was airlifted to safety during a 55-mile mountain race in the Lake District on Saturday, highlighting the dangers faced by athletes taking part in extreme endurance events. The incident occurred when the runner collapsed due to heat exhaustion while navigating the challenging Grisedale Valley route during the 70th anniversary of the race.nnThe Patterdale Mountain Rescue Team (PMRT) was dispatched to the scene at around 16:00 BST after receiving reports that the participant became pale, began vomiting, and eventually lost consciousness. The North West Ambulance Service collaborated with the coastguard, who urgently dispatched a helicopter to the remote area to winch the runner to safety, transporting him to the King George playing field in Patterdale.nnUpon arrival, the runner was assessed by a team doctor stationed at the Patterdale Base before being transferred to a hospital for more comprehensive medical examinations. The rescue operation required the involvement of 11 rescue team members working in coordination with the coastguard, illustrating the significant resources often required in such high-risk sporting events.nnThis rescue emerges as a stark reminder of the inherent hazards of trail running, particularly in challenging terrains such as those found in the Lake District. As the race celebrates its 70-year history, this incident underscores the importance of safety measures and the critical role of rescue services, such as the Galloway Mountain Rescue, known for assisting in emergencies across various terrains in the UK.
