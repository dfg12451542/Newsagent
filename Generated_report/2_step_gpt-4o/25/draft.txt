The Royal Observatory Greenwich has unveiled the shortlist for its prestigious ZWO Astronomy Photographer of the Year competition, highlighting some of the most breathtaking images capturing the wonders of space. The competition, known for showcasing stunning astrophotography from around the world, offers a glimpse into the captivating and often awe-inspiring phenomena that occur beyond our planet's atmosphere.nnSam Wen, the founder and CEO of ZWO, emphasized the impact of astrophotography in bridging gaps and encouraging exploration and curiosity. '...we believe that astrophotography is not only a way to record the cosmos, but also a way to inspire curiosity, foster education and build communities that transcend borders,' Wen stated, underscoring the global nature of the endeavor. 'Everyone deserves a chance to connect with the Universe — and through our support, we hope to bring that experience to more people.'nnThis year's submissions included a striking photo of an eruption captured on November 7, 2024, and an image shaped like a pomegranate, photographed by Deqian Li during a six-day camping trip in China. Another notable entry features the passage of Comet C/2023 A3, snapped on October 12, 2024, at Pu'u O Kaimukī Park in Hawaii—one of the year's most anticipated astronomical events. Moreover, the striking image of the aurora in Senja, Norway, depicts the aurora’s green and violet hues against the starry sky.nnThe winners of the competition's nine categories, alongside two special prizes and an overall winner, are set to be announced on September 11, 2025. Enthusiasts and admirers of these celestial wonders will have the opportunity to view these stunning photographs at a special exhibition at the National Maritime Museum, starting September 12. With each photograph being a testament to the art and science of astrophotography, the exhibit promises to be a visual journey through the universe's vast and intricate beauty.
