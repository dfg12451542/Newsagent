West Indies' Test series against Australia took an exciting turn as the visiting team collapsed dramatically to 225 all out on the first day of the day/night Test in Barbados. After building a solid foundation at 157-3, the tourists succumbed to the West Indies' bowling attack, losing their remaining seven wickets for only 68 additional runs.nnAustralia's Steve Smith top-scored with a cautious 48, while Cameron Green contributed 46 runs before both batsmen departed in quick succession around the dinner break, triggering the batting collapse. Reflecting on the day, Green noted the challenging conditions, saying, 'Every pitch has been tough. The more balls you spend in the middle you hope it gets better but it just hasn't been the case. It's a bit of a grind first time with these balls, but a lot of learnings. Time in the middle is key - just trying to get used to their bowlers, their conditions.'nnThe West Indies' bowling unit, led by Shamar Joseph, who claimed 4-33, with Jayden Seales and Justin Greaves each taking three wickets, played a crucial role in dismantling the Australian batting lineup. The home team ended the day at 16-1, trailing by 209 runs. Mitchell Starc, marking his 100th Test appearance, provided an early breakthrough for Australia by bowling opener Kevlon Anderson for three, with Anderson failing to contain an inside edge.nnStarc's milestone sees him join the ranks of just two Australian pace bowlers to reach a century of Test matches, following in the footsteps of Glenn McGrath. His contribution on the field underscores the extensive experience and skill he brings to the Australian attack. As the Test continues, both teams will look to capitalize on the day's events to gain an influential foothold in the series.
