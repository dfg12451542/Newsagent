Steam locomotive starts new journey in Sheringham, sparking excitement among railway enthusiasts and history buffs alike. The historic steam engine that re-entered service at the North Norfolk Railway impressed crowds on its inaugural journey, described as 'absolutely incredible' after years of restoration efforts. The locomotive, now running from Sheringham to Holt, marks a triumphant milestone in its remarkable story.nnAlex Alder, a key figure in the engine's revival, expressed his amazement: 'After nine years of efforts, for the very first time ever she pulled passenger trains. It was absolutely incredible.' The engine, which Alder likened to 'a mythical Egyptian tomb,' was discovered in an unusual setting, a convent in Hertfordshire. The 39-ton marvel, submerged in obscurity for years, was restored at the cost of approximately £200,000, a venture that involved a dedicated team of volunteers.nnGraham Hukins, a representative of the North Norfolk Railway, hailed the occasion as 'absolutely wonderful' as they celebrated the engine's addition to their fleet. This event holds special significance as it coincides with the railway's 50th anniversary of operations, elevating the locomotive to the status of a national icon. As noted by Stuart Nelhams, driving steam locomotives is always special, but this one has become a 'bit of a pop star' with its storied past and bright future.nnThe resurrection and operational debut of the steam locomotive not only serves as a testament to diligent restoration efforts but also symbolizes a renewed appreciation for England's railway heritage. As the evocative sound of the steam engine echoes once more through the countryside, it reminds us of a bygone era, now revived to stir the admiration of generations to come.
