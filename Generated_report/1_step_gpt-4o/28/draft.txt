Japan 22-31 Wales: Fly-half Dan Edwards praises character after elusive winnnOn a sweltering July afternoon in Kobe, the Welsh rugby team emerged victorious against Japan, securing a 31-22 win that both players and fans had eagerly anticipated. The win was particularly satisfying for the team and their supporters after enduring a challenging period with 18 previous losses. Dan Edwards, Wales' young fly-half, expressed his delight, saying, 'It was about getting a win, I'm absolutely chuffed.' His performance on the field, including a pivotal late try, was instrumental in Wales' success.nnThe match was marked by moments of brilliance, particularly from Ospreys fly-half Dan Edwards and replacement flanker Taine Plumtree. Edwards, at just 22 years old, showcased both skill and composure, scoring a crucial try set up by Plumtree's audacious reverse pass. Plumtree, emphasizing the team's mantra 'to be brave and express ourselves,' came on for Cardiff flanker Alex Mann and made an immediate impact despite the challenging heat and humidity.nnReflecting on their performance, interim head coach Matt Sherratt praised the team's decision-making under pressure. 'That last play summarises it for me,' Sherratt said, acknowledging the risky strategy that deviated from safer options. Across the team, resilience and determination were evident, themes echoed by winger Keelan Giles, who experienced a personal triumph as well. After nearly nine years of missed opportunities against Japan, Giles finally played and contributed to the memorable Welsh victory, noting, 'A win has been a long time coming for fans of Wales. It's something to build on now.'nnThe victory also serves as a significant morale boost for Welsh rugby, a sentiment shared by Edwards, who hopes this is the start of a turnaround. With the upcoming autumn internationals, there is a renewed sense of optimism within the squad. Edwards' assertion that 'this group has worked extremely hard over the last couple of years' underlines the collective effort that has driven them, even through adversity. As Wales looks to future encounters, the win against Japan stands as both a confidence booster and a foundation for future success.
