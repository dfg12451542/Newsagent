Steam locomotive enthusiasts and the community of Sheringham celebrated a historic moment on Saturday, as a steam engine re-entered service at the North Norfolk Railway. This moment marked the culmination of a nine-year refurbishment project led by Alex Alder, who poured approximately £200,000 and countless hours of volunteer effort into restoring the locomotive. nn'After nine years of efforts, for the very first time ever she pulled passenger trains. It was absolutely incredible,' expressed a jubilant Alex Alder. His journey to discovering the locomotive reads like a tale out of a storybook. 'Discovering the engine was like hearing of a mythical Egyptian tomb,' he recounted, referring to the surprise and excitement of finding the 39-ton engine in a convent in Hertfordshire. nnThis restored locomotive will now serve the North Norfolk Railway, pulling passenger carriages from Sheringham to Holt. Graham Hukins, of North Norfolk Railway, expressed his delight at seeing the engine in action, describing the scene as 'absolutely wonderful'. This weekend marked not only the engine's re-debut, but also the 50th anniversary of the North Norfolk Railway's operation of train services, making the event even more special for all involved. nnHukins emphasized the significance of this historical restoration by noting that this was the first time the locomotive was paired with passenger carriages, stating it is 'an absolute delight to have it join our working fleet.' As the locomotive now commences a new chapter on the tracks, it serves as a testament to the passion and dedication of the community and volunteers who revived it from a state of neglect into a symbol of engineering marvel.
