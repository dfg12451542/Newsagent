West Indies v Australia: Tourists Collapse to 225 All Out on Day One of Day/Night TestnnAustralia's tour of the West Indies began with a dramatic collapse as the tourists were dismissed for 225 on the opening day of the day/night Test. Having reached a relatively solid position at 157-3, Australia lost their final seven wickets for just 68 runs, illustrating the challenging nature of the pitch. 'Every pitch has been tough,' said Cameron Green, who contributed with a solid 46 runs, underscoring the difficult batting conditions. 'The more balls you spend in the middle you hope it gets better but it just hasn't been the case,' he remarked, reflecting the batsmen's struggle.nnDespite top-scoring, Steve Smith's dismissal for 48 triggered a collapse, coming just after the dinner break. Green, too, fell around the same period, leaving Australia vulnerable at the hands of a disciplined West Indies attack. Bowler Shamar Joseph led the charge for the hosts, claiming 4-33, while Jayden Seales and Justin Greaves each took three wickets, stifling Australia's progress further.nnIn response, West Indies closed the day at 16-1, 209 runs behind. Veteran pacer Mitchell Starc, playing his 100th Test, made a significant mark by dismissing opener Kevlon Anderson for three. Starc's milestone achievement was further celebrated as he became just the second Australian pace bowler after Glenn McGrath to achieve such a feat. 'It's a bit of a grind first time with these balls, but a lot of learnings,' Green added, emphasizing the challenges faced and the lessons absorbed from this first innings.nnWith the pitch continuing to offer challenges, both sides will be keenly aware of the critical role conditions will play in determining the outcome of this Test. As the evening fell over the day/night fixture, the anticipation grows for day two, with West Indies looking to capitalize on a promising start against an Australian side eager to shrug off their initial collapse.
