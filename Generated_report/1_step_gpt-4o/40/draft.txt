Manny Pacquiao's return to the boxing ring is causing waves of both anticipation and critique as he prepares for his comeback fight against Mario Barrios on July 19. The Filipino legend, renowned for being an eight-division world champion, is skipping the conventional path for returning fighters and stepping directly into the spotlight with a title bout. While excitement brews among fans and various segments of the boxing community, not everyone is celebrating this next chapter in Pacquiao's storied career.nnDave Coldwell, a seasoned trainer, has voiced his dissent regarding Pacquiao's immediate leap into a high-stakes fight. 'I won't be watching Manny Pacquiao's comeback fight on Saturday,' Coldwell firmly stated, highlighting his belief that Pacquiao's return prioritizes nostalgia over the merit-based progress of contemporary fighters. 'It feels like a shortcut,' Coldwell elaborated, 'a nod to nostalgia rather than merit, and that's unfair to fighters who are working their way up properly.'nnAdding historical context, Pacquiao, at 46, is significantly older than his opponent, Mario Barrios, who is just 30 and holds a professional record of 29 victories, one draw, and two defeats. Despite Pacquiao's legendary status, Coldwell's concern resides in the broader implications of such a comeback, arguing that it undermines the efforts of fighters climbing the ranks through challenging bouts. 'I had a brief exchange with Mauricio Sulaiman, the WBC president, on social media about this. He wasn't pleased with my doubt but, in my view, it sets a strange precedent,' Coldwell remarked.nnThe WBA president's disapproval does little to assuage Coldwell's fears that Pacquiao's potential successes might embolden him to prolong his career, possibly at the expense of his long-term health. 'The most concerning scenario would be if Pacquiao delivers a satisfactory performance and sees it as a moral victory, believing he can continue, which could be perilous in the long run.' As fans and pundits eagerly await the result of this boxing spectacle, the conversation around the ethics and impacts of such comebacks remains crucial to the sport's community.
