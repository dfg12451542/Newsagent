Live Aid at 40: 'My Wednesbury father was an unsung hero of the concert'nnAs the world commemorates the 40th anniversary of the legendary Live Aid concert, attention turns to the unsung heroes behind this historic event. Among them was Maurice Jones, a rock promoter from Wednesbury, whose contributions helped shape one of the most pivotal moments in music history. Reflecting on her father's legacy, Nicola Jones shared fond memories of his role in the concert with BBC WM, emphasizing, 'He was very modest. His take on it was that he was there to do a job, he wasn't there to be in the limelight.'nnMaurice Jones, born in Wednesbury and residing in Walsall, was instrumental in organizing various logistical aspects of Live Aid, such as the venue setup, backstage catering, and artist bookings. Though his name was prominently displayed on the concert tickets, his contributions have largely gone unnoticed by the public. Ms. Jones remembers attending the Wembley concert at age 15, sitting behind David Bowie and mere rows away from Princess Diana and Prince Charles. The atmosphere, she recalls, was 'wonderful,' with performances from icons like Queen, Paul McCartney, and Elton John leaving an indelible mark on her.nnLive Aid, viewed by approximately two billion people across more than 100 countries, owes much of its success to individuals like Jones. Besides Live Aid, Maurice Jones also played a critical role in launching the Monsters of Rock festival, which eventually evolved into the now-renowned Download Festival. His enduring influence on the music festival landscape underscores the significance of his work. As Ms. Jones expressed proudly, 'It will be nice if he is remembered in a small way.' As Live Aid turns 40, the event serves as a poignant reminder of the extraordinary, yet often unnoticed contributions of people like Maurice Jones in orchestrating such monumental occasions.
