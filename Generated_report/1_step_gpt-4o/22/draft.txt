Wythall PE teacher hails ex-student Glasspool for Wimbledon title win.nnJoe Maidment, a physical education teacher at Woodrush High School in Wythall, has expressed his admiration and pride for his former student, Lloyd Glasspool, who alongside partner Julian Cash achieved a historical victory at Wimbledon. The duo became the first all-British team in 89 years to clinch the men's doubles title at the prestigious All England Club. 'To think a student from Woodrush High School in Wythall can win at Wimbledon - it's just amazing,' said Maidment, highlighting the extraordinary achievement of the former pupil.nnMaidment, who traveled to Wimbledon to witness Glasspool's second-round match, described his performance throughout the tournament as 'inspirational'. Glasspool, a 31-year-old from Redditch and a member of the Warwickshire Lawn Tennis Association, has now added the Wimbledon title to his already impressive victories at Queens and Eastbourne. Maidment joyously remarked, 'It should be a message to everyone in the West Midlands to follow their dreams and see what they can achieve.' The celebration and recognition reverberated beyond Glasspool's immediate circle, inspiring students and athletes in the region.nnIn a personal nod to his cherished past, Glasspool fondly recalled his early encounters with Maidment, humorously acknowledging a time as a schoolboy when he outplayed his teacher. 'I've come a long way since when we played in year seven or eight and I gave you that little thrashing,' Glasspool jested, reflecting on the confidence these early victories instilled in him. Expressing gratitude, he praised the encouragement and support he received from both Maidment and the wider Woodrush School community.nnAdding to the celebratory atmosphere, Glasspool sent a video message from the Wimbledon courts to his alma mater's recent annual sports awards, reinforcing the bridge between past mentors and present triumphs. 'I appreciate the support from you and the whole of the Woodrush School,' the newly crowned men's doubles champion expressed, cementing a legacy of inspiration for future generations of aspiring athletes.
