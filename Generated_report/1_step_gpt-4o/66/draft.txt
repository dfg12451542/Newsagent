Warwick's historic site of 800-year-old significance is set for a transformational upgrade as £2.8 million in funds has been secured to restore two 15th-century buildings into affordable homes. The funding comes largely thanks to an award from the National Lottery Heritage Fund. These buildings, part of an ancient leper hospital complex, are set to find new life in the form of housing, while also preserving their historical legacy.nnThe initiative, known as the St Michael's Place project, aims to 'rescue and reuse' the derelict Master's House and St Michael's Chapel in Saltisford, Warwick. Councillor Helen Adkins expressed her excitement about the project, stating, 'We will be able to preserve the legacy of the site and rescue these long-neglected historic buildings, offering them a sustainable future.' The project is a collaborative effort between West Midlands Heritage and Warwick District Council, who have been working diligently to breathe new life into these Grade II* listed structures that have been abandoned for more than five decades.nnCorinne Brazier, chair of West Midlands Heritage, praised the efforts of her colleagues and the crucial support from the local community, attributing these efforts as key to reaching this 'important delivery stage.' She further added, 'We are now able to ensure that St Michael's Place will have a very homely future.' Part of the vision for the site includes creating a heritage community assistant role, implementing heritage craft skills learning opportunities, and hosting community events and activities.nnThis revitalization project not only promises to deliver affordable housing but also aims to enrich the local community by preserving a significant portion of its heritage. The plan demonstrates a keen commitment to maintaining the cultural integrity of Warwick while also providing modern amenities and opportunities for its residents. The site’s development is a step towards integrating historic preservation with community benefit, a strategy that hopes to engage and inspire future generations.
