Memories of Cirencester's Old Station are being sought as part of this year’s Cirencester History Festival, set to take place from October 25 to November 1. The festival, which aims to celebrate both local and global history, will reopen the town’s Grade-II listed Old Station for a series of activities, including tours and exhibitions. Designed by the illustrious engineer Isambard Kingdom Brunel, the station closed its doors in 1965, but it remains a poignant landmark in the community.nnFestival producer Jess Yarrow expressed a desire to collect stories and memorabilia from the station’s heyday. 'We know that there are lots of people out there who can remember the station in its heyday,' Yarrow said. Scenes from when the station was bustling with life, greeting daily commuters to welcoming evacuees and soldiers during World War Two, are sought to bring the past alive. 'We'd love to hear stories, we’d love to see photographs and have any memorabilia that people have got tucked away,' she added.nnArchitect Jonathan Rixon, currently refurbishing the station to be used as his offices, will lead the historic tours. 'The building holds many hidden treasures, including a boardroom where Brunel sought a meeting space outside London,' Rixon noted. His commitment stems from a passion for preserving the industrial heritage of Cirencester. Adding to the historical experience, students from Cirencester College, in collaboration with Rixon, have developed a virtual reality project to recreate the station's Victorian days.nnThe festival highlights the Old Station as more than just 'a slightly sad, empty-looking building,' but as a significant part of Cirencester’s history. It will feature an audio project with stories of the station's past. On the Saturdays of the festival, the community can participate in free events that revitalize the 'golden age of rail,' creating an immersive experience of times gone by.
