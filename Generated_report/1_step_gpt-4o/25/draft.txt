The Royal Observatory Greenwich has unveiled the shortlist for its prestigious annual ZWO Astronomy Photographer of the Year competition, an event celebrated for capturing the awe-inspiring beauty of space. This year’s entries once again showcase an eclectic mix of stunning celestial phenomena, snapped by talented photographers from around the globe.nnSam Wen, founder and CEO of ZWO, the competition's sponsor, emphasized the significance of astrophotography as both a record of the cosmos and a beacon of inspiration. '...we believe that astrophotography is not only a way to record the cosmos, but also a way to inspire curiosity, foster education and build communities that transcend borders,' said Wen. He further highlighted his mission to democratize access to the universe's wonders: 'Everyone deserves a chance to connect with the Universe — and through our support, we hope to bring that experience to more people.'nnThis year’s alluring images include a detailed capture of a celestial eruption on November 7, 2024, and a vibrant portrayal of Comet C/2023 A3 passing over Pu'u O Kaimukī Park in Hawaii on October 12, 2024. These photographs reflect the meticulous efforts of photographers such as Deqian Li, who spent six days camping in China's Hongyuan County to photograph an exquisitely pomegranate-shaped nebula.nnAs anticipation builds for the announcement of winners across nine categories, along with two special prizes and the overall champion on September 11, 2025, enthusiasts can look forward to a special exhibition displaying these celestial photographs at the National Maritime Museum, starting September 12. These breathtaking visuals not only celebrate human ingenuity but also invite the public to gaze beyond our earthly bounds into the vast unknown.
