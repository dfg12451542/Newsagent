Sudan civil war: RSF storms cattle market and prison in 'death trap' city  nnSudan's city of el-Fasher has become the latest battleground in the country's ongoing civil war, eerily described as a 'death trap' by Mathilde Vu from the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC). On Friday, the already volatile situation deteriorated further when the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), a powerful paramilitary group, stormed parts of the city including a cattle market, a prison, and a military base. The incursion led to a fierce seven-hour battle, witnesses reported to the BBC. 'What we're hearing is stories of horror and terror and weekly shelling, attacks on civilian infrastructure,' Vu stated, accentuating the grim reality of those trapped in el-Fasher.nnThe RSF fighters publicly flaunted their temporary dominion on social media platforms by sharing videos of themselves strolling through vacant stockyards, further taunting the fragility of the city's defenses. As the morning sun rose on Saturday, the national army launched a retaliatory attack that repelled the RSF beyond the city limits. However, the reprieve was short-lived as continued shelling and drone strikes engulfed the area, according to local resident Siddig Omar, who described the incessant attacks that claimed civilian lives, including five individuals who were killed when a shell hit a nearby vehicle.nnThis tragic scene unfolds within a broader context of civil upheaval. Sudan plunged into civil war in April 2023 due to a bitter power tussle between the nation's military and the RSF, leading to a relentless wave of violence and tragedy. Today, nearly 379,000 people have fled to Tawila, a precarious refuge threatened by a cholera outbreak and the impending monsoon rains that could devastate their makeshift shelters. 'People [in el-Fasher] just rely on the solidarity of others,' added Vu, highlighting the severe humanitarian crisis, 'If they have a little bit of food, they will be sharing it among themselves.'nnThe situation in el-Fasher is dire, compounded by lack of food, water, and medical supplies. Desperate voices, like that of a local resident, resonate with a plea for global attention: 'We are calling out for help - please, we urgently need assistance.' The haunting specter of food scarcity, with even basics like ombaz and other commodities becoming rare, underscores the urgent need for humanitarian aid in a city grappling with the ravages of civil strife.
