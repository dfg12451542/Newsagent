F1: The Movie's Callie Cooke took to the track quite literally as she performed a live pit stop during last year's British Grand Prix at Silverstone. The actress, who stars as part of a fictional Formula 1 team, APXGP, embraced the high-speed world of motorsports with enthusiasm. Cooke described the experience as 'mad,' expressing disbelief at having been allowed to participate in such a critical element of the race. 'It was one of the best experiences of my life,' she stated, reflecting on the thrill of working in real grand prix conditions.nnThe film, which opened globally on June 25, showcases Silverstone not merely as a backdrop but as an authentic race playground where Cooke engaged in training to blend seamlessly with seasoned mechanics and engineers. The Northamptonshire native leveraged her proximity to Silverstone and familiarity with local car culture, which she credited for helping her 'exist in the world of a garage' effectively. 'I knew that if I was going to play a woman in this male-dominated industry, I had to hold my own and get stuck in,' Cooke shared.nnAmongst her co-stars, Brad Pitt, known for his devotion to authenticity in filmmaking, was particularly smitten with the British locale and its people. 'Pitt loved it and he loved the crew and he has such a British sense of humour,' Cooke revealed, highlighting the camaraderie built over a year of immersion in the racing circuit. This unique filming setup allowed not just for the authenticity of the scenes but also for Cooke to be the only actor integrated into a pit crew seasoned in real-world motorsport operations, confirming the film’s commitment to genuine portrayal of the high-octane sport.nnThe production’s connection to Silverstone extended beyond memorability; it served as a linchpin for driving training and substantial portions of the movie's first half. In being part of such engagements, especially the live pit stop executed as competitively as any other professional team, Cooke exemplified the blend of cinematic magic and motor racing culture, a once-in-a-lifetime convergence cherished by the film's cast and crew.
