The Wurzels, a beloved British band known for their Scrumpy and Western style of music, were forced to abruptly end their performance at Party at the Palace in Wells, Somerset due to the extreme temperatures. The set, which reached its peak amidst the blazing 31C heat, became unbearable for the band members, leading them to leave the stage for health reasons.nnThe band's accordionist, Tommy Banner, recounted the harrowing moment, explaining, 'Both Pete and I appear to have fully recovered from sun and heat exhaustion. Up on stage, there was no air moving and we were facing straight into the direct, unobstructed rays of a very strong sun.' Banner himself felt disoriented, suffering from symptoms akin to sunstroke, while the band's lead singer, Pete Budd, was notably missing from his usual spot. 'I was about to announce the last song and Pete wasn’t beside me where he normally is. He was in a bad way,' Banner elaborated, highlighting the urgency of their situation.nnPete Budd, who is due to celebrate his 85th birthday on July 18, took over as the lead singer of the Wurzels back in 1974. His tenure with the band has witnessed numerous tours and performances, but this particular incident stands out due to the adverse weather conditions, which are becoming a growing concern for outdoor events. As temperatures soared, medical personnel were quick to assist both Budd and Banner, providing necessary care and advice, including exercises for Banner and a recommendation not to drive.nnWith seasoned careers and a dedicated fan base, the incident underscores the challenges aging performers face in unpredictable weather. Adjusting to such extreme conditions is essential to ensure the safety and well-being of artists and attendees alike. As the Wurzels recover, their health remains a priority, echoing the sentiments of their supporters who eagerly await their next performance.
