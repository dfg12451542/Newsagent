Gower: Fire Causes Major Damage to Landmark HotelnnA significant fire engulfed the Worm's Head Hotel in Rhossili, Gower, in the early hours of Monday morning, leaving the cherished establishment heavily damaged. The Mid and West Wales Fire and Rescue Service responded swiftly to the call at 00:51 BST and worked diligently to extinguish the fire, which was brought under control by 05:30. Despite the extensive damage, officials confirmed that there were no casualties and all occupants were safely accounted for.nnThe blaze, which broke out in the property's accommodation block, caused severe damage to the hotel, including collapsing a 25-meter span of roof. Blackened beams and an exposed chimney stack now mark the iconic hotel's skyline, as it waits for a resolution. South Wales Police also attended the scene alongside fire services. The cause of the fire remains unknown, with investigations ongoing.nnLocal sentiment reflects the deep sense of loss felt by the community. In a heartfelt social media post, the owners expressed their devastation: 'It is particularly heartbreaking and soul-destroying to see 25 years of hard work destroyed in less than 30 minutes.' Despite this, they remain resolute: 'Not sure how we come back from this at all let alone in the near-term future, but we will strive to come back stronger.'nnRegular visitors to the Worm's Head Hotel shared in the shock and sadness. 'We used to come every summer. It's absolutely shocking to see it like this,' remarked Sian Nichol. Beverly, another frequent guest, added, 'We've spent many a fantastic new year here, so to see it like this, I felt quite emotional when we turned the corner.' These sentiments highlight the hotel's role as a beloved community hub and destination over the years.nnThe hotel management reassured the public that 'everyone is safe' and expressed gratitude for the supportive messages received. As they assess the damage and determine the next steps forward, the community stands by in hope, eager to see the Worm's Head Hotel restored to its former glory.
