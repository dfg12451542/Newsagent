West Belfast's St. John's GAA club was engulfed in flames on Saturday evening, a fire that police have confirmed was deliberately set. The Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI) reports that the incident occurred around 18:20 BST, causing substantial damage to the community's beloved sports facility. Inspector Dalton from PSNI highlighted the destructive impact of the fire, describing it as 'absolutely unacceptable' and an attack on a vital community hub.nnThe ramifications of the fire are far-reaching. Local Belfast city councillor Arder Carson condemned the act as a 'blatant act of vandalism' and expressed the frustration felt by the community. 'It definitely fills you with despair and some anger,' Carson stated, emphasizing the pivotal role the club plays in nurturing young talent and providing essential services. With the club preparing for its children's summer camps and a new competitive season, the timing of the fire is a significant setback.nnThe Northern Ireland Fire and Rescue Service responded to the blaze at the club's grounds in Hannahstown shortly after its igniting. Images from the scene show burnt debris across the grass with the charred remains of what was once a vibrant sporting venue. This act of recklessness, occurring amid a period of prolonged dry weather, endangers not only the immediate area but also emphasizes the broader risk these fires pose in rural green areas.nnAuthorities are urging those with information about the fire to come forward as they continue their investigation. Inspector Dalton and Councillor Carson both appealed to the public’s sense of community to aid in identifying the individuals responsible. This deliberate attack not only disrupts the ongoing activities at the GAA club but also resonates deeply with the shared spirit and resilience of the West Belfast community.
