Shubhanshu Shukla, the first Indian astronaut to step foot on the International Space Station (ISS), is on the journey back to Earth after a notable mission. History was made as the Axiom-4 mission (Ax-4), consisting of a four-member crew, including Group Captain Shukla, undocked from the ISS and is en route to splash down off the Californian coast within the next 24 hours. The successful completion of his mission signifies a remarkable milestone in India's burgeoning space exploration endeavors.nnThis mission is particularly significant as it positions Group Captain Shukla as only the second Indian to venture into space, following in the historic footsteps of Rakesh Sharma, who voyaged into the cosmos aboard a Russian Soyuz 41 years ago, in 1984. The mission was not just a symbolic win but was packed with scientific inquiry, with the team conducting 60 experiments, including seven developed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). A representative from Axiom Space confirmed that the transition from the ISS was broadcasted live and preluded its scheduled splashdown on July 15 at approximately 15:00 India time (09:30 GMT).nnGroup Captain Shukla expressed his reflections on this pioneering journey, capturing the spirit of contemporary India. 'Even today we want to know how it looks from space,' he stated. 'From space, today's India looks ambitious. It looks fearless. It looks confident. It looks proud.' He further emphasized the long and arduous path of India's nascent human space missions, affirming that with determination, even the heavens are not out of reach. Before his journey, he eloquently articulated the collective aspirations he carried into space, urging his countrymen to support the mission with their hopes and prayers.nnThe Ax-4 mission, initially expected to last two weeks on board the ISS, was extended by a few days, thus allowing extensive engagement in scientific research. Sponsored by ISRO with a significant investment of 5 billion rupees (approximately $59 million USD), Shukla's mission is anticipated to return invaluable insights, which are likely to bolster India's forthcoming human spaceflight initiatives. This mission marks a milestone for ISRO, as its direct involvement in international space exploration signifies an evolution in India's space narrative from ambition to active participant on the global stage.
