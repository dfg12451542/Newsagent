South Elmsall's waste recycling site operator, recently stripped of its license over health hazard concerns, is contesting the decision, spotlighting tensions between environmental regulation and industry operations. Minore, also known as Mineral Processing Ltd, faces accusations of creating significant health risks due to the storage of large quantities of hazardous materials. The controversy extends back years, as residents of East Lancashire have long complained about the site's noxious odors, which have intensified as the site expanded into residential areas.nnCarly Chambers, area manager for the Environment Agency (EA) in Yorkshire, confirmed Minore has lodged an appeal against the license revocation. Despite the ongoing appeal, Chambers emphasized that a suspension notice remains in place, restricting further waste intake and mandating the removal of existing waste violating permit conditions. 'If the operator does not comply with the suspension notice, it will be committing an offence,' Chambers stressed, highlighting the EA's firm stance on regulatory compliance.nnA spokesperson for Minore responded, affirming their current operations—material excavation and processing—are consistent with EA directives. They assured that water quality monitoring has consistently shown no adverse effects on nearby Frickley Beck, hoping to quell public health fears. 'Our employees have been working at the site for over 12 years and now fear for their jobs due to this escalation,' the spokesperson added, reflecting growing concerns over job security amidst regulatory turmoil.nnThe site, operational since the 1970s, has transformed from a basic household waste collector to a contentious neighbor, drawing scrutiny from both the Planning Inspectorate and local communities. The latest appeal, echoing a previous unresolved appeal from 2020, underscores the ongoing struggle to balance environmental safety with industrial activity. As the Planning Inspectorate continues to deliberate, the environmental permit remains active, marking a critical juncture in a saga that has stirred both local concern and national regulatory debate.
