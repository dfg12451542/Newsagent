Thames Water has announced a hosepipe ban affecting 1.1 million residents across Gloucestershire, Oxfordshire, Berkshire, and Wiltshire. The initiative, which will commence on July 22, is a response to a prolonged dry spell coupled with a surge in water demand, leading to alarming pressure on the water supply. Residents will be prohibited from using hoses for activities such as watering gardens, washing cars, and filling paddling pools. Those found non-compliant with these regulations may face fines of up to £1,000.nnNevil Muncaster, the strategic water resources director at Thames Water, emphasized the urgency of the situation: 'We have to take action now,' he stated, referencing the challenging spring and summer characterized by high customer demand and insufficient rainfall. Despite efforts to encourage water conservation, Muncaster remains pessimistic about improvement in weather patterns and the water situation, urging all customers to adopt water-saving habits.nnThis announcement comes on the heels of England experiencing its warmest June on record, as well as the driest and sunniest spring in over a century. Gary Carter, representing the GMB union, criticized the timing of the ban amidst rising water bills, calling it 'disgraceful.' He highlighted the significant water wastage of 570 million liters per day, attributing it to outdated infrastructure and lack of investment. In defense, Thames Water stated it is already addressing leakage issues, undertaking repairs of 650 leaks weekly using advanced technologies like acoustic loggers and smart meters to accelerate detection and rectification.nnWhile businesses that require hosepipe usage for their operations, such as garden centers and car washes, are exempt from the ban, Thames Water has called for heightened awareness and reduced water usage from all residents in the affected regions. The water company acknowledges the efforts made in managing water loss but assures customers of its continued commitment to reducing leakages and optimizing resource management.
