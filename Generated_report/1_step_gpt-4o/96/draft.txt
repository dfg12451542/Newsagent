Great Britain's Oliver Rowland celebrated a monumental achievement on Sunday, securing the Formula E title for the first time with a strategic fourth-place finish in Berlin, Germany. This win comes after a remarkable season, marked by victories in Mexico, Jeddah, Monaco, and Tokyo, showcasing Rowland's consistent dominance on the track.nnThe Nissan driver clinched the championship with two races still to go in the season, becoming the 10th different champion in the sport's 11-season history. 'Honestly, I was just thinking before the race not to have too much damage going into [the final round in] London,' Rowland remarked, reflecting on his cautious strategy. Yet, the aggressive field pushed him to opt for an all-in approach, as he noted, 'I can't believe it, I have no words. It's incredible. I was trying to play it safe but everybody was so aggressive, so at some point I was all in.'nnRowland's closest competitor, Porsche's Pascal Wehrlein, faced a challenging race, sliding to 16th despite starting from pole position at Berlin's old Tempelhof Airport. This left Rowland comfortably leading with 184 points, compared to Wehrlein's 125, going into the final two races in London later this month. 'Thankfully, I stayed out of trouble,' Rowland added with relief as he reflected on the race.nnOn Sunday's podium, Jaguar's Nick Cassidy emerged victorious, with Jake Dennis from Andretti finishing second and DS Penske's Jean-Eric Vergne claiming third. The results highlight the competitive nature of the season and Rowland's exceptional drive towards securing the prestigious title. All eyes will now be on London, where Rowland will take to the track with an unassailable lead, a testament to his skill and perseverance throughout the season.
