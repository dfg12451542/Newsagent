Wales' New Sustainable Farming Scheme Draws Praise and Criticism Ahead of LaunchnnIn a move set to reshape Wales' agricultural landscape, the Welsh government is on the brink of unveiling a new iteration of its Sustainable Farming Scheme (SFS). Huw Irranca-Davies, Wales' Deputy First Minister and Secretary for Rural Affairs, anticipates a positive reception for the flagship policy, though he acknowledges some dissenters. 'Speaking ahead of its launch, Huw Irranca-Davies said he was 'hopeful it will land to a good reception', even though he conceded 'there will be the odd person' who wished it had been done differently,' highlighting the inclusive nature of consultations with various farming and environmental stakeholders over the past year.nnThe announcement comes after the original scheme faced pushback from farming unions, leading to protests staged at Cardiff Bay. Acknowledging the need for refinement, Irranca-Davies emphasized the comprehensive nature of the project, describing it as 'the result of more than 12 months of intense work' to balance agricultural productivity with environmental stewardship. 'Although he would not give any detail about the scheme, he told BBC Politics Wales it was 'going to be unlike anything else that's been delivered in the UK'.'nnHistorically, Welsh farmers have been integral to managing the nation's landscape, with 80% of it under their care. This pivotal role underscores the importance of their involvement in efforts to combat climate change and degradation of ecosystems. The scheme's proposed reforms aim for sustainable practices capable of delivering for both the farming communities and the Welsh environment by 2030. However, concerns exist over the economic impact, as a consideration raised in discussions highlighted that 'it's going to cause problems and... make your plants in Wales less competitive because it's going to be harder to bring animals into Wales.'nnPolitical voices add another layer to the conversation. While Plaid Cymru's Delyth Jewell MS insists on listening to the farming community's needs, Conservative Senedd leader Darren Millar MS insists the scheme should undergo a vote for 'democratic legitimacy' and stresses that 'the new scheme has food security at its heart.' As the launch date approaches, stakeholders await further details that will determine the future trajectory of Welsh agriculture and its alignment with environmental goals.
