Whitby's memorial bench dedicated to Sophie Lancaster, a victim of a horrific 2007 hate crime, has been temporarily relocated to make space for a giant sightseeing wheel. The decision to move the bench, situated on West Cliff, has been met with shock and disappointment from Sophie Lancaster Foundation representatives. 'We were shocked to find the bench had been temporarily relocated without consultation,' stated a spokesperson for the charity.nnNorth Yorkshire Council, responsible for the move, assured that the memorial would be restored to its original site on 4 August. 'The big wheel was intended as a respectful, exciting addition to the town,' explained a council spokesperson. The wheel's placement is part of a broader tourism strategy aimed at offering 'stunning views across the harbor and the town's famed abbey,' North Yorkshire councillor Mark Crane elaborated. Despite the oversight, initial feedback from local businesses and the council's commitment to discussing local engagement at the next town meeting reflect a positive outlook on the project's impact.nnThe bench serves as a poignant reminder of Lancaster's tragic death in 2007, when she and her boyfriend were attacked by two teenagers in Bacup, Lancashire, due to their appearance. The incident aroused national outcry and spurred significant efforts towards promoting tolerance and combating hate crimes. Sophie’s mother, Sylvia Lancaster, established the charity in her memory, and for many, the bench is a place to reflect and remember.nnWhitby's mayor, councillor Sandra Turner, acknowledged the situation as 'a serious oversight,' noting apologies from the council and commitments to prevent future occurrence. The charity plans to hold a rededication ceremony at the Tomorrow's Ghosts Festival on 1 November. 'Whitby is such a special place for us, and we will be overjoyed when the bench returns to its home on West Cliff,' the foundation spokesperson conveyed, expressing gratitude for the community's support. The temporary relocation, though challenging, underscores the need for sensitive community engagement in town planning decisions.
