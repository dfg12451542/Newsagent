Thames Water has announced a hosepipe ban affecting 1.1 million people across Gloucestershire, Oxfordshire, Berkshire, and Wiltshire, starting July 22. The ban, caused by a lack of rain and increased demand, restricts activities such as watering gardens, washing cars, and filling paddling pools. 'We have to take action now,' said Nevil Muncaster, strategic water resources director at Thames Water. 'This has been a challenging spring and summer with big spikes in customer demand during hot dry days and very little rainfall to replenish local supplies in the Thames Valley.' The ban comes with a fine of up to £1,000 for those found using a hosepipe. Thames Water aims to reduce water waste, with 570 million liters wasted daily, and is fixing 650 leaks a week. A spokesperson stated, 'We're fixing 650 leaks a week with our engineers targeting leaks with the greatest impact to local water supplies.' Despite efforts to reduce leaks, some critics argue the ban is 'disgraceful' given rising bills.
