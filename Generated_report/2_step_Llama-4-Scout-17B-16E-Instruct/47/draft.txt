The operator of a waste recycling site in South Elmsall, which was stripped of its licence over concerns the facility was causing a health hazard, has appealed against the decision. Minore, also known as Mineral Processing Ltd, had its environmental permit suspended due to concerns over its impact on the community.     Carly Chambers, area manager for the Environment Agency (EA) in Yorkshire, confirmed that the company had launched an appeal. As a result, the environmental permit will now stay in place until the Planning Inspectorate has announced the outcome of this appeal. However, the suspension notice remains in place, preventing more waste from being brought onto the site and requiring the removal of waste that breaches permit conditions.    The EA has emphasized that if the operator does not comply with the suspension notice, it will be committing an offence. Chambers added that the EA understands the impact this site is having on the community and that their increased regulatory response continues.     A spokesperson for Minore stated that the site is currently excavating materials and processing them in compliance with the notices issued by the EA. The company regularly samples the water and has found no effect on Frickley Beck when taking water samples. However, the spokesperson also expressed concern that the escalation of the situation has put the jobs of employees, who have been working at the site for over 12 years, at risk.
