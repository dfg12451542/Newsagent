The Sudanese paramilitary group Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has stormed the besieged city of el-Fasher, capturing a cattle market, a prison, and a military base after a seven-hour battle. The city has been under siege for 15 months, with residents describing the situation as a 'death trap'. Mathilde Vu, from the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC), said the city is witnessing 'stories of horror and terror' with weekly shelling and attacks on civilian infrastructure. 'There are local volunteers - they are really struggling, risking their lives every day to try and provide a little bit of food for people who are mostly starving,' she added. The RSF entered the city on Friday from the south and south-west, with residents reporting that one of the shells hit a civilian vehicle near their houses, resulting in the death of five civilians. The army retaliated on Saturday morning, pushing the RSF back beyond the city limits and inflicting 'heavy losses'. However, residents reported that RSF shelling continued throughout Saturday. The situation in el-Fasher is dire, with residents struggling to access basic necessities like food, medicine, and water. 'Right now, we are suffering deeply, and everyone around us is facing the same hardship,' said a resident. 'There is no bread, no food, and no work to be found. Even if you have money, there's nothing available in the markets to buy.' The city's hospitals are also facing a severe shortage of medicines, with one resident stating that 'there are no medicines in hospitals. The situation here is truly terrible.' The crisis has led to a massive displacement of people, with nearly 379,000 fleeing to Tawila, where they are facing an outbreak of cholera and expected heavy rain that is likely to destroy makeshift shelters. Mathilde Vu warned that funding for aid is decreasing, leaving people in el-Fasher relying on the solidarity of others for survival. 'If they have a little bit of food, they will be sharing it among themselves,' she said. The international community has been urged to provide assistance to those affected by the conflict.
