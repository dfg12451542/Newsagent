Liverpool paid an emotional tribute to forward Diogo Jota in their first match since his death, with fans and players alike showing their respects in a heartfelt display of grief and admiration. The match, a friendly against Preston, was played at Deepdale, where thousands of Liverpool fans had traveled to not only watch their team undertake their first run-out of the new campaign but also to pay tribute to Jota and his family. As the players walked out, the away end, filled with flags and scarves commemorating the former Porto and Wolves attacker, loudly sang Jota's song, followed by an impeccably observed minute's silence. During the 20th minute, the crowd burst into another rendition of Jota's song, with Preston supporters joining in, standing and clapping along in rhythm. The Liverpool players also paid their tributes, with Conor Bradley opening the scoring and looking up to the heavens as he walked back to his position. Striker Darwin Nunez scored the Reds' second and did two of Jota's celebrations in front of the Liverpool fans, while Cody Gakpo held his fingers up to signal the number 20, Jota's squad number at Liverpool.Liverpool head coach Arne Slot, in his first interview since Jota's death, spoke about the emotional impact on the team. 'If we want to laugh, we laugh; if we want to cry, we're going to cry. If they want to train they can train, if they don't want to train they can not train. But be yourself, don't think you have to be different than your emotions tell you,' he said. 'We will always carry him with us in our hearts, in our thoughts, wherever we go.' Fans also expressed their emotional connection to Jota, with one fan, Jane, wearing a custom T-shirt dedicated to him, saying, 'I feel very emotional. I feel very proud of my team and all the tributes they've put together.' Another fan, Mathew, shared the difficulty his seven-year-old son faced in understanding the news, while his 15-year-old son Rafael added, 'I feel gutted. But Liverpool is a family and we've all come together to pay our respects in the right way.'The match programme featured a black and white picture of Jota holding the Premier League trophy and included tributes to the Liverpool player and his brother. Liverpool have retired Jota's number 20 shirt across all areas of the club, making the announcement at 20:20 BST on Friday. The team's actions and the fans' tributes showed an immense depth of feeling for a footballer adored by followers of his club, making the match a poignant and emotional event.
