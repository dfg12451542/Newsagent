The 2025 Wimbledon Championships have concluded, with Iga Swiatek and Jannik Sinner emerging as the winners of the Ladies' and Gentlemen's Singles titles, respectively. On Saturday, Poland's Iga Swiatek dominated USA's Amanda Anisimova in the Ladies' Singles final, claiming her first Wimbledon title with a decisive 6-0 6-0 victory in just 57 minutes. Despite being a first-time finalist for both players, Swiatek took control of the match quickly. 'It seems super surreal,' said the 24-year-old Iga. 'Honestly, I didn't even dream of winning Wimbledon because it was way too far. I feel like I'm an experienced player, having won other Slams, but I didn't expect this.'In the Gentlemen's Singles final, Jannik Sinner emerged victorious after a nail-biting match. Keep going, keep pushing, you are going to hold this trophy many times - you already have twice!'The Wheelchair Singles title was won by 19-year-old Tokito Oda, who defeated Great Britain's Alfie Hewett. Despite losing the opening set, Oda went on to win the next two sets, claiming victory. Alfie shared his gratitude for the support he received during the match, saying: 'When you lose, to have this amount of love and support, this sort of atmosphere doesn't happen to us on a regular basis. It is really incredible the amount of support wheelchair tennis has got.'Other notable winners include Britain's Julian Cash and Lloyd Glasspool, who won the Men's Doubles title; Russia's Veronika Kudermetova and Belgium's Elise Mertens, who won the Women's Doubles title; and The Netherlands' Sem Verbeek and Chechia's Katerina Sin, who won the Mixed Doubles title. The Wheelchair Doubles title was won by Martin de la Puente and Ruben Spaargaren, who defeated reigning British champions Alfie Hewett and Gordon Reid.
