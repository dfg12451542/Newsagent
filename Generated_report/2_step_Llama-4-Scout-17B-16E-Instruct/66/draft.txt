A historic leper hospital in Warwick is set to be transformed into affordable homes after securing £2.8m in funding. The St Michael's Place project, a collaboration between West Midlands Heritage and Warwick District Council, aims to 'rescue and reuse' the Master’s House and St Michael’s Chapel in Saltisford, Warwick. The 15th-century buildings, which have been derelict for over 50 years, will be restored and converted into homes.The site, which features two Grade II* listed buildings, has a rich history dating back 800 years as a leper hospital. Councillor Helen Adkins said, 'We will be able to preserve the legacy of the site and rescue these long-neglected historic buildings, offering them a sustainable future.' The project also includes the creation of a heritage community assistant role, opportunities to learn about heritage craft skills, and a series of local community events and activities.Corinne Brazier, chair of West Midlands Heritage, credited the work of her colleagues and support from the local community for reaching an 'important delivery stage.' She added, 'We are now able to ensure that St Michael's Place will have a very homely future.' The funding, awarded by the National Lottery Heritage Fund, will not only restore the buildings but also provide a valuable community resource. The transformation of St Michael's Place is a significant step towards preserving Warwick's historical heritage while providing much-needed affordable housing. The project is expected to breathe new life into the site, giving it a 'homely future' while maintaining its historical significance.
