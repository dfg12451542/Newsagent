Proposed parking changes in Ilkley have sparked controversy, with critics likening the plans to the work of Ebenezer Scrooge. Ilkley Conservative councillor Andrew Loy told a Bradford Council meeting that the changes could see people forced to pay to park outside church on Christmas Day, describing the policy as 'written by Ebenezer Scrooge.' The plans, which aim to standardize parking charges across the district, have been met with opposition from local residents and business owners. Richard Downing, a local resident who presented a petition against the changes, said that the removal of free parking would be 'immensely damaging' for local businesses, making it inconvenient for people to shop locally and visit doctors and post offices. 'It helps people shop locally and make visits to doctors and post offices,' he said. 'Having to pay to visit shops for a few items will make it inconvenient to shop locally – footfall will be down and people will be drawn to retail parks.' Councillor Alex Ross Shaw acknowledged the strength of feeling against the proposals, stating that the council recognizes the number of responses to the petition. However, he also emphasized the need to manage the budget and standardize parking charges across the district. The petition has been voted to go to a future meeting of the council's executive for further discussion.
