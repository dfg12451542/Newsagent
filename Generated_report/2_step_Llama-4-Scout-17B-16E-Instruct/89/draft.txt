South African President Cyril Ramaphosa has suspended Police Minister Senzo Mchunu from his duties amid allegations of links to organized crime groups. The allegations, which include interference in investigations into political killings and corruption within law enforcement agencies, have sparked a comprehensive investigation.In a live televised speech to the nation on Sunday, Ramaphosa announced Mchunu's immediate 'leave of absence' and the establishment of a judicial commission to probe the claims. The commission, led by the country's deputy chief justice, will examine all allegations against Mchunu, as well as current and former police officials and members of the national executive.Mchunu has denied any wrongdoing, stating that he 'stood ready to respond to the accusations' against him. In a statement, he welcomed the president's decision and pledged his commitment to the investigation, emphasizing his commitment to upholding 'honour and integrity.' The allegations against Mchunu were first made public by KwaZulu-Natal provincial police boss Gen Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi. He claimed that Mchunu was receiving financial support from a controversial businessman, Vusimuzi Matlala, to fund his 'political endeavours.' Mkhwanazi also alleged that Mchunu had ties to a drug cartel and had ordered the disbandment of a task force investigating high-profile killings and corruption cases.Ramaphosa has appointed law professor Firoz Cachalia as interim police minister. The president emphasized that the allegations against Mchunu 'undermined the constitution and threatened national security,' necessitating an urgent and comprehensive investigation.
