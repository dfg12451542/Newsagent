The Memories of Cirencester's Old Station are being sought for a history festival. The Cirencester History Festival, taking place from 25 October to 1 November, is calling for people to share their memories of the town's former railway station, which will be temporarily reopened during the event. The festival, which offers talks and activities on local and global history, will feature a free event on both Saturdays, celebrating the 'golden age of rail.' Festival producer Jess Yarrow said, 'We know that there are lots of people out there who can remember the station in its heyday. We'd love to hear stories, we'd love to see photographs, we'd love to have any memorabilia that people have got tucked away.'The Old Station, designed by Isambard Kingdom Brunel and closed in 1965, is a Grade-II listed building that most people know as 'a slightly sad, empty-looking building.' However, Yarrow hopes that some people will remember using it for their commute or watching it welcome evacuees and soldiers during World War Two. Architect Jonathan Rixon, who is refurbishing the station to use as his offices and will lead the tours, said, 'The building holds many hidden treasures, including a boardroom where Brunel sought a meeting space outside London. It's essential to protect our industrial heritage. I'm passionate about preserving this piece of history and sharing it with the town.'During the festival, people will be able to tour the Old Station, and there will also be an audio listening project sharing stories of the station. Additionally, Cirencester College students have worked with Rixon to create a virtual reality experience of the station during the Victoria era. The goal is to 'dress the station as if it were a station with prams and benches and cases,' and the organizers are eager to hear from anyone with memories or memorabilia of the Old Station.
