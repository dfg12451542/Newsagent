South Korea's medical students end 17-month boycott of classes. Thousands of South Korean medical students are set to return to classes after a 17-month long boycott, the Korean Medical Association has announced. The decision comes as a significant development in the country's healthcare sector, which has been under strain due to ongoing disputes over medical education and training conditions.The boycott, which began in 2023, was sparked by disagreements over the government's plan to raise the annual enrollment quota at medical schools. The Korean Medical Association had urged the government to restore the academic calendar and improve training conditions. While no timeline for the students' return has been provided, the group has committed to returning to school to help normalize medical education and the healthcare system.Prime Minister Kim Min-Seok welcomed the end of the boycott, describing it as a 'big step forward.' He also emphasized the need for a deeper examination of the medical field, the Congress, and the government to address the underlying issues. 'It's time to take a deeper look at the medical field, the Congress, and the government, so that citizens can help solve problems,' he said.The decision to end the boycott comes with a consequence, however. According to the education ministry, 8,305 students will be subject to grade retention, requiring them to repeat the same academic year. The Korean Medical Association has placed its trust in the government and parliament, vowing to work together to improve the medical education system. With this development, the healthcare sector in South Korea is expected to see a gradual return to normalcy.
