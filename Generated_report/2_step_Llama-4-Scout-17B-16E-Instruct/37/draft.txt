This week in Wearside, several stories emerged that may have flown under the radar. One of the most significant was the ongoing fallout from the Post Office's Horizon IT scandal. Betty Brown, a victim of the scandal, spoke out about the devastating impact it had on her health, stating that it 'destroyed' her. This comes as the first part of the official inquiry report was published, which you can read here. In other news, a dispute between two sculptors has been making headlines. Ray Lonsdale from South Hetton, County Durham, claimed that his work was stolen by New Zealand sculptor James Wright. Ahead of a legal challenge to protect his livelihood, Lonsdale raised thousands of pounds. Mr. Wright has since come forward to assert that his artwork was independently researched and that there was 'no copyright infringement.'The Horizon IT scandal has been a long-standing issue, with a report regarding the scandal expected to be released on Tuesday. This report is highly anticipated, as it is expected to shed light on the extent of the scandal and the damage it has caused. In a separate event, students graduating from Durham University recently gathered at a ceremony at Durham Cathedral, where they were addressed by Mark Gatiss, who was dressed in gold and red robes.
