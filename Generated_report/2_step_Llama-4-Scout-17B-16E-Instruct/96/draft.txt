Great Britain's Oliver Rowland has secured the Formula E title for the first time after a fourth-place finish in Berlin, Germany. The Nissan driver won the championship with two races to spare and becomes the 10th different champion in 11 seasons. Rowland has had a remarkable season, securing wins in Mexico, Jeddah, Monaco, and Tokyo, culminating in a strong performance in Germany on Sunday. 'Honestly, I was just thinking before the race not to have too much damage going into [the final round in] London,' said Rowland. Rowland's closest title rival, Porsche's Pascal Wehrlein, struggled to 16th place in Sunday's race despite starting on pole at Berlin's old Tempelhof airport. This result sees Rowland with 184 points going into the final two races of the season in London (26-27 July), while Wehrlein sits on 125. When asked about his feelings on securing the title, Rowland expressed his elation: 'I can't believe it, I have no words. It's incredible. I was trying to play it safe but everybody was so aggressive, so at some point I was all in.' He added that he was fortunate to stay out of trouble, saying 'Thankfully I stayed out of trouble.' Sunday's race was won by Jaguar's Nick Cassidy, with Jake Dennis (Andretti) finishing second and Jean-Eric Vergne (DS Penske) coming home in third. With this title win, Rowland cements his place in the history of Formula E as a champion.
