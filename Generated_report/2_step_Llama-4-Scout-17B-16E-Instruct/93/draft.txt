This week in Tyneside, several stories have emerged that may have flown under the radar. A permanent memorial to the Sycamore Gap tree, which was illegally cut down two years ago, has been unveiled at the Sill National Landscape Discovery Centre in Northumberland. The memorial allows visitors to touch part of the tree's trunk, evoking strong emotions in those who encounter it. Artist Charlie Winney, who has seen the memorial, said he had been 'blown away by how huggable it is.' The Sycamore Gap tree was a beloved landmark, and its destruction sparked widespread outrage. The decision to create a permanent exhibition of a part of the tree aims to provide a lasting tribute to the tree's significance. In other news, issues with the new Tyne and Wear Metro trains continue to persist. Three of the trains were pulled from service due to air conditioning leaks, which caused water to pour into the carriages. This is not the first time these new trains have faced problems, and efforts are being made to rectify the faults.On a more positive note, TV historian David Olusoga will be among those receiving an honorary degree from Newcastle University this summer. This recognition highlights his contributions to the field of history and his impact on the academic community.These stories showcase the mix of challenges and celebrations in Tyneside, from the preservation of memories to the pursuit of excellence.
