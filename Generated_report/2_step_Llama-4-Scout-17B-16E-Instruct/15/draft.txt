England vs India: Brydon Carse keeps England alive on fourth day at Lord'sEngland's chances of a dramatic comeback in the Test match against India received a significant boost on the fourth day at Lord's, thanks to a stellar performance by Brydon Carse. The hosts were on the brink of defeat, but Carse's crucial wickets kept them alive in the contest. Carse's intervention was pivotal as he bowled Karun Nair and India captain Shubman Gill leg before, giving England a glimmer of hope. Earlier, India had made significant inroads into England's batting lineup, with both past and present captains, Ben Stokes and Joe Root, falling to Washington Sundar. England's batting had buckled under pressure, with the team struggling to post a competitive total.England batting coach Marcus Trescothick acknowledged that while they would have loved to have scored 250 and beyond, they were content with 190 and the opportunity to bowl on a challenging pitch. 'We would have loved 250 and beyond but we would have taken 190 and our chance to bowl on that pitch and it'll come down to who holds the pressure best,' he said.India all-rounder Washington Sundar expressed confidence in his team's ability to win the game, despite being only four wickets away from victory. 'We'd have loved to have about nine wickets [in hand]. It is what it is and we'll definitely win the game,' he said. The match is set for a thrilling conclusion on Monday morning, with England needing to bowl out India to stay alive in the series.The fourth day's play was marked by high drama and tension, with the pitch becoming increasingly difficult to bat on. England's fielders revved up the crowd, and the home team's chances of a comeback were boosted by Carse's impressive bowling. The match has been described as 'absolutely gripping' by BBC chief cricket commentator Jonathan Agnew, who added that it was 'a bit like a World Cup final.'
