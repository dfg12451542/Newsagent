A Salisbury woman has completed a remarkable 200-mile walking challenge on her 100th birthday, raising over £11,000 for a local homeless charity. Phyll Babb, from Salisbury in Wiltshire, reached the finish line on Sunday, her 100th birthday, after doubling her initial target of 100 miles in January. She had been tracking her progress on Strava, and a local accountant, Gordon Pardy, helped her log her walks.Ms Babb said she 'had a ball' completing the challenge, adding that 'walking is good for you' and that she was 'brought up to walk.' She expressed her gratitude to those who supported her, saying 'thank you all very, very much' for their generosity. The funds raised will go towards furnishing a new house that Ms Babb's family recently purchased.Ms Babb's achievement has inspired her family and supporters, with her great-niece Ruth Simmons describing it as 'really special' to see her complete her 200th lap around Salisbury Cathedral. Family friend Liz Huntley said Ms Babb was an inspiration, saying 'she is inspirational, definitely. She's been inspirational my whole life, to be honest.' David Evans, another supporter, praised Ms Babb's fitness and articulation at her age, saying 'to reach the age of 100 and to do it so fit, looking so well, being so articulate, that's the really amazing thing she's done.'The charity, Salisbury Trust for the Homeless (STFH), will benefit from Ms Babb's achievement, with the funds raised helping to support their vital work. Ms Babb's remarkable feat is a testament to her determination and spirit, and an inspiration to people of all ages.
