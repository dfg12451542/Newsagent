Hundreds of fish have died in a pond at Rossmere Park in Hartlepool due to rising water temperatures. The crisis unfolded after residents spotted hundreds of fish floating on the surface of the pond. Volunteers have since pulled 173 lbs (78 kg) of dead fish out of the water. According to Labour MP Jonathan Brash, the initial assessment suggests that the recent hot weather reduced oxygen levels and increased ammonia in the pond, creating a dangerous mix for the fish. The council and Environment Agency teams are working to mitigate the damage. 'They're currently aerating the water to boost oxygen levels and are testing the water quality,' Brash said. An Environment Agency spokesperson previously noted that prolonged dry weather, combined with reduced or stagnant water flow and increased water temperatures, had led to a significant drop in dissolved oxygen levels, resulting in fish mortality. However, Brash offered some reassurance, stating that 'the good news is there's still a living fish population in the pond, and we've now moved into the recovery phase.' In response to the incident, three local men have committed to volunteering long-term to help maintain the pond after removing the dead fish. Hartlepool Borough Councillor Quewone Bailey-Fleet expressed gratitude for their efforts. The council and Environment Agency teams continue to monitor the situation, with MP Brash meeting with them at the pond to assess the damage and plan for recovery.
