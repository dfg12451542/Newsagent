As the world marks 40 years since the iconic Live Aid concert, the daughter of a behind-the-scenes hero has spoken about her father's unsung contribution to the event. Maurice Jones, from Wednesbury, was approached to help with Live Aid due to his industry connections. His daughter Nicola revealed that he played a crucial role in organizing the venue, backstage catering, and booking the bands. 'He was very modest. His take on it was that he was there to do a job, he wasn't there to be in the limelight. He did play an important part,' Nicola told BBC WM. Nicola, who attended the Wembley concert with her father at the age of 15, recalled the excitement of the event. She sat in the royal box, a row behind David Bowie and two rows behind Princess Diana and Prince Charles. She also saw stars including Paul McCartney, Elton John, and Adam Ant backstage, and witnessed the crowd's electrifying reaction to Freddie Mercury's legendary performance. 'Watching the reaction of the audience was really spectacular,' she added.Nicola's father, who passed away in 2009, was a modest man who didn't seek to promote himself despite his expertise in promoting bands and acts. 'I think, because of my dad's nature, he didn't want to promote himself even though he was very good at promoting bands and acts,' Nicola said. Now, she wants to ensure her father's contribution to Live Aid is remembered. 'It will be nice if he is remembered in a small way,' she said, adding that she is 'very proud' of his work on the concert.The Live Aid dual-venue benefit concert was held on July 13, 1985, to raise funds for famine relief in Ethiopia. The event, which was watched by an estimated global audience of 1.5 billion people, featured some of the biggest names in music, including Queen, U2, and David Bowie. As the world celebrates 40 years since Live Aid, Nicola's story shines a light on the often-overlooked heroes who helped make the concert a success.
