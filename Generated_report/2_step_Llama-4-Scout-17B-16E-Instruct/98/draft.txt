Swansea hosted the UK's first ever IRONMAN Pro Series edition, with thousands of athletes and supporters lining the 70-mile course. The event saw defending champion Harry Palmer claim back-to-back victories in Swansea, taking the overall Pro Series win in a time of 3:51:18. Palmer, who had a challenging swim, was in the second or third pack back but managed to make his way up during the bike ride. 'It was hot, and I do feel for all the age-groupers who are out there today, because they've got much harder conditions than we did,' Palmer told BBC Sport Wales.The race was held in extreme weather conditions, with air temperatures soaring above 28°C and water temperatures exceeding 24°C. As a result, race organisers ruled the professional swim non-wetsuit, as it surpassed the wetsuit-legal limit. This marked the first time elite athletes in the Ironman Pro Series have raced under such conditions in the UK. Palmer's training partner, France's Leon Chevalier, finished just 49 seconds behind, while fellow Brit Kieran Lindars claimed third place.In the female professional race, overall Ironman Pro Series 2024 winner Kat Matthews claimed victory in Swansea with a time of 4:20:37. Matthews, who last won in Swansea in 2022, felt like a 'completely different person' this time around. 'Three years feels like a lifetime. A lot has happened since then,' Matthews said. 'I didn't have as much deficit coming out of the swim as I expected, so I was really motivated to attack at the start of the bike and catch the girls at the front.' Britain's Rebecca Anderbury and Danila Kleiser of Germany completed the podium, finishing second and third respectively.The event was well-supported by thousands of spectators and volunteers, who provided encouragement and welfare support throughout the day. 'The course is amazing, especially with weather like this, and the Gower is incredible,' Palmer said. 'But it's the people — it's the Welsh. They just know how to put on an event and how to support. I'll keep coming back to Welsh events because of that.' The race is set to return to the coastal city next summer for the fourth edition of the event. Race Director Rebecca Sutherland expressed her satisfaction with the event, saying, 'It's always great fun when we go racing in Swansea, and this year was no different. It was another incredible race with thousands of athletes crossing the famous red carpet finish line to rapturous spectator support.'
