Trust in police in the Peterborough area has shown significant improvement thanks to the Alliance project, a community-led initiative aimed at combating organised crime. The project, established in 2023 in the Millfield and Lincoln Road area as part of the Home Office's Clear, Hold and Build strategy, has led to increased confidence in policing and a notable surge in community intelligence reporting. According to Peterborough City Council, there has been a 700% increase in community intelligence reporting since the project's inception.The council has reported several successes attributed to the Alliance project, including the seizure of 700,000 illegal cigarettes, 20,000 vapes, and £115,000 in cash. Additionally, new drug needle bins have been installed in the area, further demonstrating the project's commitment to improving community safety. However, despite these achievements, the council's report to a scrutiny committee revealed that the project struggled to make significant connections with local groups.Insp Sam Tucker from Cambridgeshire Police acknowledged the project's potential, stating that while some concepts could be adopted, replicating the initiative in its entirety was unlikely. The police and other agencies involved in Alliance plan to continue seeking funding for the project in Millfield but do not intend to immediately implement it elsewhere in the city. According to police, the project is an 'intense initiative about longevity and sustainability,' requiring careful consideration and planning.The improvement in trust in police in the Peterborough area is a positive development, particularly given the historical challenges in community policing. As the Alliance project continues to evolve, it will be closely watched to see if its successes can be replicated in other areas, ultimately contributing to a safer and more cohesive community.
