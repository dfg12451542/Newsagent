A historic steam locomotive has begun a new journey in Sheringham, Norfolk, after a nine-year refurbishment. The engine, which was discovered in a convent in Hertfordshire, re-entered service at the North Norfolk Railway on Saturday. Alex Alder, who was instrumental in the locomotive's restoration, described the moment it pulled passenger trains for the first time as 'absolutely incredible.' 'After nine years of efforts, for the very first time ever she pulled passenger trains. It was absolutely incredible,' he said. The locomotive's journey to restoration was nothing short of remarkable. Mr. Alder described discovering the engine as like hearing 'of a mythical Egyptian tomb.' He had been following a wild tip-off about a lost engine, which led him to a convent in Hertfordshire, where the engine had been stored. 'I could never have imagined taking tea and biscuits with [a] mother superior at a convent, and hearing of 'Malcolm's engine',' he recalled. The restoration process was a labor of love, costing around £200,000 and involving a team of volunteers. North Norfolk Railway will use the engine to pull passenger carriages from Sheringham to Holt. Graham Hukins, a representative of the railway, said it was 'absolutely wonderful' to see the engine running in Sheringham. 'This weekend is the very first time it has been paired with passenger carriages, and it is an absolute delight to have it join our working fleet,' he added. The locomotive's debut comes as North Norfolk Railway celebrates 50 years of running trains.
