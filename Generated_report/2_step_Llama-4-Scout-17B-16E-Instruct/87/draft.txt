Artists fear AI using their talent for free. Lily Dooner, a 28-year-old artist from Leominster, expressed her concerns about the impact of artificial intelligence (AI) on her livelihood. Her worst fear is finding her own distinctive vocals have been stolen from her by the power of AI. 'What really scares me is the idea that one day I might be watching a film, and hear a voice created out of snippets of mine,' she said. The fear of AI using artists' talents without permission or compensation is shared by many in the creative industry. Ms. Dooner noted that AI's vast potential and speed were already impacting creative industries like her own. 'AI can give you something passable in seconds… for me to give you something it's going to be hours of my time,' she said. However, she emphasized that AI's reliance on mimicry would always hold it back, saying 'The difference is AI can't give you something incredible. I can.'Tim Evans, artistic director and CEO of Powerhouse, a Herefordshire-based theatre organization, also weighed in on the issue. He acknowledged that AI models cannot be built without 'hoovering up' vast amounts of data, but also saw potential benefits to the technology. After his brother Alex died in 2017, he used AI software to recreate his voice for a play, training the model with dozens of recordings. However, Mr. Evans emphasized that artists needed to be compensated for their private intellectual property being used, saying 'As yet the compensation question is being left unanswered.'The UK government is considering the issue, with Peter Kyle, a minister, offering to look comprehensively at the challenges creatives face in the future. 'I am offering to look comprehensively at the challenges creatives have into the future,' he said. 'I will give them the rights that they have in the analogue age… fit for the digital age.' However, some artists feel that the government is not doing enough, with Rebecca Farkas, a visual artist, saying that the government currently favors putting the onus on creators to opt out of having their work scraped by AI. As the debate continues, artists like Ms. Dooner and Ms. Farkas remain concerned about the impact of AI on their livelihoods.
