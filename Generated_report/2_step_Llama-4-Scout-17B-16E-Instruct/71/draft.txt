The Fifpro player union has strongly criticized the Fifa Club World Cup, with general secretary Sergio Marchi launching a scathing attack on the tournament's organization and Fifa's priorities. In a statement, Marchi appeared to compare Fifa president Gianni Infantino to the Roman emperor Nero, saying 'what was presented as a global celebration of football was nothing more than a fiction created by Fifa, promoted by its president, without dialogue, sensitivity, and respect for those who sustain the game with their daily efforts.' Marchi also claimed that Fifa chose to continue increasing its revenue at the expense of players' bodies and health. 'A grandiloquent staging inevitably reminiscent of the 'bread and circuses' of Nero's Rome, entertainment for the masses while behind the scenes inequality, precariousness, and the lack of protection for the true protagonists deepen,' he said. The criticism comes after the tournament was held under what Fifpro described as 'unacceptable conditions' that put players' physical integrity at risk. Marchi warned that such situations must not be repeated, stating, 'This situation must not only be denounced, but must be firmly warned against. What happened cannot be repeated under any circumstances at next year's Fifa World Cup.' Fifpro's concerns have been echoed by a long-standing issue with the overcrowded schedule, lack of physical and mental rest for players, and the lack of dialogue from Fifa. As Marchi emphasized, 'We cannot continue to play with the health of players to fuel a marketing machine. There is no spectacle possible if the voices of the protagonists are silenced.' In response to these claims, it's worth noting that Fifa officials have previously stated that player welfare has been central to their decision-making process. However, Fifpro was not invited to a key meeting on player welfare held by Infantino on the eve of the Club World Cup final, sparking concerns about the organization's commitment to addressing player concerns.
