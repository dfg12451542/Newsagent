The Royal Observatory Greenwich has revealed the shortlist for its annual ZWO Astronomy Photographer of the Year competition. The competition, which aims to inspire curiosity and foster education, has received stunning images from around the world. Among the shortlisted images are breathtaking captures of the cosmos, including a pomegranate-shaped nebula and a spectacular display of the aurora in Norway. Sam Wen, the founder and CEO of ZWO, emphasized the importance of astrophotography in inspiring curiosity and building communities that transcend borders. '...we believe that astrophotography is not only a way to record the cosmos, but also a way to inspire curiosity, foster education and build communities that transcend borders,' said Wen. He further added that 'Everyone deserves a chance to connect with the Universe — and through our support, we hope to bring that experience to more people.'The shortlisted images are a testament to the skill and dedication of the photographers. One image, captured by Deqian Li, shows a stunning nebula in the shape of a pomegranate, which required six days of camping in Hongyuan County, China to capture. Another image, taken on the evening of 12 October 2024 at Pu'u O Kaimukī Park in Hawaii, shows the passage of Comet C/2023 A3, one of the most anticipated astronomical events of the year.The winners of each of the competition's nine categories, two other special prizes, and the overall winner will be announced on Thursday, 11 September 2025. Those looking to catch a glimpse of the photos up close will be able to do so at a special exhibition at the National Maritime Museum from Friday, 12 September. With no clouds in sight, the golden light of sunset can be seen right across the mountains in one of the images, snapped above the peaks of the Dolomite mountains in Italy.
