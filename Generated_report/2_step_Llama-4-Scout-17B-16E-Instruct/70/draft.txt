A child's struggle to fit in at school in Oxfordshire has been described as 'harrowing' by their mother, Lauren. The child's anxiety around school became so severe that they once told Lauren they 'didn't want to live any more.' Lauren's child has found it extremely difficult to cope with the pressures of school, often spending only half an hour in school each day. Lauren described the emotional toll of her child's struggles, saying, 'You can tell they can't cope with being there.' She also expressed her frustration with the education system, stating, 'The frustration of them not having those opportunities to fit into a normal education system is really harrowing.' In an effort to address the issue of school absence and anxiety, some schools have introduced special sessions to help children build their confidence and connect with their peers. Heather Richards, a head teacher, said these sessions have led to 'real improvement' in attendance figures. Lauren's child attends an online version of these sessions and has shown significant improvement, 'literally leap[ing]' to attend and returning 'really buoyant and happy.'The Department for Education has acknowledged the issue, stating that 'the government inherited a broken system, with children and families facing poor outcomes and barriers to opportunity.' They emphasized the importance of a national effort to address school absence, highlighting the need for improved mental health support, additional investment in family support, and a greater focus from Ofsted. By working together, the government, schools, parents, and children can help turn the tide on poor attendance and break down barriers to opportunity.
