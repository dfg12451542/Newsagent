A PE teacher from Wythall has hailed his former student, Lloyd Glasspool, for his historic win at Wimbledon. Glasspool, alongside his partner Julian Cash, became the first all-British pair to win the men's doubles title at the All England Club in 89 years. 'To think a student from Woodrush High School in Wythall can win at Wimbledon - it's just amazing,' said Joe Maidment, who taught Glasspool at the school. Maidment had travelled to Wimbledon to watch Glasspool in his second-round match and described his performance throughout the tournament as 'inspirational.' He believes Glasspool's achievement should be a message to everyone in the West Midlands to follow their dreams and see what they can achieve. Maidment also recalled playing tennis against Glasspool when he was just a student, saying 'I can now say I've played tennis against a Wimbledon champion. Unfortunately, I did lose 6-0, 6-0, and he was only in year eight at the time.' Glasspool, now 31, jokingly referenced the match, saying 'I've come a long way since when we played in year seven or eight and I gave you that little thrashing.' The win is 'the icing on the cake' for Glasspool, who has now secured 'the triple crown' having won Wimbledon, Queens, and Eastbourne. Maidment expressed his pride in his former student, saying 'I am so, so pleased for him and everyone involved.' Glasspool also thanked his former teacher and the school for their support, saying it gave him 'a nice little confidence boost to go and do great things in the sport.'
