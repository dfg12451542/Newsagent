Wales' new Sustainable Farming Scheme is 'absolutely the right scheme', according to Huw Irranca-Davies, Wales' agriculture minister. The second iteration of the Sustainable Farming Scheme (SFS) and the rate of payment farmers can expect for joining it is due to be announced next week, after unions and farmers rejected the original scheme, staging protests. Speaking ahead of its launch, Irranca-Davies said he was 'hopeful it will land to a good reception', even though he conceded 'there will be the odd person' who wished it had been done differently. The final scheme was 'the result of more than 12 months of intense work' involving farming unions, meat, livestock and dairy sectors as well as environmental groups 'to get the balance'. With 80% of Wales' landscape managed by farmers, the government contends that these farmers have a crucial part to contribute in addressing climate change and the decline in natural ecosystems. Irranca-Davies said the scheme would be 'unlike anything else that's been delivered in the UK', adopting 'a whole farm approach and a whole nation approach that delivers for the people of Wales.' The minister emphasized that the scheme aims to promote farming that produces good food to high animal welfare standards while doing the right thing for the environment. Although he would not commit to publishing an impact assessment of the final iteration, he expressed his confidence that it 'will be better' for both 'viable farm businesses and environmental benefits as well'. However, not everyone is convinced. Plaid Cymru's Delyth Jewell MS expressed frustration that some people in the farming community feel they haven't been heard, emphasizing the need to make sure their lives are made sustainable too. Conservative Senedd leader Darren Millar MS called for the policy to be voted on by politicians to ensure 'democratic legitimacy' for the plan, stressing that food security must be at its heart.
