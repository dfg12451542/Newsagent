Australia's tourists faced a dramatic collapse on the first day of the day/night Test against the West Indies, finishing with a score of 225 all out. The visitors seemed poised for a strong total at 157-3, but the loss of their final seven wickets for just 68 runs highlighted their struggles against West Indian bowlers. Steve Smith was the standout performer for Australia, top-scoring with 48 runs, while Cameron Green contributed 46 before a late flurry of dismissals plagued the team. 'Every pitch has been tough. The more balls you spend in the middle you hope it gets better but it just hasn't been the case,' Green reflected on the team's batting woes.nnDespite their collapse, Australia had a moment of relief at the end of the day as West Indies stood at 16-1, trailing by 209 runs. Mitchell Starc, celebrating his 100th Test appearance, made a crucial breakthrough by bowling opener Kevlon Anderson for just three runs. Starc now joins the ranks of Australia’s great pace bowlers, only the second after Glenn McGrath to reach this milestone.nnShamar Joseph emerged as the spearhead of the West Indies attack, claiming 4 wickets for just 33 runs, supported by Jayden Seales and Justin Greaves who took three wickets apiece. The match has drawn attention due to the threading together of tight bowling performances in a contest reminiscent of earlier matches between the two teams, where neither side managed to score over 200 runs in their initial innings. Both teams continue to navigate challenging pitch conditions, which have contributed to the ongoing struggle for runs.nnAs the match unfolds, the historical context looms large.
