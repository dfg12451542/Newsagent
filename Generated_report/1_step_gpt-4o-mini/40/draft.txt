As Manny Pacquiao prepares for his highly anticipated comeback fight on Saturday, trainer Dave Coldwell has made it clear he will not be tuning in. 'I won't be watching Manny Pacquiao's comeback fight on Saturday,' Coldwell stated, expressing his concern that such events prioritize nostalgia over merit. He elaborated, 'It feels like a shortcut...and that's unfair to fighters who are working their way up properly.' Despite recognizing Pacquiao's impressive legacy as an eight-division world champion and a global icon, Coldwell argues that the return of seasoned fighters like Pacquiao sets a troubling precedent for the sport.nnThe shock of Pacquiao's return has raised eyebrows within the boxing community, particularly regarding the management of aging athletes. Typically, fighters returning from retirement ease back into competition through preliminary bouts, yet Pacquiao is making a bold leap straight into a significant matchup. As Coldwell noted, 'That's the part that really shocked me when I heard of Pacquiao's return.' Instead of taking the traditional route of tune-up fights, Pacquiao is skipping ahead, which, according to Coldwell, undermines the efforts of younger competitors who are grinding their way through the ranks.nnThis sentiment echoes concerns raised by figures like Mauricio Sulaiman, the WBC president, who recently clashed with Coldwell on social media over the implications of veteran fighters returning to the ring. Coldwell remains adamant that this practice could detract from the sport's integrity. As boxing continues to evolve, the debate over how fighters age within the sport remains critical, especially highlighted by the excitement surrounding Pacquiao's return, whose energetic style and groundbreaking achievements have made him a true legend in boxing history.
