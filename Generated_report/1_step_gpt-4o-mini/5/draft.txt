Trust in police in the Peterborough area has seen notable improvement, thanks to the successful implementation of the Alliance project, according to a report from the Peterborough City Council. Centered around Lincoln Road in Millfield, the initiative has reportedly led to a staggering 700% increase in community intelligence reporting, which the council attributes to enhanced confidence in local policing efforts. 'Trust in police in a city centre community has risen as a result of a project aimed at fighting organised crime,' the council stated.nnThe positive impact of the Alliance project is further reflected in significant law enforcement achievements, with the council reporting the seizure of 700,000 illegal cigarettes, 20,000 vapes, and £115,000 in cash throughout the area. These results underscore the project’s effectiveness in addressing local crime and fostering a safer environment for residents. Inspector Sam Tucker from Cambridgeshire Police acknowledged the effectiveness of certain concepts used in the initiative, though he noted that fully adopting the entire project in other areas might be challenging.nnDespite the reported successes, the council acknowledged that the project faced difficulties in engaging local community groups. A recent report to a scrutiny committee indicated that while the Alliance project struggled to establish connections with these groups, the involved agencies remain committed to searching for additional funding to sustain the initiative in Millfield.nnHistorically, the Alliance project was initiated in 2023 as part of the Home Office's Clear, Hold and Build strategy, which aimed to enhance public safety and improve perceptions of policing. However, the Home Office previously indicated that there was 'no evidence' nationally of similar initiatives positively influencing community perceptions of policing, although they acknowledged a positive impact on crime rates. As the Alliance project continues, the long-term goal emphasizes sustainability and community involvement in the fight against organized crime.
