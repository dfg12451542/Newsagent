The operator of a waste recycling site in South Elmsall has lodged an appeal against the revocation of its environmental permit due to concerns that the facility poses a health hazard to the local community. Carly Chambers, area manager for the Environment Agency (EA) in Yorkshire, confirmed that the appeal was filed by Minore, also known as Mineral Processing Ltd. 'The environmental permit will now stay in place until the Planning Inspectorate has announced the outcome of this appeal,' she stated. This suspension, according to Chambers, is crucial as it prevents further waste from being brought onto the site and mandates the removal of waste that breaches current permit conditions. 'If the operator does not comply with the suspension notice, it will be committing an offence,' she added.nnLocal concerns have intensified surrounding the site as residents fear potential health impacts. Chambers noted that the EA is acutely aware of the situation, acknowledging, 'We understood the impact this site is having on the community, and our increased regulatory response continues.' As it stands, the site is still excavating and processing materials legally, a spokesperson for Minore emphasized, also highlighting that regular water samples taken from Frickley Beck have shown no adverse effects. 'Our employees have been working at the site for over 12 years and now fear for their jobs due to this escalation,' the spokesperson lamented, reflecting the anxiety among local workers.nnThe ongoing appeal underscores a significant environmental concern that has mobilized the community and regulatory agencies alike. Local governance and regulatory bodies are now tasked with balancing operational compliance with the health and safety of residents, as the outcome of this appeal remains to be seen.
