In a dramatic escalation of violence in Sudan, the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) stormed the beleaguered city of el-Fasher on Friday, capturing key locations including a cattle market and a prison. Witnesses report that the offensive, which lasted seven hours, turned the already chaotic situation into what one observer called a 'death trap.' Mathilde Vu, from the Norwegian Refugee Council, described the horrific ongoing shelling and attacks on civilian infrastructure, saying, 'What we're hearing is stories of horror and terror and weekly shelling.' nnThe assault on el-Fasher marks a significant moment in the ongoing struggle for dominance over the city, which has been under siege for 15 months. Siddig Omar, a resident, recounted the devastating impact on the civilian populace, stating that one of the shells hit a vehicle, killing five civilians inside. Civilians increasingly find themselves at risk, with many reporting attempts to flee the violence under the cover of darkness, sometimes on donkeys, while others manage to sneak away foot by foot, only to find there's nothing available in markets even if they have money. 'There is no bread, no food, and no work to be found,' lamented one local, adding that 'the situation here is truly terrible.' nnAlthough the Sudanese army managed to repel the RSF temporarily, the violence persisted, with reports of continued drone shelling. Almost 379,000 people have now fled to Tawila, where they are facing dire humanitarian challenges, including an outbreak of cholera aggravated by a lack of resources. As Mathilde Vu pointed out, local volunteers are risking their lives daily to provide food to those who are starving, stating, 'People just rely on the solidarity of others.' nnThe situation in el-Fasher is further complicated by a communications blackout, leaving accurate information about conditions difficult to obtain. With the RSF gaining ground, civilians find themselves cut off from humanitarian aid, contributing to a desperate state for many residents. This conflict, which erupted in April 2023 between the RSF and the Sudanese army, has shown no signs of abating, leaving civilians trapped in an increasingly perilous environment.
