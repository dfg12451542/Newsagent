F1: The Movie star Callie Cooke experienced a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity when she performed a real pit stop during last year's British Grand Prix at Silverstone. Reflecting on the moment, Cooke described it as 'mad' to have been granted access to the high-speed world of Formula 1 racing, emphasizing the unique connection she felt to the Northamptonshire location. 'Because of the Northamptonshire-Silverstone connection, it feels so special and so real, it's just been the coolest thing,' she stated.nnCooke's involvement in the film extended beyond acting, as she immersed herself in training with the pit crew. She recalled, 'I was the only actor in the pit crew and all of the other guys were mechanics or engineers or worked in motor sports or stunt guys.' This experience helped Cooke navigate the male-dominated environment of F1, where she acknowledged the importance of holding her own, saying, 'I knew that if I was going to play a woman in this male-dominated industry, I was just going to have to hold my own and get stuck in.'nnThe actress spent an entire day in the garage, changing tyres and oil, and grasping the technical aspects of the role. 'We did a real pit stop in between Mercedes and Ferrari, and we did it as quick as the other teams,' she recounted, highlighting her remarkable experience working alongside elite mechanics and engineers. 'It was one of the best experiences of my life,' she added, showcasing the adrenaline and excitement involved in the sport. nnOverall, Cooke's journey not only highlights the personal growth she experienced while preparing for her role but also showcases the collaborative spirit that exists within the Formula 1 community. As the cast and crew worked amidst real F1 teams and events, it undoubtedly solidified the film's authenticity and the thrilling allure that motorsport holds for many.
