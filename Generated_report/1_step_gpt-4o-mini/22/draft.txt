Wythall's Woodrush High School is celebrating as PE teacher Joe Maidment hails the historic achievement of former student Lloyd Glasspool, who recently won the men's doubles title at Wimbledon. Alongside partner Julian Cash, Glasspool's victory marks a momentous occasion, as it is the first time in 89 years that an all-British pair has claimed the title at the prestigious All England Club. 'To think a student from Woodrush High School in Wythall can win at Wimbledon - it’s just amazing,' Maidment expressed, clearly overwhelmed by the success of the 31-year-old from Redditch.nnMaidment, who attended the tournament to support Glasspool during his second-round match, referred to the athlete's performance throughout the competition as 'inspirational.' He emphasizes that this achievement should serve as motivation for the youth in the West Midlands, stating, 'It should be a message to everyone to follow their dreams and see what they can achieve.' This thrilling accomplishment is more than just a win for Glasspool; it represents the culmination of dedication, hard work, and an unwavering belief in one’s potential.nnIn a playful nod to their past, Glasspool reminisced with Maidment about their tennis matches during his school days, humorously recalling, 'I gave you that little thrashing.' He acknowledged the role that supportive educators like Maidment played in his journey, stating, 'I appreciate the support from you and the whole of the Woodrush School.' With this recent title, Glasspool has secured what Maidment describes as 'the triple crown' after victories at Wimbledon, Queens, and Eastbourne, placing him among the elite in tennis history.nnAs the local community reflects on this monumental achievement, Maidment expressed immense pride not only for Glasspool but for the inspiration he represents to future generations of athletes. Their local hero's success underscores a narrative of perseverance, ambition, and community support, reinforcing the belief that dreams can indeed become a reality.
