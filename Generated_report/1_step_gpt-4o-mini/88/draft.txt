As the world commemorates the 40th anniversary of Live Aid, the legendary concert that captivated nearly two billion viewers, one woman is eager to shed light on an unsung hero of the event—her father, Maurice Jones. 'He was very modest. His take on it was that he was there to do a job, he wasn’t there to be in the limelight. He did play an important part,' Nicola, Jones's daughter, shared with BBC WM. Maurice Jones, who passed away in 2009, was approached to help organize various elements of the monumental concert due to his extensive connections in the music industry.nnMs. Jones recalls attending the Wembley concert at the tender age of 15, a day filled with spectacular performances from artists like Queen and David Bowie. 'I was very lucky,' she noted, describing the thrilling atmosphere filled with excitement and energy, both in the audience and backstage. 'Watching the reaction of the audience was really spectacular,' she added, reflecting on the unforgettable moment when Freddie Mercury captivated fans with his electrifying performance. Her realization of Queen's massive appeal during the concert left her in awe.nnForty years ago, on July 13, 1985, Live Aid was broadcast to viewers worldwide, uniting fans in support of famine relief efforts for Ethiopia. This landmark event featured iconic performances from superstars, including Paul McCartney and Tina Turner, and became a defining moment in music history. The enduring legacy of Live Aid continues to resonate, with many reflecting on their once-in-a-lifetime experiences at the concert.nnIn celebrating her father's contributions, Nicola expressed her desire for his efforts to be remembered. 'It will be nice if he is remembered in a small way,' she said. As the music world reflects on Live Aid's remarkable impact, the stories of those behind the scenes, like Maurice Jones, highlight the multifaceted nature of such historic events, reminding us that every role, big or small, contributes to a remarkable legacy.
