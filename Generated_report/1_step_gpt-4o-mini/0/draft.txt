Cirencester's rich railway history takes center stage as the town prepares for its upcoming History Festival, scheduled from October 25 to November 1. As part of the festivities, the town's iconic Old Station, designed by renowned architect Isambard Kingdom Brunel, will be temporarily reopened to the public. Festival producers are calling for memories, photographs, and memorabilia related to the station, which many locals remember from its vibrant past. 'We know that there are lots of people out there who can remember the station in its heyday,' said Jess Yarrow, the festival producer. She emphasized their desire for personal stories and artifacts to help recreate the atmosphere of the station at its peak.nnThe Old Station, which closed in 1965, has been described by Yarrow as a 'slightly sad, empty-looking building,' yet she remains hopeful that some community members will cherish their memories of using it or witnessing significant wartime events, such as the welcoming of evacuees and soldiers during World War Two. 'We’d love to hear stories, we’d love to see photographs, we’d love to have any memorabilia that people have got tucked away,' Yarrow added. The event promises to revive the spirit of the 'golden age of rail,' with free tours scheduled for both Saturdays of the festival.nnArchitect Jonathan Rixon, who is refurbishing the Old Station for use as his offices, will guide the tours and is dedicated to uncovering the hidden treasures within the building, such as a boardroom where Brunel is said to have held meetings. Rixon expressed his passion for preserving industrial heritage, stating, 'It’s important to protect our industrial heritage. I’m passionate about preserving this piece of history and sharing it with the town.' In addition to the tours, a unique audio listening project will showcase personal stories from the station's history, further enriching the festival experience. Students from Cirencester College have also collaborated to create a virtual reality experience, providing a glimpse into what the station looked like during the Victorian era.nnAs locals gather to reminisce about this significant landmark, the festival is set not only to celebrate Cirencester’s railway legacy but also to strengthen community ties through shared memories and experiences.
