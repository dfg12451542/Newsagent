Swansea has made history by hosting the UK’s first-ever IRONMAN Pro Series edition, showcasing elite endurance athletes against the breathtaking backdrop of the Gower Peninsula on July 13, 2025. The event unfolded under sweltering conditions, with air temperatures exceeding 28°C, prompting race organizers to decree a non-wetsuit swim for the professionals, marking a unique challenge in the UK. Defending champion Harry Palmer triumphed with a time of 3:51:18, narrowly edging out France's Leon Chevalier by just 49 seconds, while Kieran Lindars captured third place.nnReflecting on the demanding race, Palmer commented on the heat, expressing sympathy for age-group competitors facing tougher conditions. 'It was hot, and I do feel for all the age-groupers who are out there today,' he told BBC Sport Wales, emphasizing the high caliber of the race, particularly during the cycling segment where he noted he was among elite riders. 'I felt I was in good company,' Palmer said.nnIn the female professional category, Kat Matthews claimed victory with a time of 4:20:37, elaborating on her return to Swansea: 'Three years feels like a lifetime. A lot has happened since then,' she remarked, highlighting her transformation since her last triumph in 2022. Matthews initially faced challenges but quickly surged to the front, enjoying a mix of control and heat-induced exhaustion during the final stretch. 'It was quite nice to feel in control until the run, when I felt a bit hot,' she noted.nnThousands gathered to support the athletes along the 70-mile course, a testament to the passionate community spirit of Swansea. Race Director Rebecca Sutherland lauded the event's success, stating, 'It was another incredible race with thousands of athletes crossing the famous red carpet finish line to rapturous spectator support.' As the day concluded, the excitement built for the future, with plans already underway for the fourth edition of this remarkable event in the captivating coastal city next summer.
