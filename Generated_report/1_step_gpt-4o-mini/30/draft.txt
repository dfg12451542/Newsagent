In a thrilling conclusion to the Evian Championship, Australian golfer Grace Kim secured her first major title with a remarkable finish, defeating her close friend Atthaya Thitikul in a playoff. Kim, who starved off the competition with a clutch 12-foot eagle putt on the 18th hole, had previously countered Thitikul’s birdie attempt to force the extra holes, showcasing the intense rivalry and sportsmanship between the two rising stars on the LPGA tour.nnLottie Woad, the young English golfer who had captured the Irish Open just a week prior, finished on 13 under par, narrowly missing the playoff opportunity after she overshot a 10-foot birdie putt. “I was trying to be in contention and winning this, so it will be the same there,” said Woad, reflecting on her competitive spirit. Facing the reality of Kim's and Thitikul's formidable performances, Woad quipped to her caddie, “Is there even any point, she's not making six,” as both players stormed through the final holes.nnThis tournament marks an important milestone in the careers of these young athletes. While Kim celebrated her victory, Woad solidified her future on the LPGA tour by reaching the magical number of 20 points in the top 25 finishes, as part of the LPGA’s Elite Amateur Pathway. Much like Woad, players can use the championship as a stepping stone towards securing their positions in professional golf.nnHistorically, the Evian Championship has been a crucial event on the women's golf calendar, part of the major series that allows golfers to gain international recognition and establish a lasting legacy. For Woad, a strong finish is not solely about accolades but also about ensuring her opportunities in the competitive landscape of women’s golf. As she reflected on her ambitions, Woad stated, “It has always been in my dreams to play on the LPGA Tour and I'm grateful for the opportunities of the programme,” underscoring her passion for the game and her determination to succeed.
