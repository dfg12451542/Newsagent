Wimbledon 2025 has crowned Iga Swiatek and Jannik Sinner as the champions of this year's prestigious tournament held in London. On Saturday, Polish sensation Swiatek captured her first Wimbledon Ladies' Singles title in an astonishing match against America's Amanda Anisimova, finishing with a commanding score of 6-0, 6-0 in just 57 minutes. 'It seems super surreal,' said the 24-year-old Swiatek. 'Honestly, I didn’t even dream of winning Wimbledon because it was way too far.' This victory solidifies her status as one of the sport’s leading figures, especially after her prior success at other Grand Slam events.nnFor the men, Jannik Sinner emerged victorious following a thrilling contest, showcasing his skills against a formidable opponent. After the match, he expressed admiration for fellow competitor Carlos Alcaraz, stating, 'It is so difficult to play you, but we have a great relationship off the court.' He also encouraged Alcaraz, saying, 'Keep going, keep pushing, you are going to hold this trophy many times - you already have twice!'nnAdditionally, the tournament featured strong performances in wheelchair tennis, where Great Britain's Alfie Hewett faced off against world number one Tokito Oda. Hewett expressed gratitude for the support he received, stating, 'When you lose, to have this amount of love and support, this sort of atmosphere doesn’t happen to us on a regular basis.' Oda ultimately triumphed, winning the match after losing the initial set.nnOther winners at Wimbledon 2025 included Britain’s Julian Cash and Lloyd Glasspool in the Men’s Doubles, Russia's Veronika Kudermetova and Belgium's Elise Mertens in the Women's Doubles, and the Netherlands' Sem Verbeek with Chechia's Katerina Sin in the Mixed Doubles. In Wheelchair Doubles, Martin de la Puente and Ruben Spaargaren claimed victory over reigning British champions Alfie Hewett and Gordon Reid. The tournament continues to attract international acclaim, highlighting the vibrant and evolving nature of the sport.
