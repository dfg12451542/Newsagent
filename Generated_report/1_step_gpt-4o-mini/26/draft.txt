A historic steam locomotive has commenced a new journey at the North Norfolk Railway in Sheringham, marking a significant milestone after a nine-year refurbishment. For the very first time, the renowned locomotive is now pulling passenger trains, with Alex Alder, a key figure in the restoration, expressing his joy: 'After nine years of efforts, for the very first time ever she pulled passenger trains. It was absolutely incredible.' This achievement not only revives the charm of steam travel but also celebrates the enduring legacy of heritage railways in the UK.nnThe locomotive's journey to this moment was filled with unexpected twists. Alder recounted how discovering the engine felt like 'hearing of a mythical Egyptian tomb.' He never anticipated that he would be led down a Hertfordshire country lane, following a tip about a lost engine, which he later found in a convent, describing it as '39 tons of miracle.' The restoration, which cost nearly £200,000, was a labor of love carried out by a dedicated team of volunteers over nearly a decade.nnGraham Hukins, another enthusiast involved with the North Norfolk Railway, shared his excitement, stating, 'It was absolutely wonderful to see the engine running in Sheringham.' This weekend marks an important celebration, as it is the first time the locomotive has been paired with passenger carriages, coinciding with the railway's 50th anniversary.nnThe re-entering of this steam locomotive into service reflects a wider trend in heritage railways across the UK, where classic trains are honored and maintained for future generations. In July, the railway is expected to feature other iconic engines such as the Flying Scotsman during a special event, providing enthusiasts and families alike with a chance to experience the nostalgia and history of steam locomotives firsthand. With the continued passion surrounding steam engines, the story of this locomotive in Sheringham is just one of many in the ongoing celebration of Britain's rich railway heritage.
