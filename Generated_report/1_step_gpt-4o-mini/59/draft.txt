Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla, the first Indian astronaut to reach the International Space Station (ISS), is now on his way back to Earth following a historic mission. Describing his experience, Shukla stated, 'It has been an incredible journey. Even though now it is coming to an end, for you and me there is a long way to go.' He emphasized the significance of India’s human spaceflight program, remarking that 'if we are determined, even the stars are attainable.' Shukla's return follows the undocking of the Axiom-4 (Ax-4) mission from the ISS, which happened through a live broadcast, showcasing the craft separating from the orbiting laboratory.nnAs Shukla and his three crewmates journey back home, they are expected to splash down off the coast of California on July 15 at approximately 15:00 India time (09:30 GMT), after a mission duration longer than initially planned. During their stay at the ISS, the Ax-4 crew conducted around 60 scientific experiments, seven of which were designed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). This experience, according to ISRO, will significantly contribute to India’s ambitions for future human spaceflights.nnShubhanshu Shukla's mission marks a significant moment in India’s space exploration history, being only the second Indian to travel to space, 41 years after Rakesh Sharma's historic flight aboard a Russian Soyuz in 1984. With India aiming to launch its first manned mission, Gaganyaan, into orbit by 2027, and further plans for a space station by 2035 and lunar missions by 2040, Shukla’s journey can be viewed as a pivotal step toward achieving these ambitious goals.nnBefore venturing into space, Shukla expressed that the past year had been 'nothing short of transformative,' reflecting the aspirations of millions. He remarked, 'As I go into space, I carry not just instruments and equipment, I carry hopes and dreams of a billion hearts.' His return is eagerly awaited by the nation, which sees this mission as a beacon of confidence in its growing capabilities in the realm of space exploration.
