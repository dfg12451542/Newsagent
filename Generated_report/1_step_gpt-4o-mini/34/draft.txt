Huge crowds flocked to Royal Portrush on Monday as golf enthusiasts eagerly waited to watch Rory McIlroy's first practice round ahead of The Open 2025. The sight was a remarkable confirmation of McIlroy's enduring popularity, particularly as he prepares to compete on home turf for the first time since earning his elusive Green Jacket at the Masters. Onlookers were treated to an electrifying moment when McIlroy, currently ranked world number two, sunk a birdie putt on the 18th green, igniting cheers from the sizeable galleries. After his round, he took the time to engage with fans, signing autographs for about 15 minutes and sharing in the excitement of the day.nnMcIlroy's return to Royal Portrush carries significant weight, not just for him personally but also for Northern Ireland, where he is considered one of the greatest sportspersons in its history. His victory at Hoylake in 2014 solidified his status as a major champion, and now, as he heads into this Open, he carries the hopes of his fans and country. As his practice round wrapped up, anticipation grew for his scheduled media address, where he was expected to share insights on his preparation and objectives for the tournament.nnThis year’s Open is particularly poignant as it marks McIlroy’s first significant appearance in front of a home crowd after his triumphant 2023 Masters victory, which enhanced his already remarkable legacy. The success at the Masters is heralded by many, including Darren Clarke, as a defining moment that cements McIlroy's standing in Northern Irish sporting history. As the competition approaches, fans remain hopeful that McIlroy will deliver another signature performance, one that could further elevate his status both at home and abroad.
