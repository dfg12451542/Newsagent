This week in Tyneside, a mix of arts, infrastructure issues, and academic achievements highlighted local news. A significant event was the unveiling of a permanent memorial for the iconic Sycamore Gap tree, which was unlawfully chopped down nearly two years ago. Artist Charlie Winney expressed his admiration for the memorial, stating he was 'blown away by how huggable it is.' The memorial, placed at the Sill National Landscape Discovery Centre, allows visitors to physically engage with a part of the tree's trunk, commemorating its cultural significance in the region.nnOn a more logistical note, Tyne and Wear Metro trains faced operational challenges again, as three new units were pulled from service due to air-conditioning leaks. This follows previous concerns addressed by operator Nexus, who remain optimistic and 'reassured' by the findings as they continue to rectify the faults that have affected the service.nnIn the realm of academia, Newcastle University announced that renowned TV historian David Olusoga will receive an honorary degree this summer, celebrating his contributions to the field and his connection to the region.nnThese events reflect Tyneside's vibrant community spirit, dealing with both loss and celebration amidst operational hurdles.
