Wales celebrated a hard-fought 31-22 victory over Japan in Kobe, a win that not only reflects their tenacity but also serves as a vital morale booster for the team. Fly-half Dan Edwards, who scored a decisive late try, expressed his joy following the match. 'It was about getting a win, I’m absolutely chuffed,' said Edwards. He emphasized the team's resilience, particularly after facing criticism for their previous performance, stating, 'Especially with everyone saying we didn’t deserve to win last week and were writing us off.' This victory is a significant milestone for Welsh rugby, which has faced a challenging period recently.nnThe match was characterized by outstanding individual performances, most notably from Taine Plumtree, who made a game-changing impact after his second-half introduction. His audacious reverse pass led to Edwards' try, illustrating the bold approach encouraged by interim head coach Matt Sherratt. 'That last play summarises it for me... I didn’t intend for him to be that brave, but it worked!' Sherratt remarked. Plumtree echoed this sentiment, noting that the team's strategy was rooted in bravery and expression, bolstered by the unwavering support from their bench players.nnKeelan Giles, who made a significant contribution after replacing the injured Josh Adams at halftime, shared his emotional connection to the game, reflecting on his personal journey within the squad. 'A win has been a long time coming for fans of Wales. This group has worked extremely hard over the last couple of years... It’s almost the monkey off our backs,' he stated, signaling a newfound optimism for the future of Welsh rugby.nnWales' victory in Kobe showcases not just their ability to overcome adversity but also highlights their depth in talent, as contributions came from both starters and substitutes alike. As they look ahead to upcoming matches, the team aims to build on this success, signaling their determination to reclaim their status as a competitive force in international rugby.
