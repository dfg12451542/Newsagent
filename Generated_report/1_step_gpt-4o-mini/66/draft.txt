In a major step towards revitalizing Warwick's architectural heritage, more than £2.8 million has been secured to transform two historic 15th-century buildings into affordable homes. The project, known as St Michael's Place, aims to rescue and repurpose the Master’s House and St Michael's Chapel in Saltisford, a site that has remained derelict for over 50 years. The funding comes from the National Lottery Heritage Fund, which has facilitated a collaborative effort between West Midlands Heritage and Warwick District Council.nnCouncillor Helen Adkins expressed her enthusiasm for the project, stating, 'We will be able to preserve the legacy of the site and rescue these long-neglected historic buildings, offering them a sustainable future.' This restoration not only seeks to provide much-needed housing but also to breathe new life into a locality that holds significant historical value. nnCorinne Brazier, chair of West Midlands Heritage, echoed this sentiment, emphasizing the project's importance for the community. 'We are now able to ensure that St Michael's Place will have a very homely future,' she remarked. As part of the initiative, the project will create a heritage community assistant role and offer opportunities for local residents to learn about heritage craft skills. Additionally, a series of community events and activities are planned to engage the public and raise awareness of the site's historical significance.nnThe National Lottery Heritage Fund's commitment is vital, with initial estimates suggesting that over 90% of the required £19.4 million for the restoration is now in place. As the project moves towards this important delivery stage, both local residents and heritage enthusiasts are coming together to support what promises to be a transformative chapter for St Michael's Place and its surrounding community.
