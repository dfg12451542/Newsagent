The Royal Observatory Greenwich has unveiled the shortlist for its annual ZWO Astronomy Photographer of the Year competition, showcasing stunning images that capture the mystique of space. 'Astrophotography is not only a way to record the cosmos, but also a way to inspire curiosity, foster education and build communities that transcend borders,' stated Sam Wen, the founder and CEO of ZWO. 'Everyone deserves a chance to connect with the Universe — and through our support, we hope to bring that experience to more people.' This year's shortlist features breathtaking captures, including an eruption photographed on November 7, 2024, which lasted approximately one hour, and a striking image taken by Deqian Li resembling a pomegranate, captured after six days of camping in Hongyuan County, China.nnAmong the highlights, one striking photograph depicts the passage of Comet C/2023 A3, snapped on October 12, 2024, at Pu'u O Kaimukī Park in Hawaii. This event was one of the most anticipated astronomical occurrences of the year and reflects the deepening public engagement with space phenomena. Another standout photo, taken above the Dolomite mountains in Italy, showcases a mesmerizing sunset illuminating the landscape with golden light, providing a breathtaking contrast against the deepening twilight sky.nnThe competition will announce the winners of its nine categories, along with two special prizes and the overall winner, on Thursday, September 11, 2025. For those keen to experience the visuals in person, a special exhibition featuring the shortlisted works will open at the National Maritime Museum starting September 12, offering a unique opportunity for the public to engage with these remarkable cosmic visuals.
