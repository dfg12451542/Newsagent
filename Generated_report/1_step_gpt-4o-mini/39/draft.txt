Rory McIlroy, the 36-year-old Northern Irish golfer, has stated that winning The Open Championship at Royal Portrush this week would be 'just as emotional' as his recent victory at the Masters. Speaking to BBC Sport NI, McIlroy reflected on the significance of a home victory, emphasizing, 'It would be amazing,' and adding, 'I think it would be just as emotional, if not more emotional than Augusta - and everyone saw the mess I was after that.' His declaration underscores the deep connection he feels to his roots and the unique pressure of performing on home soil.nnMcIlroy, who won The Open in 2014, acknowledged the intense anticipation surrounding this year's tournament, stating, 'I’m so happy it’s back so soon because I want to have another crack at it.' He recognizes the mistakes of his last appearance at Portrush in 2019, where he struggled with an opening round of 79, contrasting it with his current level of play. 'I’m a better player, manage my game better,' he said, emphasizing confidence in his skills.nnIn a show of gratitude towards his supporters, McIlroy expressed how their enthusiasm uplifts him during competition. 'I want to embrace that this week,' he remarked, suggesting a willingness to connect with the fans and the emotional warmth he derives from their presence. He further added, 'It’s nice to accept adulation, even though I struggle with it at times.' This statement reflects both the joy and burden of being a local hero in the sport.nnAs he prepares for the tournament, McIlroy feels optimistic about his form following a strong performance in Scotland and a brief break that has revitalized him. 'I’m excited with where my game’s at,' he stated, revealing he has been looking forward to this tournament more than any other on the calendar. With the weight of expectation on his shoulders, McIlroy is ready to turn the pressure into the motivation needed for another major triumph at his home course.
