Channel Islands athletes had a memorable experience meeting Princess Anne during the opening ceremony of the 20th Island Games in Orkney. Describing the encounter as a 'once in a lifetime' opportunity, participants expressed their excitement about the event, which emphasizes camaraderie and competition among diverse regions. Ms. Longden, an Alderney runner, said, 'The encounter was amazing,' and noted her delight in discussing sports with the Princess, who has a well-known passion for athletics.nnThe ceremony was graced by broadcaster Lorraine Kelly, who shared her emotional connection to the Orkney Islands and praised the organization of the games. 'I feel very emotional... I'm so proud of the way the event had been organised,' she commented, reflecting the sentiments of many present. As spectators cheered for their home teams, like Jersey's Tilly Le Boutillier, families showcased their support, demonstrating the inclusive spirit the games foster.nnWeather conditions were favorable, adding to the joyous atmosphere as families cheered on their athletes. 'We were in the queue for two and a half hours trying to get in,' Mr. Le Boutillier said, underlining the enthusiasm of supporters eager to witness the opening spectacle. Doreen Manson, who performed during the ceremony with her exercise group, noted the buzz the games have created in Orkney, a community she has been a part of her entire life.nnThe Island Games, a gathering of athletes from various islands, celebrates not only sporting talent but also community spirit and international camaraderie. This year marks a significant milestone, as the games are being held for the 22nd time, officially opened by Princess Anne, who serves as a notable ambassador for sport. The excitement surrounding the event confirms that it remains a cherished tradition among island communities, uniting them through healthy competition.
