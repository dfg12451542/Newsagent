Thames Water has announced a hosepipe ban affecting 1.1 million people in Gloucestershire, Oxfordshire, Berkshire, and Wiltshire due to unprecedented demand for water amid hot and dry weather conditions. The ban, which will go into effect on July 22, prohibits the use of hosepipes for vulnerable activities such as watering gardens, washing cars, and filling paddling pools, with violators facing potential fines of up to £1,000.nnNevil Muncaster, the strategic water resources director at Thames Water, emphasized the urgent need for water conservation, stating, 'We have to take action now.' He noted that this spring and summer have seen significant spikes in customer demand along with minimal rainfall to replenish local supplies. Furthermore, the ongoing drought situation has raised concerns about the sustainability of water resources in the region, prompting the necessity for this measure.nnWhile the ban applies to households, it will not affect businesses where hosepipe use is integral, such as garden centres and car washes. Authorities at Thames Water have been actively working to reduce water leakage, reportedly fixing 650 leaks a week. A spokesperson for the company mentioned that leakage levels across the network are at their lowest ever, but acknowledged that there is still 'more work to do.'nnCritics, including Gary Carter, have decried the ban as 'disgraceful,' especially as customers face rising bills while the utility company struggles with aging infrastructure and leakage issues. The hosepipe ban raises awareness about the ongoing water scarcity crisis, an issue Thames Water has previously warned could intensify unless significant changes occur. Residents have been encouraged to take proactive steps to conserve water, such as turning off taps while brushing teeth and taking shorter showers, as the company anticipates that the situation is unlikely to improve in the near future.
