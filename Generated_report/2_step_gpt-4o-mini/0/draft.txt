As Cirencester prepares for its upcoming History Festival, organizers are seeking personal memories and memorabilia from the town's historic Old Station, which was designed by the renowned engineer Isambard Kingdom Brunel. The festival, scheduled from October 25 to November 1, will allow visitors to explore the Grade-II listed structure, which has been closed since 1965, but is often described as 'a slightly sad, empty-looking building' according to festival producer Jess Yarrow.nnYarrow is keen to gather stories and artifacts related to the station's past, stating, 'We know that there are lots of people out there who can remember the station in its heyday. We’d love to hear stories, we’d love to see photographs, we’d love to have any memorabilia that people have got tucked away.' The event aims to recreate the 'golden age of rail' and invites community participation to dress the station with vintage items like prams and benches.nnArchitect Jonathan Rixon, who is refurbishing the Old Station for his offices, will guide the tours during the festival. He remarked, 'The building holds many hidden treasures, including a boardroom where Brunel sought a meeting space outside London. It’s important to protect our industrial heritage. I’m passionate about preserving this piece of history and sharing it with the town.' His commitment underscores the significance of the station in local history and its architectural value.nnIn addition to physical tours, the festival will feature an audio listening project that shares stories of the station, while students from Cirencester College have created a virtual reality experience that captures life at the station during the Victorian era. These initiatives aim to rekindle community memories of the Old Station, which served as a focal point during key moments in history, including its role in welcoming evacuees and soldiers during World War II.
