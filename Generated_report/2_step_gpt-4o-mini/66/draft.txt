In a significant step towards preserving heritage, Warwick has secured more than £2.8 million to restore the historic leper hospital site, St Michael's Place, into affordable homes. This funding, awarded by the National Lottery Heritage Fund, will transform two 15th-century buildings, which have remained derelict for over 50 years, into sustainable housing for the community. 'We will be able to preserve the legacy of the site and rescue these long-neglected historic buildings, offering them a sustainable future,' said Councillor Helen Adkins, underlining the project's importance for the locality.nnThe St Michael's Place project aims to 'rescue and reuse' the Master's House and St Michael's Chapel, both of which are designated as Grade II* listed buildings that date back over 800 years. Corinne Brazier, chair of West Midlands Heritage, expressed her enthusiasm about the project's progression, stating, 'We are now able to ensure that St Michael's Place will have a very homely future.' This restoration project highlights the collaborative effort between West Midlands Heritage and Warwick District Council to breathe new life into this historical site while addressing the community's housing needs.nnIn addition to creating much-needed affordable homes, the initiative also plans to foster community engagement through a heritage community assistant role and various local events focused on heritage craft skills. The project not only honors the historical significance of St Michael's Place but also aims to create a vibrant community hub that connects residents to their cultural heritage. As the project moves forward, it promises to blend history with modern needs, ensuring that this iconic site is not only preserved but also utilized for generations to come.
