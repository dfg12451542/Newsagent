As Live Aid celebrates its 40th anniversary, the contributions of Maurice Jones, a lesser-known but pivotal figure in the event’s success, are being highlighted. Jones, a rock promoter from Wednesbury, was instrumental in various aspects of the concert, including organizing the venue, backstage catering, and booking the artists. According to his daughter Nicola, his dedication to the job overshadowed any desire for recognition. 'He was very modest. His take on it was that he was there to do a job, he wasn’t there to be in the limelight. He did play an important part,' she remarked.nnNicola reminisced about attending the iconic Wembley concert with her father at the age of 15, recalling the memorable atmosphere. 'I was sitting in the royal box – a row behind David Bowie and two rows behind Princess Diana and Prince Charles. I was very lucky.' She noted the excitement in the crowd during Freddie Mercury's unforgettable performance and the impact it had on her understanding of Queen's enormous reach, saying, 'I sort of knew who Queen were but I wasn’t that familiar with them – it was then I realised what a huge band they were.'nnReflecting on the event, Nicola expressed pride in her father's contributions, stating, 'It will be nice if he is remembered in a small way.' This acknowledgment comes as the world revisits an event that not only raised funds for famine relief in Ethiopia but also solidified its place in music history.nnMaurice Jones's name, prominently featured at the top of the Live Aid tickets, serves as a reminder of the many unsung heroes behind historic events. He passed away in 2009, but his legacy continues through the memories shared by those who experienced the event, especially his daughter, who feels compelled to honor him for his behind-the-scenes work that played a significant role in the concert's success.
