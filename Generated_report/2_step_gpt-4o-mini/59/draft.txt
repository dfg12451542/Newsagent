Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla, who made history as the first Indian astronaut to visit the International Space Station (ISS), is on his way back to Earth after a groundbreaking mission. 'It has been an incredible journey. Even though now it is coming to an end, for you and me there is a long way to go. The journey of our human space mission is very long and difficult. But if we are determined, even the stars are attainable,' Shukla stated as his Axiom-4 (Ax-4) mission undocked from the ISS. A live broadcast captured the exciting moment, showcasing the spacecraft's departure from the orbiting lab on Monday.nnThe Ax-4 mission was initially expected to last two weeks but extended a few days longer. During their stay, the crew conducted 60 scientific experiments, including seven developed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (Isro). 'Even today we want to know how it looks from space. I'll tell you. From space, today's India looks ambitious. It looks fearless. It looks confident. It looks proud,' Shukla reflected on the view from above, highlighting the progress and aspirations of modern India.nnThe Indian Science Minister, Jitendra Singh, confirmed that the crew is scheduled to splash down in the ocean off the coast of California on July 15 at approximately 3:00 PM India time. A recovery vessel will be on hand to pick up the astronauts after their journey back.nnGroup Captain Shukhla's mission comes 41 years after Indian cosmonaut Rakesh Sharma flew aboard a Russian Soyuz spacecraft in 1984. With a significant investment of 5 billion rupees ($59 million) made by Isro to secure Shukla's seat and training, the knowledge gained during this mission is expected to bolster India’s future human spaceflight initiatives. Reflecting on his journey, Shukla previously expressed that the past year has been 'nothing short of transformative,' carrying the hopes and dreams of a billion Indians as he ventured into the final frontier.
