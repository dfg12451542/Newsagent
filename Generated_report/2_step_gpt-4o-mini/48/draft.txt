The Sudanese paramilitary group Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has intensified its hold on the war-torn city of el-Fasher, launching an aggressive assault that has left the area in turmoil. On July 13, 2025, RSF fighters stormed the city, capturing key locations, including a cattle market, a prison, and a military base. Witnesses reported that the attack lasted for seven hours, with RSF fighters broadcasting videos of their presence amid desolate stockyards. Siddig Omar, a local resident, described the harrowing scene, noting, 'One of the shells hit a civilian vehicle near my house, resulting in the death of five civilians who were inside the car.' nnThe efforts of local residents and volunteers to provide aid amidst this escalating violence is dire. Mathilde Vu from the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) described harrowing accounts of individuals fleeing on foot and donkeys to escape shelling, while many arrive in Tawila facing severe shortages of food and clean water. 'Right now, we are suffering deeply, and everyone around us is facing the same hardship,' lamented a resident of el-Fasher. The situation is exacerbated by a lack of medical supplies, with another resident stating unequivocally, 'When someone gets sick, we can't find any medicine or treatment.' nnThe clashes come nearly two years after a brutal power struggle between Sudan's army and RSF plunged the nation into civil war in April 2023. Despite a brief military counter-offensive that pushed RSF forces back beyond the city limits, shelling continued on Saturday, leaving the community in fear and desperation. With nearly 379,000 people fleeing to Tawila, where conditions are equally grim due to an outbreak of cholera and inadequate shelter, the need for urgent humanitarian assistance is more critical than ever. nnVu highlighted the challenges facing local volunteers. 'The funding is completely decreasing, and the consequence is that you can see it on the ground. People just rely on the solidarity of others.' As the plight of el-Fasher's residents intensifies, they are calling for immediate aid, expressing their desperate need for food and medical supplies as the ongoing conflict continues to wreak havoc on their lives.
