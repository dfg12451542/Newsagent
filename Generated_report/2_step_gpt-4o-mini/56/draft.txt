A runner participating in a grueling 55-mile mountain race in the Lake District was airlifted to safety after collapsing from the heat on Saturday afternoon. The incident occurred around 16:00 BST in Grisedale Valley, prompting a swift response from the Patterdale Mountain Rescue Team (PMRT). Witnesses reported that the runner became pale, suffered from vomiting, and ultimately lost consciousness, necessitating immediate medical attention.nnThe North West Ambulance Service quickly mobilized rescue efforts by contacting mountain rescue units and the coastguard, which dispatched a helicopter to extract the man from the remote location. He was successfully winched to safety and transported to the King George playing field in Patterdale, where he received further medical assessments. PMRT confirmed that the rescue operation lasted two and a half hours and involved a total of 11 team members alongside coastguard personnel.nnAfter being evaluated by a team doctor at the Patterdale Base, the runner was transferred to a local hospital for additional checks. This incident highlights the physical toll and risks associated with long-distance running, particularly under extreme weather conditions.nnIn a broader context, the situation comes on the heels of a series of running events, including ultramarathons, throughout the region being called off due to the unprecedented heat. These cancellations in Eryri, also known as Snowdonia, underline the increasing challenges faced by outdoor endurance athletes as climate patterns change, leading to more frequent extreme weather events.
