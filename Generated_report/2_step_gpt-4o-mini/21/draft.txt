In a climate of cautious optimism, businesses in Jersey are calling for robust collaboration with their Guernsey counterparts, as revealed in a recent economic confidence survey conducted by the Institute of Directors (IoD) Jersey. The survey drew responses from 70 members and highlighted a striking disparity: while 56% of participants expressed confidence in their own organizations, only 17% believed Jersey's overall economy was on solid footing. 'The survey provided a clear mandate for action,' stated Alex Ruddy, IoD Jersey Chair, emphasizing the need for strategic investment and policy clarity to bolster wider economic confidence.nnRuddy further articulated that collaboration across the Channel Islands, along with support for innovation and a focus on essential infrastructure and housing, are vital for Jersey’s future prosperity. Importantly, the report echoes findings from the Guernsey IoD’s recent survey, indicating that both islands face strikingly similar economic challenges. 'There is clear recognition from the business community in both islands that the best way to confront our mutual challenges is to work together more closely,' noted Richard Hemans, Guernsey's lead on economics.nnThe results of the survey have outlined key priorities for growth, including investment in infrastructure, attracting new industries, and addressing housing affordability. Respondents from Jersey expressed a desire to collaborate with Guernsey to tackle fiscal deficit strategies and to support entrepreneurship through tax incentives, youth talent attraction, and skills development. Although AI adoption is still in its infancy, with 70% of companies offering training and 33% developing strategies, the comprehensive findings suggest a forward-thinking approach to enhancing economic resilience in both islands.nnHistorically, the Channel Islands could benefit from significant tax revenue by addressing workforce participation and the challenges posed by an ageing population. The latest survey dovetails with previous reports indicating that while Guernsey's business leaders are more optimistic about their economic outlook compared to six months prior, their profit expectations remain muted. As both islands navigate these waters, the call for collaboration is more relevant than ever, with implications that could reshape the economic landscape across the Channel Islands.
