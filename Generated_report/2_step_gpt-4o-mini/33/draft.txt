A Guernsey woman, Jacky Meinke, heroically saved her husband Chris from drowning at Port Grat beach on Friday afternoon. The alarming incident unfolded around 4:30 PM BST when Mrs. Meinke noticed her husband floating face down in the water. 'I heard a splash and carried on swimming, thinking he was behind me,' she explained, realizing the severity of the situation only when she turned back. 'When I looked round his head was face down in the water.' Quickly reacting, she screamed his name, pulled his head out, and performed first aid, saying, 'I walloped his back and he spluttered and came to.' nnChris Meinke, 77, who later described the terrifying moment, revealing he was 'effectively dead' when pulled from the water, had collapsed while swimming. 'The hospital thinks I had vertigo; I just didn't know which way was up,' he said. The shocking episode serves to remind beachgoers about the sudden dangers posed by water, as Mrs. Meinke noted, 'I was amazed how quickly someone can get into difficulty.' Their fellow swimmer, Rosie Mclauchlan, played a crucial role in alerting others for help and subsequently assisted in rescuing Mr. Meinke.nnMr. Meinke was taken to the hospital where he spent the night for observation, but the cause of his distress remains uncertain. 'It was very scary, something happened to disorientate me, and we shall find out what it was,' he remarked. The couple expressed their gratitude to Ms. Mclauchlan and passersby who provided immediate support by calling emergency services and fetching a defibrillator.nnHistorically, water safety awareness has been pivotal on the island of Guernsey, which has witnessed over 100 drownings in the past decade as individuals navigated its challenging coastal waters. Local authorities, like Alan Jenner of the Guernsey Sports Commission, emphasize the importance of understanding aquatic safety, stating, 'It’s a vital part of learning for children, because we live on an island.' This incident underscores the necessity for persistent vigilance and education around water safety to protect lives, particularly in the popular bathing areas.
