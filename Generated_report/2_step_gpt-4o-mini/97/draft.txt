The Wurzels, a beloved British band known for their lively performances, were forced to end their gig abruptly on July 14, 2025, at the Party at the Palace festival in Wells, Somerset, due to severe health concerns among band members. The temperature soared to a sweltering 31C, leading to dangerous conditions on stage. Accordionist Tommy Banner recounted, 'Both Pete and I appear to have fully recovered from sun and heat exhaustion. Up on stage there was no air moving and we were facing straight into the direct, unobstructed rays of a very strong sun.'nnThe incident escalated when lead singer Pete Budd, who is set to celebrate his 85th birthday on July 18, had to leave the stage halfway through the set. Banner noticed his absence, noting, 'I was about to announce the last song and Pete wasn’t beside me where he normally is. He was in a bad way.' Medical assistance was promptly provided, with both band members receiving care from medical staff who arrived on site to evaluate their conditions.nnThis unfortunate episode highlights the risks associated with performing in extreme temperatures, especially for older individuals. NHS urgent care doctor Lorna Powell emphasized, 'When someone has a health condition like a weak heart, kidney problems, or diabetes, an extra load such as extreme heat can just deplete the body to the extent it can’t control that condition anymore.' Both Banner and Budd are said to have since recovered from their episodes of sunstroke and dizziness, though they were advised not to drive following the performance.nnThe Wurzels have built a legacy on the British music scene, known for their humorous and heartwarming songs. As they continue to entertain audiences, the health of their members, especially in the summer months, will remain a critical concern.
