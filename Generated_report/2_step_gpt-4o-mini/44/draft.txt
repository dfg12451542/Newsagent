Donegal's manager Jim McGuinness asserts that his squad has 'deserved the right' to face Kerry in the All-Ireland final, scheduled for July 27. After a comprehensive victory over Meath in the semi-finals, where Donegal dominated with a scoreline of 3-26 to 0-15, McGuinness expressed confidence in his team's capability. 'We know what we will get with Kerry; they are a team who know their way around Croke Park like the back of their hand,' McGuinness stated to BBC Sport. He acknowledged the significant challenge that Kerry poses, emphasizing the importance of maintaining focus during the two-week build-up to the final.nnDonegal's semi-final display was a testament to their dominance, with standout performances from Oisin Gallen, Ciaran Moore, and Conor O'Donnell, who each contributed goals that solidified their victory. 'Full credit to the boys; they have done a brilliant job,' McGuinness remarked, reflecting on the impressive performance that racked up a comfortable win. He is optimistic that the freshness noted in the group during training will translate onto the pitch.nnHistorically, Donegal seeks to reclaim a place among the elite in Gaelic football after a disappointing semi-final loss to Galway last year. McGuinness, who is in his first year back as manager, hopes to lead Donegal to their first All-Ireland title since 2012. The upcoming clash against Kerry, who convincingly overcame Tyrone in their semi-final, will be a defining moment for the team and a chance to revitalize their championship legacy.nnAs anticipation builds for the final, McGuinness is focused not only on the emotional engagement of the fans but also on ensuring that this spirit does not detract from their performance aspirations. 'We have to bring the highest level possible to our performance,' he noted, keenly aware that to succeed against a seasoned team like Kerry, preparation and intensity will be crucial.
