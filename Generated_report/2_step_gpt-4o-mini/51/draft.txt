Wales' Deputy First Minister and Secretary for Rural Affairs, Huw Irranca-Davies, has expressed his optimism regarding the upcoming launch of the Sustainable Farming Scheme (SFS), deeming it 'absolutely the right scheme' for the future of agriculture in the region. Speaking ahead of the scheme's unveiling, Irranca-Davies commented, 'I hope it will land to a good reception,' although he acknowledged that 'there will be the odd person' who may have preferred a different approach.nnThe SFS is positioned as a critical evolution of Wales' agricultural policies, with its second iteration set to be introduced next week. This update follows significant backlash from farming unions and communities, who previously protested the original scheme, voicing their concerns over inadequate representation in the planning process. 'I think there has been frustrations for a long time that lots of people in the farming community feel they haven’t been heard,' noted Plaid Cymru's Delyth Jewell MS, highlighting ongoing communication challenges between farmers and policymakers.nnFor over a year, extensive collaboration among various stakeholders, including environmental groups and the livestock and dairy sectors, has influenced the SFS’s development. Irranca-Davies pointed out that the scheme is 'the result of more than 12 months of intense work' aimed at achieving balance and sustainability in farming practices. The plan is intended to foster a farming model that prioritizes high animal welfare standards while simultaneously addressing environmental concerns, pivotal issues in today’s climate considerations. 'They want to see farming that produces good food to high animal welfare standards and also does the right thing for the environment,' he stated.nnIn a landscape where 80% of Wales' territory is under agricultural management, the government believes that farmers play an essential role in countering climate change and preserving natural ecosystems. However, the new scheme's detailed parameters, including payment rates for participating farmers, remain to be unveiled, with Irranca-Davies declining to provide specific insights until the formal announcement. Meanwhile, Conservative Senedd leader Darren Millar MS has urged for a democratic voting process to ensure the legitimacy of the policy, emphasizing the need for food security and a sustainable future for farmers. As Wales prepares for this transformative agricultural initiative, the success of the SFS will depend not only on its design but also on its acceptance by the farming community it aims to support.
