The North Norfolk Railway in Sheringham celebrated a significant milestone this weekend as a historic steam locomotive re-entered service for the first time after a nine-year refurbishment. This remarkable journey began with extensive efforts to restore the engine, culminating in its debut pulling passenger trains, a moment described by Alex Alder, a member of the restoration team, as 'absolutely incredible.' Alder, who was pivotal in the locomotive's recovery, recounted discovering the engine at a convent in Hertfordshire, likening the experience to 'hearing of a mythical Egyptian tomb.'nnThe 39-ton steam locomotive, now a proud addition to the North Norfolk Railway's fleet, will transport passengers on routes from Sheringham to Holt. Alder detailed the extensive restoration process, which required approximately £200,000 and the dedication of many volunteers over nearly a decade. 'But there it was in this convent in Hertfordshire, 39 tons of miracle,' he expressed, reflecting on the journey from obscurity to a working engine once again.nnGraham Hukins, another key figure at the railway, echoed the sentiment of excitement and nostalgia surrounding the locomotive's reintroduction. 'It was absolutely wonderful to see the engine running in Sheringham,' he stated, emphasizing the significance of the occasion as the railway also marks its 50th anniversary of operation. This weekend's event not only highlights the revival of a cherished piece of history but also strengthens the railway's ongoing commitment to preserving the heritage of steam transport in the region.
