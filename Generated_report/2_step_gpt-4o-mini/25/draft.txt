The Royal Observatory Greenwich has unveiled the shortlist for its annual ZWO Astronomy Photographer of the Year competition, showcasing spectacular cosmic imagery that captures the beauty of our universe. 'We believe that astrophotography is not only a way to record the cosmos, but also a way to inspire curiosity, foster education and build communities that transcend borders,' said Sam Wen, the founder and CEO of ZWO. Wen emphasized that 'everyone deserves a chance to connect with the Universe — and through our support, we hope to bring that experience to more people.'nnAmong this year’s contenders are stunning captures such as an eruption documented on November 7, 2024, which lasted about an hour, and a breathtaking image aptly resembling a pomegranate, taken by photographer Deqian Li during a six-day camping trip in Hongyuan County, China. Each photograph tells a unique story, such as the one capturing the passage of Comet C/2023 A3 snapped in Hawaii on October 12, 2024, an event that sparked significant anticipation among astronomers and enthusiasts alike.nnAdditionally, the shortlisted selections showcase mesmerizing scenes received under perfect conditions, like the golden sunset illuminating the Dolomite mountains in Italy, an image that highlights both the beauty of local landscapes and the grandeur of outer space. Those eager to view this visual feast can expect a special exhibition at the National Maritime Museum starting September 12, culminating in the announcement of the winners across the competition’s nine categories and the overall grand prize winner on September 11, 2025.nnThe ZWO Astronomy Photographer of the Year contest has a rich history, dating back to its inaugural event that welcomed submissions from photographers globally, both professional and amateur. As this competition continues to encourage creativity and a passion for the cosmos, it promises to captivate a new generation of astrophotographers and enthusiasts alike.
