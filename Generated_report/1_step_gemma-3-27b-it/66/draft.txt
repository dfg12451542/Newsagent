Warwick, UK – More than £2.8 million has been secured to transform two 15th-century buildings, formerly part of a historic leper hospital, into much-needed affordable homes. The St Michael’s Place project, located in Saltisford, Warwick, will see the “rescue and reuse” of the Master’s House and St Michael’s Chapel, structures that have remained derelict for over half a century. This development marks a significant step towards preserving a vital piece of Warwick’s history while addressing the ongoing need for affordable housing.nnThe award, provided by the National Lottery Heritage Fund, will facilitate a collaborative effort between West Midlands Heritage and Warwick District Council. The project aims to restore and convert the buildings into affordable rented homes, breathing new life into the site.  The buildings, once integral to the treatment of individuals afflicted with leprosy, have suffered from years of neglect but retain significant architectural and historical value.nnCouncillor Helen Adkins highlighted the importance of the restoration, stating, “We will be able to preserve the legacy of the site and rescue these long-neglected historic buildings, offering them a sustainable future.”  The revitalization will not only provide housing but also safeguard a tangible link to the past.nnThe project’s success is a testament to the dedication of various parties involved. Corinne Brazier, chair of West Midlands Heritage, credited her colleagues and the local community support for reaching this “important delivery stage.”  The collaborative approach ensures that the restoration respects the historical context while also addressing practical needs.nnBeyond the provision of homes, St Michael’s Place will also contribute to community engagement by creating a heritage community assistant role, offering opportunities to learn heritage craft skills, and hosting a series of local events and activities. As Corinne Brazier noted, “We are now able to ensure that St Michael’s Place will have a very homely future.”  The project ensures that the site's history will be accessible and celebrated for generations to come.
