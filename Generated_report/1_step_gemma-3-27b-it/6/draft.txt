WIMBLEDON, ENGLAND – In a stunning display of dominance, Iga Swiatek of Poland has secured her first Wimbledon Ladies’ Singles title, defeating Amanda Anisimova 6-0, 6-0 in a remarkably swift 57-minute match on Saturday. The victory marks a full circle moment for the 24-year-old, who first tasted success at the All England Club winning the junior Wimbledon title in 2018. “It seems super surreal,” Swiatek remarked, visibly moved by the occasion. “Honestly, I didn’t even dream of winning Wimbledon because it was way too far.”nnSwiatek's powerful groundstrokes and strategic play proved too much for Anisimova, who was also competing in her first Wimbledon final. The Polish star controlled the match from the outset, showcasing a level of play that left her opponent with little opportunity. Despite having already claimed multiple Grand Slam titles, Swiatek expressed her surprise at the win, stating, “I feel like I’m an experienced player, having won other Slams, but I didn’t expect this.”nnOn the men’s side, Jannik Sinner emerged victorious after a hard-fought contest, adding another Grand Slam title to his growing collection. Following the match, Sinner offered gracious words to his opponent, Carlos Alcaraz, acknowledging their competitive spirit. “It is so difficult to play you, but we have a great relationship off the court,” Sinner said. He also expressed confidence in Alcaraz’s future, adding, “Keep going, keep pushing, you are going to hold this trophy many times - you already have twice!”nnThe tournament also saw success for Great Britain’s Alfie Hewett, though ultimately he was defeated by Tokito Oda in the Wheelchair Singles final. Despite the loss, Hewett expressed immense gratitude for the overwhelming support he received. “When you lose, to have this amount of love and support, this sort of atmosphere doesn’t happen to us on a regular basis,” he shared. Hewett also highlighted the increasing recognition of wheelchair tennis: “It is really incredible the amount of support wheelchair tennis has got.”nnBeyond the singles championships, victories were claimed by Britain’s Julian Cash and Lloyd Glasspool in Men's Doubles, Russia’s Veronika Kudermetova and Belgium’s Elise Mertens in Women’s Doubles, and The Netherlands’ Sem Verbeek and Chechia’s Katerina Sin in Mixed Doubles. The Wheelchair Doubles title went to Martin de la Puente and Ruben Spaargaren, who defeated British champions Alfie Hewett and Gordon Reid.
