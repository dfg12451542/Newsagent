El-Fasher, Sudan – The Sudanese paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) stormed the besieged city of el-Fasher on Friday, engaging in seven hours of intense fighting before being pushed back by the army on Saturday morning. This marks the first time RSF fighters have entered the city in significant numbers since the beginning of a 15-month siege, a struggle for dominance over the key western Darfur city.  The RSF briefly seized control of a cattle market, a prison, and a military base, broadcasting videos of their presence in the now-empty stockyards.nnThe city, described by Mathilde Vu of the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) as a “death trap,” remains under immense pressure.  “What we're hearing is stories of horror and terror and weekly shelling, attacks on civilian infrastructure,” Vu stated, highlighting the ongoing suffering of residents. Despite the army’s success in repelling the initial assault, RSF shelling, utilizing drones, continued throughout Saturday, according to resident Siddig Omar, who reported that a shell struck a civilian vehicle, killing five people.nnThe humanitarian situation within el-Fasher is dire, with residents facing severe shortages of food, medicine, and essential supplies. Residents speaking to BBC Arabic described a desperate struggle for survival.  “Right now, we are suffering deeply, and everyone around us is facing the same hardship,” one resident said. Another lamented, “There is no bread, no food, and no work to be found. Even if you have money, there's nothing available in the markets to buy.”  The lack of medical care is particularly concerning, with residents reporting a complete absence of medicine in hospitals.nnBeyond el-Fasher, the impact of the conflict is widespread.  Nearly 379,000 people have fled to Tawila, where they face a cholera outbreak and the threat of heavy rains destroying their makeshift shelters.  Aid organizations are struggling to provide assistance as funding dwindles.  “The funding is completely decreasing and the consequence is that you can see it on the ground,” Vu explained.  She added that people are relying on the solidarity of others, sharing even meager food supplies.  The crisis underscores the urgent need for increased humanitarian support to alleviate the suffering of civilians caught in the crossfire.
