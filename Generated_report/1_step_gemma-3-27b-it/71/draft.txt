Fifa has faced sharp criticism from the world players’ union, Fifpro, following the recent Club World Cup, with accusations of prioritizing revenue over player welfare. In a strongly worded statement, Fifpro’s president, Sergio Marchi, likened Fifa’s approach to that of the Roman emperor Nero, suggesting the tournament was a superficial “bread and circuses” spectacle masking deeper inequalities and risks to player health.nnMarchi accused Fifa of creating a “fiction” around the tournament, alleging that it was “promoted… without dialogue, sensitivity, and respect for those who sustain the game.” He further stated that the tournament was held under “unacceptable conditions” that put players’ physical integrity at risk, and warned against a repeat occurrence at next year’s Fifa World Cup.  Fifpro also revealed they were excluded from a crucial player welfare meeting hosted by Fifa president Gianni Infantino prior to the Club World Cup final.nnThe criticism stems from concerns over an increasingly congested football schedule and a perceived lack of consideration for the physical and mental strain placed on players. Marchi emphasized that Fifpro has repeatedly warned Fifa about the dangers of this overloaded schedule, accusing the governing body of adopting a “unilateral, authoritarian” approach driven solely by “economic profitability” rather than player sustainability.  He argued, “We cannot continue to play with the health of players to fuel a marketing machine.”nnThis isn’t the first time Fifa’s Club World Cup has drawn fire.  Last year, Fifpro filed a legal complaint against Fifa, claiming the expansion of the tournament abused European competition law and further burdened the already packed fixture list. Maheta Molango, chief executive of the Professional Footballers' Association, previously stated the Club World Cup was “devaluing football” due to player fatigue and the resulting drop in performance quality.  The current rebuke from Fifpro underscores a growing tension between governing bodies and the players who are central to the sport’s success.
