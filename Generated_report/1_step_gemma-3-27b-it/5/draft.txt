Trust in policing within Peterborough’s Millfield community has demonstrably increased following the implementation of the “Alliance” project, a collaborative effort between Peterborough City Council and Cambridgeshire Police. The initiative, focused on combating organised crime along Lincoln Road, has yielded significant results, according to council reports released this week.nnPeterborough City Council reports a remarkable “700% increase in community intelligence reporting” since the project’s inception, suggesting a growing willingness among residents to cooperate with law enforcement. This surge in trust correlates with tangible outcomes – the council reports the seizure of 700,000 illegal cigarettes, 20,000 vapes, and £115,000 in cash within the targeted area.  These successes indicate a disruption of criminal networks operating in Millfield. New drug needle bins were also installed as part of the project’s broader effort to improve community wellbeing.nnHowever, the Alliance project wasn’t without its challenges. A recent council report highlights difficulties in fully engaging with local community groups, suggesting that building relationships and fostering consistent collaboration proved complex. Despite this, the council and police are exploring continued funding to maintain the project’s momentum within Millfield, recognising the benefits it has brought to the area.nnWhile Cambridgeshire Police acknowledge the positive impact, replicating the Alliance model city-wide is unlikely in the immediate future. Insp Sam Tucker stated, “We can definitely use some of the concepts in there but to use this initiative in its entirety is unlikely,” indicating the project’s intensity and need for long-term sustainability make broad implementation difficult.  The focus remains on maintaining the project’s presence in Millfield while assessing the feasibility of adapting elements for use elsewhere in Peterborough.
