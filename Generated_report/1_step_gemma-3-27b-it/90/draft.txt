Police are investigating a deliberate fire that caused extensive damage to a Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA) club in west Belfast over the weekend. Authorities have confirmed the blaze, which destroyed facilities at the club, was started intentionally around 18:20 BST on Saturday and are appealing for information from the public.nnThe incident has drawn immediate condemnation from local political representatives and community figures, highlighting the significant role the GAA club plays in the area.  The club provides vital facilities and programs for young people, making the act of vandalism particularly disheartening.  According to Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI) Insp Dalton, the fire caused “extensive damage” to the club’s premises.nnInsp Dalton stated, “It’s absolutely unacceptable that a community organisation should be targeted in this way.” Belfast city councillor Arder Carson echoed this sentiment, calling the fire a “blatant act of vandalism” and expressing deep despair.  “It definitely fills you with despair and some anger that a club that does so much for the local community… has been subject to such an attack,” Carson said.nnThe timing of the attack is particularly devastating, added Carson, as the club was “in the heart” of preparing for its children’s summer camps and the upcoming championship season.  He further emphasized the recklessness of setting a fire during a period of dry weather, especially in a rural, heavily wooded area, stating,  “Setting fire outdoors at any time is reckless but this level is just beyond reckless.” Carson added that “On the period of dry weather we have had, to set fires in an area like that which is rural and totally green is just beyond reckless.”nnThis latest incident follows a similar incident in County Antrim where a GAA club suffered significant damage after a fire destroyed a scoreboard, shop, and signage, further illustrating the vulnerability of these community hubs to targeted attacks.  Police are urging anyone with information relating to the west Belfast fire to come forward.
