Swansea made history this weekend as it hosted the UK’s first ever edition of the IRONMAN Pro Series, drawing elite athletes and thousands of competitors to the Welsh coast on July 13th. With soaring temperatures – exceeding 28°C in the air and 24°C in the water – organizers made the unprecedented decision for the UK series to hold a non-wetsuit swim for professional athletes, due to exceeding the legal limit. The challenging conditions did little to deter fierce competition, with defending champion Harry Palmer securing back-to-back victories in a time of 3:51:18, narrowly beating France’s Leon Chevalier by just 49 seconds.nnPalmer, reflecting on the race, acknowledged the demanding climate. “It was hot, and I do feel for all the age-groupers who are out there today, because they’ve got much harder conditions than we did, so fair play to everyone racing,” he told BBC Sport Wales. Despite a less-than-ideal swim, Palmer found himself in good company on the bike. “I didn’t have the best swim, I’ll be honest…I came out with the likes of Will Crudgington…Kristian Høgenhaug, who was one of the guys I was keeping an eye on. He’s an epic rider,” Palmer explained.nnOn the women’s side, Kat Matthews, the overall Ironman Pro Series 2024 winner, claimed victory in Swansea with a time of 4:20:37. Having last won in the city in 2022, Matthews described feeling like a “completely different person” and noted a shift in the event itself. “I didn’t have as much deficit coming out of the swim as I expected…I was happy to see them quite quickly and pull them back,” she said, highlighting her strong performance on the bike.nnBeyond the elite competition, the event saw over half of the age-group field competing in their first IRONMAN 70.3 race.  Thousands of spectators lined the 70-mile course, providing enthusiastic support to all athletes. Race Director Rebecca Sutherland celebrated the success of the event, stating, “It’s always great fun when we go racing in Swansea…It was another incredible race.” Palmer himself echoed the sentiment, praising the Welsh crowds: “My favourite part is the support…They just know how to put on an event and how to support. I’ll keep coming back to Welsh events because of that.”nnThe event is already slated for a return to Swansea next summer, promising another thrilling competition and cementing the city's place as a key location on the IRONMAN Pro Series calendar.
