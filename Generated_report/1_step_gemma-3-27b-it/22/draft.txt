Wythall, England – A local PE teacher is celebrating the historic Wimbledon victory of former student Lloyd Glasspool, who, alongside partner Julian Cash, secured the men’s doubles title on Saturday. The pair’s win marks the first time an all-British duo has claimed the championship at the All England Club in 89 years, a feat that has sparked immense pride within the Wythall community. nnJoe Maidment, a teacher at Woodrush High School, described his astonishment at Glasspool’s achievement. “To think a student from Woodrush High School in Wythall can win at Wimbledon – it’s just amazing,” he said, adding that he had travelled to London to watch Glasspool’s second-round match and found his performance throughout the tournament “inspirational.” Mr. Maidment noted that Glasspool, a member of the Warwickshire Lawn Tennis Association, had even sent a video message to the school ahead of their recent sports awards.nnMr. Maidment emphasized the broader significance of Glasspool’s success. “It should be a message to everyone in the West Midlands to follow their dreams and see what they can achieve,” he stated. The teacher also shared a humorous anecdote about playing tennis against Glasspool during his school days, recalling a decisive 6-0, 6-0 defeat when his former student was still in year eight.  nnBeyond the local celebration, Glasspool’s victory is a landmark moment for British tennis.  The triumph of Cash and Glasspool echoes a historical period of British dominance at Wimbledon, recalling a time when British players consistently competed for and won major titles. Glasspool himself acknowledged his journey, playfully reminding Mr. Maidment of a past “thrashing” and crediting the early competition with boosting his confidence.  He expressed gratitude for the support from Woodrush School and the wider community, calling the Wimbledon title “the icing on the cake,” alongside previous wins at Queens and Eastbourne, effectively securing “the triple crown.”
