Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla, the first Indian astronaut to journey to the International Space Station (ISS), is currently en route back to Earth, marking a momentous occasion for India’s space program. The Axiom-4 (Ax-4) mission, a collaboration between NASA, ISRO, ESA, and SpaceX, undocked from the ISS on Monday with its four-member crew, including Shukla, and is expected to splash down off the coast of California within the next 24 hours. Indian Science Minister Jitendra Singh confirmed the scheduled splashdown for July 15th at approximately 15:00 India time (09:30 GMT).nnShukla’s historic flight echoes the pioneering journey of Rakesh Sharma, who became the first Indian in space in 1984 aboard a Russian Soyuz spacecraft – a gap of 41 years before India sent another citizen beyond Earth’s atmosphere. The Ax-4 mission, initially planned for a two-week stay, was extended slightly, allowing the crew to complete a comprehensive set of 60 scientific experiments, including seven specifically designed by the Indian Space Research Organisation (Isro). Isro invested 5 billion rupees ($59m; £43m) in securing Shukla’s seat and training, recognizing the invaluable experience he will bring to India’s future human spaceflight endeavors.nnDuring his time on the ISS, Shukla provided a unique perspective on India from space, describing it as “ambitious, fearless, confident and proud.” He remarked, “From space, today’s India looks better than the rest of the world,” embodying a sense of national pride and optimism.  Speaking about the broader implications of the mission, Shukla powerfully stated, “It has been an incredible journey. Even though now it is coming to an end, for you and me there is a long way to go. The journey of our human space mission is very long and difficult. But if we are determined, even the stars are attainable.”nnBefore his departure, Shukla expressed the transformative nature of the past year and highlighted the weight of expectation he carried. He spoke of representing the “hopes and dreams of a billion hearts,” requesting the prayers of all Indians for the mission’s success. This return not only signifies the completion of a groundbreaking mission but also represents a pivotal step towards India's ambitious goals for independent human spaceflight and a brighter future in space exploration.
