Lord's witnessed a dramatic fourth day of the second Test between England and India, as Brydon Carse’s inspired bowling display breathed life back into the home side’s campaign. Carse claimed crucial wickets, pinning Karun Nair and captain Shubman Gill leg before, shifting the momentum after a challenging batting session for England. The day concluded with Ben Stokes dismissing nightwatchman Akash Deep, leaving India 58-4 and 135 runs short of their target of 193, setting up a thrilling final day.nnEarlier, England’s batting lineup struggled against the relentless Indian attack, with Harry Brook, Joe Root, and Ben Stokes all falling victim to the disciplined bowling. India’s seamers were particularly effective in the morning session, and all-rounder Washington Sundar proved a handful, dismissing both past and present England captains. “We’d have loved to have about nine wickets [in hand],” Sundar remarked confidently, adding, “It is what it is and we'll definitely win the game.”nnEngland batting coach Marcus Trescothick acknowledged the team’s difficult position, stating, “We would have loved 250 and beyond but we would have taken 190 and our chance to bowl on that pitch and it'll come down to who holds the pressure best.” The pitch, described as a ‘snake pit’, contributed to the chaotic nature of the day, with tension rising between the teams following a minor incident.  The exciting turn of events had BBC chief cricket commentator Jonathan Agnew remarking, “We’re all a bit hoarse after that. It was absolutely gripping. Even World Cup finals don't quite generate that kind of madness.”nnWhile India’s bowlers dominated, particularly Sundar, the late surge from Carse dramatically altered the complexion of the match. Sundar emphasized the importance of every wicket, exclaiming, “All four of them, honestly. Lord's Test, big Test, it's going to be amazing when we win the game and go 2-1 up in the series so every wicket was very very crucial.” Akash Deep's impressive match haul of 6-99 solidifies his impact on the game, while the stage is now set for a captivating finish on Monday as England attempt to salvage victory.
