Donegal manager Jim McGuinness has declared his team has “deserved the right” to compete for the All-Ireland title, following their decisive 3-26 to 0-15 semi-final victory over Meath. The Ulster champions dominated at Croke Park, securing their place in the final against Kerry on July 27th, thanks to second-half goals from Oisin Gallen, Ciaran Moore, and Conor O'Donnell. nnThe comprehensive win was a testament to Donegal’s performance, establishing a five-point lead by halftime and extending it dramatically in the second half. While acknowledging the formidable challenge presented by Kerry, McGuinness expressed confidence in his team's preparation and ability to compete. nn“We know what we will get with Kerry, they are a team who know their way around Croke Park like the back of their hand,” McGuinness told BBC Sport, recognising Kerry's experience at the iconic venue. Despite this, he was adamant about his team’s readiness, stating, “They know how to win an All-Ireland so it’s a massive, massive challenge but we’ve deserved the right to play that game and I’m delighted they’ve got that opportunity.”nnMcGuinness acknowledged the importance of the upcoming two weeks, urging his team to stay focused as they prepare for the final. “Please god, now they can take it,” he added. He also praised the team’s performance, adding, “Full credit to the boys, they have done a brilliant job, and we got over the line today.” The manager stressed the need to maintain intensity and focus on preparation, balancing the excitement for the fans with the demands of the championship decider.
