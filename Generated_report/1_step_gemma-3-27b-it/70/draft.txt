Oxfordshire, UK – A mother, identified only as Lauren, has shared a deeply concerning account of her child’s struggle to integrate into mainstream schooling, highlighting a growing issue of school anxiety and absence among young people. Lauren described how her child’s distress became so acute they expressed suicidal ideation, a stark illustration of the emotional toll taken by difficulties in adapting to the school environment. The Department for Education acknowledged a 'broken system' inherited, outlining a commitment to address escalating rates of school absenteeism and its impact on children’s futures.nnSpeaking about the challenges, Lauren revealed the severity of her child’s anxiety prevented regular attendance.  “Even if they’re having a really bad week, they’ll go to that session and they’ll come out really buoyant and happy,” she said, referring to specially designed online sessions her child now attends.  The Department for Education echoed the seriousness of the issue, stating, “The case for tackling the epidemic of school absence could not be clearer: improved grades, higher wages, better life chances.”  They emphasize a collaborative approach – government, schools, parents, and children – is crucial to reversing dwindling attendance rates.nnFor a significant period, Lauren explained, her child was only able to manage “half an hour in school each day,” and frequently required prolonged reassurance.  She recounted instances of waiting with her child at school for up to two hours, witnessing firsthand their overwhelming inability to cope. Distraught and questioning her own parenting, Lauren expressed, “Am I imagining these things? Am I a bad parent? You question all those things when, deep down, you know your child and you know they’re unhappy.” She poignantly described the situation as “the frustration of them not having those opportunities to fit into a normal education system is really harrowing.”nnThe innovative approach of online sessions, however, has brought about a significant positive change. Lauren reports her child “literally leaps” to attend these sessions, experiencing a notable boost in mood and confidence afterward. Heather Richards, the head teacher overseeing the program, acknowledged that missing school can create a “vicious circle”, but affirmed the initiative has demonstrably improved attendance figures and fostered a stronger sense of connection amongst students. Richards added students are also showing “a stronger connection with some of the children in their year group” and building confidence.nnThe Department for Education detailed that their plan for change includes attendance mentors, free breakfast clubs, expanded mental health support, increased investment in family support services, and a greater focus from Ofsted on attendance rates, demonstrating a multifaceted attempt to tackle the root causes of school avoidance and create a more inclusive and supportive educational experience for all students.
