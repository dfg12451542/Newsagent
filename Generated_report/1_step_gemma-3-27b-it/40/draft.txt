British boxing trainer Dave Coldwell has voiced his disapproval of Manny Pacquiao’s upcoming comeback fight, stating he will not be watching the bout. The eight-division world champion is set to return to the ring on Saturday, a decision Coldwell views as detrimental to the sport. “I won’t be watching Manny Pacquiao’s comeback fight on Saturday,” Coldwell stated, adding, “It feels like a shortcut. A nod to nostalgia rather than merit, and that's unfair to fighters who are working their way up properly.”nnColdwell emphasized his deep respect for Pacquiao's legendary career, acknowledging his status as a global icon. “I realise that might sound harsh given he is one of the sport's all-time greats, an eight-division world champion. I have immense respect for what he achieved in his prime. He's a global icon, a true legend,” he explained. However, he believes the circumstances surrounding the return diminish the accomplishments of those who are diligently climbing the ranks.nnWhat particularly concerns Coldwell is the manner of Pacquiao’s return. Unlike typical comeback trails that involve tune-up fights to regain ring rust, Pacquiao is immediately challenging a current contender. “That's the part that really shocked me when I heard of Pacquiao's return. Usually, a fighter coming back from retirement eases into the sport with tune-up fights,” said Coldwell. “Not Pacquiao. He's skipping all that and going right to the front of the queue.”nnThis direct jump to a significant fight, according to Coldwell, sets a concerning precedent within the sport. He expressed his concerns to WBC President Mauricio Sulaiman, though the conversation was met with disagreement. His sentiment reflects a wider unease about boxing legends returning to competition without adhering to standard career progression, potentially overshadowing the efforts of actively building fighters. Though he acknowledges Pacquiao’s stature, Coldwell privately hopes for a loss, ensuring the legend retires with his legacy intact.
