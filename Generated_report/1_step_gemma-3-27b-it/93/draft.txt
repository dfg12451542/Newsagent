A permanent memorial to the beloved Sycamore Gap tree has been unveiled this week, while ongoing issues continue to plague the new Tyne and Wear Metro fleet. The unveiling at the Sill National Landscape Discovery Centre allows the public to connect with a tangible piece of the iconic tree felled by vandalism two years ago. Simultaneously, three of the newly introduced Metro trains were again removed from service due to persistent air-conditioning leaks, mirroring problems experienced during June’s heatwave.nnThe memorial offers a poignant opportunity for reflection on the loss of the landmark, previously famed for its appearance in the film *Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves*. Visitors will now be able to touch a section of the tree’s trunk, providing a physical connection to the natural icon. The Sycamore Gap tree stood for centuries and its sudden destruction in September 2023 sparked outrage and grief across the nation. nnArtist Charlie Winney, who visited the memorial, described the experience as deeply moving. “I was blown away by how huggable it is,” Winney stated, highlighting the emotional impact of being able to physically interact with a part of the lost tree. The memorial aims to provide a lasting tribute and a space for remembrance.nnBeyond the memorial and ongoing Metro troubles, Newcastle University announced that TV historian David Olusoga, a Gateshead native, will be awarded an honorary degree this summer. Olusoga’s work has been instrumental in bringing a more nuanced understanding of British history to a wider audience, earning him recognition as a leading voice in historical scholarship.nnThe contrasting news stories reflect a week of both loss and recognition in the Tyneside region, with the remembrance of a felled landmark alongside the celebration of a local historian’s achievements, all while everyday transport challenges persist.
