NORTH BERWICK, Scotland – Rory McIlroy expressed contentment despite finishing just shy of victory at the Scottish Open, concluding the tournament at 13-under par after a week highlighted by 19 birdies. While a win eluded him, McIlroy remains optimistic and well-prepared as he shifts his focus to The Open Championship at Royal Portrush, set to begin this weekend. The Scottish Open concluded on Sunday, and McIlroy immediately turned his attention towards the upcoming major.nnMcIlroy acknowledged the challenging conditions at North Berwick, particularly the windy weather which impacted scoring. Despite this, he highlighted the positives in his performance. “No frustration; it’s been a great week,” McIlroy stated, emphasizing his satisfaction with his overall game. He also noted his control over ball flight and the number of scoring opportunities he created, even if the trophy ultimately wasn’t his. nnReflecting on his final round, McIlroy pointed to subtle issues with the greens as a potential factor in missed putts. “I feel like the greens got a little bumpy. I had some good putts that just didn’t go in, whether they were mis-reads or poor speed,” he explained. However, he downplayed any significant disappointment, stressing that he achieved his goals for the week. nnLooking ahead to Royal Portrush, McIlroy is eager to take to the course, anticipating the challenge of a major championship on familiar ground. “But I feel like I’ve gotten out of this week what I wanted. I’m looking forward to getting to Portrush tonight and getting out on to the golf course early tomorrow,” McIlroy said. With The Open Championship commencing on July 13th, McIlroy appears confident and ready to contend, despite the near miss at the Scottish Open. The upcoming championship is poised to be a landmark event, potentially coinciding with Scotland's first heatwave of the year.
