A memorial bench dedicated to Sophie Lancaster, a 20-year-old woman tragically murdered in 2007 due to her association with the Goth subculture, has been temporarily removed from Whitby’s West Cliff to make way for a new Ferris wheel. The bench serves as a poignant tribute to Lancaster, who died as a result of a brutal attack in Bacup, Lancashire, and has become a place of reflection for many who visit Whitby.nnThe Sophie Lancaster Foundation expressed shock at the relocation, stating they were not consulted about the decision. “It was a shock to learn that the bench has been removed to locate a giant sightseeing wheel. Unfortunately, we weren’t informed or consulted about this decision,” a charity spokesperson said. For Sylvia Lancaster, Sophie’s mother, the bench held a particularly deep significance as “a tangible connection to Sophie.”nnNorth Yorkshire Council has responded to the criticism, explaining the big wheel was “intended as a respectful, exciting addition to the town.” A council spokesperson assured the public that the memorial would be restored to its original location by August 4th. Councillor Mark Crane further elaborated that the wheel was part of a wider tourism strategy, offering “stunning views across the harbour and towards the town’s famous abbey.”nnThe decision to move the bench has sparked local outrage, with Whitby’s mayor, Councillor Sandra Turner, calling it “a serious oversight.” The council has apologised for the lack of consultation and pledged to improve engagement with local communities on future projects. Despite the initial controversy, the council highlights overwhelmingly positive feedback from local businesses, who anticipate the wheel will boost foot traffic.nnThe Sophie Lancaster Foundation plans to hold a small ceremony to rededicate the bench at the Tomorrow's Ghosts Festival on November 1st, expressing their joy at its eventual return to West Cliff. The foundation also acknowledged the outpouring of support from the alternative community following the incident, demonstrating the lasting affection for Sophie and the impact of her memory.
