Nuseirat, Gaza – A Palestinian father is expressing outrage and grief after an Israeli airstrike Sunday killed his son, Abdullah, and nine others, including five other children, while they waited in line for water in the central Gaza Strip. Mahmoud Abdul Rahman Ahmed recounted how Abdullah was “searching for a sip of water” when he headed with the family’s jerrycans to a distribution point in the Nuseirat camp, a common and desperate necessity for residents enduring the ongoing conflict and severe shortages. nnAccording to Ahmed, the area was a gathering point for displaced people and those simply exhausted by the war and the long-standing blockade of Gaza. “The children, Abdullah among them, stood in a queue with empty stomachs, empty jerrycans, and thirsty lips,” he stated. Moments after the group assembled, he alleges, the area was bombed without warning. Ahmed believes the strike was intentionally meant to send a message of control, denying even basic necessities like drinking water to the population. nnGraphic video footage verified by the BBC depicts the chaotic aftermath of the strike, showcasing destroyed structures and scattered yellow jerrycans. Witnesses and first responders worked frantically to pull survivors from the rubble, while others lay motionless. Al-Awda hospital in Nuseirat confirmed the deaths of ten people, six of whom were children, and reported sixteen injuries. The Israeli military acknowledged targeting a Palestinian Islamic Jihad “terrorist” in the area, but attributed the civilian casualties to a “technical error” with the munition, stating it fell dozens of meters from the intended target and that the incident is under review.nnThe strike comes just days after another tragic incident in the nearby town of Deir al-Balah, where ten children and three women were killed while waiting for nutritional supplements. UNRWA’s acting Gaza director, Sam Rose, described the scenes of children queuing for water as a daily occurrence, highlighting the desperation for basic resources. “Every death is a tragedy. This one is particularly emblematic, given the circumstances in which it took place,” Rose said, adding that these deaths represent only a fraction of the children lost in Gaza since the start of the war, estimating that, on average, a classroom full of children have been killed each day.  Catherine Russell, executive director of UNICEF, has called for an urgent review of Israel’s rules of engagement and full compliance with international humanitarian law.
