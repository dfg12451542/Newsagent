Wales’ new Sustainable Farming Scheme (SFS) is “absolutely the right scheme,” according to Minister for Rural Affairs and the Environment Huw Irranca-Davies, despite previous iterations facing strong opposition from the farming community. The announcement of the final scheme, including payment rates for farmers, is expected next week, following widespread protests and rejection of the initial proposal. The revised scheme aims to balance food production with environmental sustainability, a critical issue given that 80% of Wales’ land is managed by farmers and their critical role in addressing climate change and declining ecosystems.nnIrranca-Davies expressed optimism that the scheme would be well-received, acknowledging that not everyone will fully agree with the changes. “Speaking ahead of its launch, I was 'hopeful it will land to a good reception,' even though he conceded 'there will be the odd person' who wished it had been done differently,” he stated. The final version represents over a year of collaborative work involving farming unions, sector representatives, and environmental groups, designed to strike the right balance between competing interests.nnDetails of the scheme remain largely undisclosed, though Irranca-Davies hinted at its innovative nature.  “It’s going to be unlike anything else that’s been delivered in the UK,” he claimed, emphasizing a “whole farm approach and a whole nation approach” focused on delivering benefits for the Welsh people. The minister underscored the importance of farming that provides high-quality food, prioritizes animal welfare, and protects the environment – all objectives Welsh consumers reportedly want to see. nnWhile an impact assessment of the final scheme remains unpublished, Irranca-Davies indicated a positive outlook. He anticipates that the scheme will be beneficial for both “viable farm businesses and environmental benefits as well.” He also assured stakeholders that mitigations will be in place to ensure a just transition towards a greener economy, acknowledging “real subtleties” within the scheme.  However, concerns persist amongst opposition parties.nnPlaid Cymru’s Delyth Jewell MS voiced frustrations that the agricultural community hasn’t felt adequately heard throughout the process, stating, “We have to make sure – these are people who sustain our lives – we need to make sure their lives are made sustainable too.” Meanwhile, Conservative Senedd leader Darren Millar MS called for a parliamentary vote on the policy to ensure “democratic legitimacy,” and emphasized the need for food security to be at the heart of the plan, ensuring a sustainable future for Welsh farmers.”
