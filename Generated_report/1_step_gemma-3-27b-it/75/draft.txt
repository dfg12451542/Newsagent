NORTH BERWICK, Scotland – In a stunning upset at the Renaissance Club, Chris Gotterup, ranked 158th in the world, held off a late charge from Rory McIlroy to win the 2025 Scottish Open. The 25-year-old American carded a final-round 66, finishing at 15 under par, two shots ahead of McIlroy and England’s Marco Penge. The victory marks Gotterup’s second PGA Tour title and secures his coveted debut appearance at The Open Championship next week at Royal Portrush.nnThe final round began as a two-horse race between Gotterup and McIlroy, but McIlroy’s challenge faded on the back nine. Despite a strong start, the world number two struggled to find birdies, ultimately finishing with a disappointing score.  “It was pretty windy and hard to get the ball super close,” McIlroy admitted after the round. “I gave myself plenty of chances coming down the stretch. Chris played a great round of golf. He was so solid.”nnGotterup, playing in only his second competitive event on a links course, demonstrated remarkable composure throughout the day. He overcame an early bogey to deliver a stellar performance, punctuated by key birdies that established and maintained his lead.  “I just hung in there tight and tried to keep it together,” said a visibly emotional Gotterup after his victory. nnWhile McIlroy shared second place with Penge, the day belonged to Gotterup, who earned a £1.2 million prize and the prestige of a major championship berth.  Matt Fitzpatrick and Nicolai Hojgaard finished a further shot back, while Justin Rose delivered the low round of the day with a bogey-free 63.  “It’s amazing. It’s all hitting me. It's just so cool. I played really well this week and I knew today was going to be tough,” Gotterup reflected.nnOvercoming the pressure of playing alongside the popular McIlroy, Gotterup added,  “It was a pleasure to be out there with Rory and obviously all the support he gets is amazing, but it was nice to kind of ruin that a little bit.”  The American’s triumph represents a significant breakthrough, signaling the arrival of a promising talent on the global golf stage, and as a bonus, ensures two additional players – Dane Hojgaard and Germany's Matti Schmid – will also be teeing it up at Royal Portrush.
