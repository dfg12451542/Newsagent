Businesses in Jersey and Guernsey are urging greater collaboration between the Channel Islands to address shared economic challenges, according to a new survey released by the Institute of Directors (IoD) Jersey. The findings, based on responses from 70 members, reveal a significant disparity between optimism regarding individual businesses and broader economic confidence within Jersey. While 56% of respondents expressed optimism about their own organizations, only 17% felt positive about the island’s overall economic outlook.nnThe IoD Jersey's second economic confidence survey highlights key priorities for growth, including increased infrastructure investment, attracting new industries, and tackling housing affordability. These concerns echo similar findings from Guernsey’s IoD survey published in late June, indicating a collective need for strategic action across both islands. Both islands face comparable hurdles, prompting calls for aligned policies from business leaders.nnDespite individual firm confidence, a “clear mandate for action” is evident, according to Alex Ruddy, IoD Jersey Chair. Members are calling for “bold, strategic investment and policy clarity to restore wider economic confidence,” Ruddy stated, emphasizing the need to move beyond individual successes and foster collective prosperity. Jersey respondents specifically expressed interest in collaborating with Guernsey on tackling fiscal deficits through reduced public spending and economic growth initiatives.nnFurther bolstering the call for unity, Richard Hemans, IoD Guernsey’s lead on economics, pointed to the islands’ strikingly similar economic profiles. “There is clear recognition… that the best way to confront our mutual challenges is to work together more closely,” he explained. The survey also touched on emerging trends such as AI adoption, finding it in its early stages, with 70% of companies offering training and a third developing formal strategies.nnBeyond collaboration, respondents identified support for innovation through tax incentives, attracting youth talent, and skills development as crucial for future growth. The findings align with broader discussions around leveraging digital advancements, as Damian Stirrett recently noted, to improve digital experiences for islanders. Furthermore, recent reports suggest improved workforce participation—addressing an aging population—could yield over £150m annually in combined tax revenue.
