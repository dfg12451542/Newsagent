A petition to restore the 128-year-old Victoria Marine Lake in St Helier, Jersey, has garnered over 1,000 signatures, signaling growing public concern over the future of the historic sea pool. The surge in support demonstrates a strong desire amongst residents to preserve a beloved landmark and ensure its continued use for generations to come.nnThe remarkable level of interest, according to campaigners, means the Jersey government will now be compelled to respond to the public outcry. The petition's success underscores the community's commitment to the pool and its potential as a valuable asset. Organisers are hopeful this will prompt meaningful action towards its restoration.nnBuilt in 1895, the Victoria Marine Lake has long been a focal point for leisure and recreation in St Helier, providing a safe and accessible swimming environment for locals and tourists alike. Over the years, it has served as a community hub, hosting swimming clubs, family days, and various water activities. However, the pool has fallen into disrepair in recent years, raising concerns about its structural integrity and safety.nnPetition organiser Nina Zaech described the pool as a “vital piece of Jersey’s heritage” that could support health, leisure, and tourism. Campaigners, including the newly-formed Save Our Pool Working Group, are calling for urgent restoration work to bring the pool back to its former glory. They argue that preserving the pool is an investment in the island’s cultural identity and tourist appeal.nnA 2024 inspection revealed the pool requires “comprehensive work,” but the future remains uncertain. Last month, Infrastructure Minister Andy Jehan suggested repairs would be “uneconomical” due to planned sea defence upgrades along the waterfront, sparking further fears that the pool may be permanently closed. The petition represents a direct challenge to this assessment, urging the government to reconsider its position and prioritize the preservation of this important piece of Jersey’s history.
