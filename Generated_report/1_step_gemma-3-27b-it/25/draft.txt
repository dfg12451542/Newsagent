The Royal Observatory Greenwich has unveiled the shortlist for the 2025 ZWO Astronomy Photographer of the Year competition, showcasing breathtaking images of celestial events and landscapes. From vibrant auroras over Norway to a striking eruption on the sun, the collection highlights the beauty and power of the universe as captured by talented astrophotographers around the globe. Among the shortlisted images is a stunning shot of Comet C/2023 A3, an event anticipated by astronomers throughout the year, as well as a captivating image of a solar eruption lasting roughly an hour. nnThe competition’s shortlist demonstrates a remarkable range of skill and dedication, with photographers going to great lengths to secure their shots. One photographer, Deqian Li, spent six days camping in Hongyuan County, China, to capture a uniquely shaped nebula resembling a pomegranate. Another image skillfully combines detail captured during the blue hour – the period just before sunrise or after sunset – with the golden light illuminating distant mountains. Several of the images are composites of numerous individual shots, with one requiring 300 separate exposures to create the final result.nnAccording to Sam Wen, founder and CEO of ZWO, “we believe that astrophotography is not only a way to record the cosmos, but also a way to inspire curiosity, foster education and build communities that transcend borders.” This sentiment underscores the broader goals of the competition, which seeks to make the wonders of the universe accessible to all. Wen further states, “Everyone deserves a chance to connect with the Universe — and through our support, we hope to bring that experience to more people.”nnAstrophotography has a rich history, evolving from early attempts to photograph the moon in the mid-19th century to the highly sophisticated techniques used today.  The capability to capture such detail owes a substantial debt to advancements in telescopes and camera technology.  Comet C/2023 A3, for example, is estimated to be six to twelve miles in length and travels at over sixty kilometers per second – details revealed through the power of modern astrophotography. nnThe winners across nine categories, along with special prizes and the overall winner, will be announced on September 11, 2025. An exhibition featuring the shortlisted images will be open to the public at the National Maritime Museum starting September 12, offering a closer look at these stunning depictions of the cosmos.
