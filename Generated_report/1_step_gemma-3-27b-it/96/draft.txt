Great Britain’s Oliver Rowland has clinched the Formula E title with two races remaining, marking a historic first for the British driver after a fourth-place finish in Berlin, Germany. The Nissan driver secured the championship in dominant fashion, becoming the 10th different champion in the series’ 11-season history. Rowland’s consistent performance throughout the season proved decisive, establishing a substantial lead over his rivals.nnPorsche’s Pascal Wehrlein entered the Berlin race as Rowland’s closest contender, but a disappointing result saw him drop to 16th despite starting from pole position at the historic Tempelhof airport circuit. This effectively ended Wehrlein’s championship hopes, leaving Rowland with an unassailable points lead.  As of Sunday’s race, Rowland leads the standings with 184 points, while Wehrlein trails significantly with 125.nnRowland acknowledged a cautious approach heading into the race. “Honestly, I was just thinking before the race not to have too much damage going into [the final round in] London,” he stated. He attributed his success to navigating a highly competitive field, commenting on the aggressive racing during Sunday's event.nnOverwhelmed with emotion following his victory, Rowland expressed his disbelief, saying, “I can’t believe it, I have no words. It’s incredible. I was trying to play it safe but everybody was so aggressive, so at some point I was all in.” His ability to stay out of trouble proved key to securing the championship. “Thankfully I stayed out of trouble,” he added.nnWhile Rowland celebrated his championship win, the Berlin E-Prix itself was won by Jaguar’s Nick Cassidy, followed by Jake Dennis of Andretti and Jean-Eric Vergne of DS Penske. Rowland’s strong season, punctuated by victories in Mexico, Jeddah, Monaco, and Tokyo, alongside his consistent points scoring, has solidified his position as Formula E champion.
