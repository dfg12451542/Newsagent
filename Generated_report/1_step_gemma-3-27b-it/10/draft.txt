St Mirren manager Stephen Robinson expressed his shock following his side's disappointing Premier Sports Cup opener against Arbroath, which ended in a 0-0 draw and subsequent penalty shootout defeat. Robinson apologized to the St Mirren supporters who made the journey to Gayfield Park, stating he felt the performance was markedly different from what he'd observed throughout pre-season. The result leaves St Mirren needing a positive response in their upcoming group stage fixture.nnThe match itself saw St Mirren, a Scottish Premiership team, struggle to break down League 1 champions Arbroath. Despite dominating possession, they failed to find the back of the net in normal time, and ultimately succumbed to defeat in the penalty shootout, forfeiting a crucial bonus point in the group stage. The unexpected result has left Robinson questioning his team's readiness despite a strong pre-season campaign.nnSt Mirren are back in action on Tuesday with a trip to League 2 side Forfar Athletic, and Robinson is clearly demanding a drastic improvement. He highlighted just how uncharacteristic his team’s display was, describing them as unrecognizable compared to their pre-season form.  “I’m in a little bit of shock,” Robinson told club media.nnRobinson was particularly critical of his players' decision-making, passing, and fundamental skills, stating he “didn’t recognise” several aspects of their play. He lamented that the team collectively abandoned the principles they had diligently practiced, leading to the unfavorable outcome, adding, “We decided as a collective not to do that and got exactly what we deserved.”  Despite the frustration, Robinson refused to dwell on the negative, insisting his squad has historically demonstrated resilience.nnLooking ahead, Robinson emphasized the need for a swift and decisive reaction from his players. He acknowledged the disappointment of the travelling fans, understanding their frustration at sacrificing a Saturday for a lackluster performance and pledged his team would improve. “We’re not going to feel sorry for ourselves. It’s disappointing…But it’s a group of boys that have never let me down over a longer period of time. As I say, we have to be and will be better than that,” he concluded. “We have to react to it, we will be better. We have to do simple things which we didn’t do.”
