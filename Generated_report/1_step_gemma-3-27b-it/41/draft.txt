Ilkley residents and business owners are voicing strong opposition to proposed parking changes, with some describing the plans as akin to those of Ebenezer Scrooge. Bradford Council is considering eliminating the town’s current one-hour free parking allowance and introducing charges on bank holidays, a move local figures warn will severely impact the community. A petition with over 1,500 signatures was presented to the council this week demonstrating the widespread concern.nnRichard Downing, who presented the petition, argued the changes would be “immensely damaging” to Ilkley’s local businesses. “It helps people shop locally and make visits to doctors and post offices,” Downing stated. “Having to pay to visit shops for a few items will make it inconvenient to shop locally – footfall will be down and people will be drawn to retail parks.” Concerns extend beyond retail, with residents fearing the impact on cultural venues as well, as Richard Downing further explained, “Many residents will stop visiting the town's small businesses. Evening charges will deter people from visiting our theatres.”nnIlkley Conservative councillor Andrew Loy echoed these sentiments, arguing the charges are unfair and will disproportionately affect vulnerable groups. “The charges will unfairly impact certain groups of people,” Loy told a Bradford Council meeting. He pointed to the elderly, less mobile individuals, parents, and churchgoers who rely on free on-street parking, dramatically questioning, “How is it fair that they might face a parking fine for parking outside their church on Christmas Day?” Loy didn’t hold back in his criticism, declaring, “It's like Ebenezer Scrooge is writing the council's parking policy.”nnCouncillor Alex Ross Shaw acknowledged the strength of public feeling, stating, “We recognise the number of responses to the petition. We do recognise the strength of feeling.” However, Councillor Shaw defended the proposals as a necessary measure for budget management, explaining, “We're looking at trying to find ways of managing the budget. Standardising parking charges is one way of doing this.” He also pushed back against the suggestion that Ilkley is being unfairly targeted, stating changes are being implemented “in similar ways right across the district.”nnThe petition has been forwarded to the council’s executive for a final decision. The outcome will undoubtedly be closely watched by residents and business owners who fear the changes will diminish Ilkley's appeal as a vibrant market town.
