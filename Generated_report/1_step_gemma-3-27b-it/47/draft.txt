The operator of a waste recycling site in South Elmsall, stripped of its licence due to health hazard concerns, has lodged an appeal against the decision. The facility, run by Minore, also known as Mineral Processing Ltd, has faced scrutiny over the storage of hazardous materials and complaints from local residents regarding noxious odors. The Environment Agency (EA) previously took action against the site, citing potential risks to public health.nnResidents have long expressed concerns about the site, which has operated since the 1970s, initially collecting household waste from East Lancashire but progressively expanding, now encroaching on residential areas. A planning inspector previously highlighted the significant health risks posed by the large quantities of hazardous materials stored on the land, echoing resident complaints about the pervasive smell. The EA's decision to revoke the licence stemmed from breaches of environmental regulations and a failure to adequately manage potential hazards.nnCarly Chambers, area manager for the EA in Yorkshire, confirmed the appeal lodged by Minore. Currently, an environmental permit remains in place while the Planning Inspectorate reviews the case. However, a suspension notice remains active, preventing further waste intake and mandating the removal of waste already in breach of permit conditions. “The environmental permit will now stay in place until the Planning Inspectorate has announced the outcome of this appeal,” Chambers stated.nnDespite the suspension, Minore maintains it is operating within the bounds of the notices issued. A company spokesperson stated that the site is currently excavating and processing materials in compliance with EA regulations. They further added, “Our employees have been working at the site for over 12 years and now fear for their jobs due to this escalation.” The company reports regular water sampling demonstrating no adverse impact on the nearby Frickley Beck. Chambers emphasized the EA's understanding of the impact the site has on the community and their continued regulatory response.  Failure to comply with the suspension notice would be considered an offense, Chambers warned.
