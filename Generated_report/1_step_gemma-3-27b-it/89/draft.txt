South Africa’s President Cyril Ramaphosa has suspended Police Minister Senzo Mchunu, effective immediately, following serious allegations linking him to organized crime groups. The decision comes amid mounting public pressure on Ramaphosa to address claims of interference in investigations and corruption within law enforcement agencies. Mchunu will be on leave of absence while a judicial commission investigates the accusations, which the President stated “undermine the constitution and threaten national security.”nnIn a televised address to the nation on Sunday, President Ramaphosa announced the establishment of a judicial commission, headed by the country’s deputy chief justice, to conduct a comprehensive inquiry into the claims against Mchunu. The commission will examine allegations of interference in investigations into political killings and broader corruption within the police force. Ramaphosa emphasized the urgency of the situation, stating the allegations “call for an urgent and comprehensive investigation.” Law professor Firoz Cachalia has been appointed as interim police minister during the investigation.nnSenzo Mchunu has denied any wrongdoing, stating he “stood ready to respond to the accusations” leveled against him. However, the allegations, first made public by KwaZulu-Natal provincial police boss Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi, paint a concerning picture. Gen. Mkhwanazi alleges Mchunu was receiving financial support from a controversial businessman, Vusimuzi Matlala, to fund his “political endeavours,” and even shared copies of text messages and a payment allegedly made by Matlala as evidence.nnAccording to Gen. Mkhwanazi, the minister was involved in the “orchestrated” disbandment of a task force established in 2018 to investigate political killings, primarily in KwaZulu-Natal.  Mkhwanazi detailed how 121 case files were allegedly removed from the unit on Mchunu’s instruction, without authorization from the national police commissioner, and have remained untouched since March. He claims several of these dockets already had instructions to proceed with arrests.  Mchunu had previously stated the unit was disbanded because it wasn’t adding value, despite numerous unresolved cases.nnRamaphosa’s swift action reflects the gravity of the allegations and the need to maintain public trust in law enforcement. The commission will also investigate current and former police officials, as well as members of the national executive, signaling a broad scope of inquiry. Mchunu, however, has pledged his commitment to the process, stating, “Honour and integrity are the virtues I personally subscribe to and which we all need to make efforts to uphold.”
