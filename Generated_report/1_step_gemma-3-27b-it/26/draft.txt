A lovingly restored steam locomotive has officially re-entered service at the North Norfolk Railway in Sheringham, marking a significant moment for the heritage line and railway enthusiasts alike. The engine, recently completing a nine-year refurbishment, began pulling passenger trains on Saturday, delighting onlookers and volunteers who dedicated years to bringing the historic machine back to life. The locomotive will now operate regularly between Sheringham and Holt, adding a touch of nostalgia to the popular tourist route.nnThe journey to return the locomotive to working order was a remarkable one, beginning with a surprising discovery. Alex Alder, instrumental in the restoration, described discovering the engine as akin to “hearing of a mythical Egyptian tomb.” He recounted a wild tip-off leading him down a Hertfordshire country lane and ultimately to a convent, where the 39-ton locomotive had been quietly residing.  “I fully never expected to be driven down a Hertfordshire country lane, following a wild tip off about a lost engine,” Alder remarked, detailing the unexpected path the restoration took.  His journey even included sharing tea and biscuits with a mother superior who spoke of “Malcolm’s engine.”nnAfter locating the engine, a team of volunteers embarked on a painstaking nine-year overhaul, costing approximately £200,000, according to Alder. The effort culminated in Saturday’s successful debut, with the locomotive finally pulling passenger trains for the first time in its history. “After nine years of efforts, for the very first time ever she pulled passenger trains. It was absolutely incredible,” exclaimed Alder, reflecting on the long-awaited moment. Graham Hukins echoed this sentiment, stating it was “absolutely wonderful” to see the engine running in Sheringham.nnThe inclusion of the restored locomotive into the North Norfolk Railway’s fleet is particularly meaningful as the railway celebrates 50 years of operation.  Hukins emphasized the significance of being a part of the engine’s story and witnessing its return to working order, noting, “This weekend is the very first time it has been paired with passenger carriages, and it is an absolute delight to have it join our working fleet.”  The locomotive’s arrival promises to enhance the experience for visitors and continue the railway’s legacy for years to come.
