Australia were skittled for 225 all out on the first day of their day/night Test against the West Indies in St Lucia, after a dramatic collapse saw them lose seven wickets for just 68 runs. Having begun the day in a promising position at 157-3, the tourists faltered, handing the initiative to the West Indies. Steve Smith top-scored with 48, while Cameron Green contributed 46, but both were dismissed either side of the dinner break, triggering the batting slide.nnThe West Indies responded with 16-1 by the close of play, trailing by 209 runs. Mitchell Starc marked his 100th Test appearance with the wicket of West Indies opener Kevlon Anderson for three, chopping an inside edge onto his stumps. Starc became just the second Australian pace bowler, after Glenn McGrath, to reach the milestone, although his individual celebration was shadowed by his team’s earlier struggles.nnShamar Joseph was the bowling star for the West Indies, claiming an impressive 4-33, with Jayden Seales and Justin Greaves both taking three wickets apiece. The relentless pressure from the West Indies attack proved too much for the Australian middle and lower order, effectively dismantling their innings. As Cameron Green explained, “Every pitch has been tough. The more balls you spend in the middle you hope it gets better but it just hasn't been the case.”nnThis latest batting performance follows a pattern of vulnerability in the Australian lineup, echoing challenges faced in the first Test where they were bowled out for just 180. Despite eventually winning that match by 159 runs, Australia again found their top order struggling. Although previously the Australian side were rescued by Travis Head, Webster and Carey, no such resistance materialised today. nnGreen also emphasized the learning process in difficult conditions, stating, “It's a bit of a grind first time with these balls, but a lot of learnings. Time in the middle is key - just trying to get used to their bowlers, their conditions.” The West Indies will look to build on their strong start when play resumes, hoping to capitalise on Australia’s fragile position and potentially take a commanding lead in the series.
